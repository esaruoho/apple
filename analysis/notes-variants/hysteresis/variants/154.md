What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), insulators, and gases.
Pure elements that do not form complex magnetic structures typically do not show significant hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a gas and does not retain memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, certain properties of air, such as its density or pressure, can change based on current conditions and do not necessarily depend on past conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific materials like magnetic substances, while most other elements and air do not exhibit this property.
