What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Polymers: Certain types of polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under stress.
Some Ceramics: Some ceramic materials can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not show hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not exhibit hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) generally do not show hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way as solid or liquid materials.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. Gases do not retain a "memory" of past conditions in the same way that solids or certain liquids do. When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it responds immediately and does not "remember" previous conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems with complex internal structures that can store and recall past states, such as magnetic materials and some polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
