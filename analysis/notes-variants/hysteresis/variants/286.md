What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), insulators, and gases.
Pure liquids generally do not show hysteresis either, although complex fluids or solutions might under certain conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials and systems, particularly magnetic materials, while air and many other elements do not exhibit this property.
