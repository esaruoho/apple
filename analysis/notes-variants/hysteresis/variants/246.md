What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they become magnetized, and when the field is removed, they retain some magnetization. This is known as remanence. The relationship between the magnetic field and the magnetization forms a hysteresis loop.

Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties, particularly in stress-strain relationships. This can be due to the internal rearrangement of molecules under stress.

Some Ceramics: Certain ceramics can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties, especially in piezoelectric materials.

Metals: Some metals, like those used in strain gauges, can show hysteresis in their resistance changes under stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Most simple materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, gases, pure liquids, and many solid materials (like most metals) do not show this behavior unless they are part of a complex system or undergo specific phase transitions.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. However, air can participate in hysteresis loops in certain systems, such as in the context of humidity and temperature cycles, but this is not inherent to the air itself but rather to the system it is part of.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields, mechanical stress, and certain types of polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
