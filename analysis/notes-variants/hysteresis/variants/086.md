Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The relationship between the magnetic field strength (H) and the resulting magnetization (M) is described by the hysteresis loop.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until a certain threshold is reached. The process of returning to the original shape involves hysteresis.
Some Polymers: Certain types of polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis in their magnetic or mechanical properties.
Linear systems, which follow a direct proportionality between input and output, do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include simple resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a homogeneous medium without memory. However, certain properties of air can exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions:

Thermal Properties: In very controlled environments, the thermal conductivity of air might show slight hysteresis if there are rapid and repeated temperature changes, but this is not a common or significant phenomenon.
Humidity: The absorption and desorption of water vapor by certain materials in contact with air can sometimes show hysteresis, but this is more related to the material properties rather than the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
