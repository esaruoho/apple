What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence and is a classic example of hysteresis.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials exhibit stress-strain behavior that depends on their previous deformation history.
Solenoids and Relays: The switching behavior of these devices can depend on the previous state of the circuit.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis in their temperature-to-voltage response.
Soil and Rocks: In geology, the permeability of soil or rock can change depending on the history of fluid flow through it.

Elements and Simple Substances:
Simple elements like metals, gases (including air), and pure liquids do not typically exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. They do not remember past states in the way that more complex materials do.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials or other complex systems do. For example, if you heat air and then cool it back down, the air will return to its original state without retaining any "memory" of the heating process.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or interactions. Simple elements and substances like air do not exhibit hysteresis.
