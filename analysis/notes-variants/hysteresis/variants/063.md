What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Polymers: Certain types of polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until the temperature is changed again, exhibiting hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) generally do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. When you compress air, it expands when the pressure is released, and there is no "memory" of previous compression cycles. Similarly, when you heat air, it cools down when the heat source is removed, and there is no retention of the heating cycle.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in complex materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic or shape-memory properties. Simple elements like air do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
