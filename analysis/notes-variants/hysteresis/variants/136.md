Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases, when not subjected to magnetic fields, do not exhibit hysteresis.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. Gases generally do not show memory effects related to past states unless they are subjected to very specific conditions (such as extreme pressure changes that might induce unusual behavior).

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials that can store and release energy in response to external stimuli, particularly in magnetic and some mechanical systems. Air, as a simple gas, does not have hysteresis under typical conditions.
