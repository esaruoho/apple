What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of being exposed to magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until the temperature changes again, exhibiting hysteresis.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic elements do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Pure gases, such as nitrogen or oxygen, do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) respond directly to the current conditions and do not depend on past conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific types of materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or interactions, such as magnetic materials and some alloys. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
