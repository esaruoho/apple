What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Hysteresis loops are often seen in magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of memory devices.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: The magnetization of ferromagnetic materials depends on the history of applied magnetic fields. This is why they retain their magnetism (or remanence) even after the external field is removed.
Rubber: When stretched or compressed, rubber exhibits hysteresis in its stress-strain curve. The energy stored during stretching is released more slowly during unloading, leading to a loop in the stress-strain graph.
Saturable Inductors: These are used in power electronics and exhibit hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Some Types of Memory Devices: Certain types of non-volatile memory, like magnetic RAM, can exhibit hysteresis in their switching behavior.

Elements and Substances Without Hysteresis:
Many materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example:
Most Metals: Unlike ferromagnetic materials, most metals do not show significant hysteresis.
Non-Ferromagnetic Materials: Materials like copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
Air: Air does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas with no internal structure that would remember past states. Its properties (like pressure and temperature) change directly in response to the current conditions without retaining any history.

Conclusion:
Air: Does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas whose properties change directly based on the current conditions.
Materials with Hysteresis: Include magnetic materials, rubber, and some specialized memory devices.
Materials Without Hysteresis: Include most metals, non-ferromagnetic materials, and gases like air.
