What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of springs.

Elements with Hysteresis

Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis when they are exposed to varying magnetic fields. The magnetization of these materials depends on both the current magnetic field and the history of previous magnetic fields.
Rubber and Elastomers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the amount of deformation depends on the history of loading and unloading cycles.
Some Types of Springs: Certain spring materials can exhibit hysteresis, particularly if they undergo significant deformation and repeated cycles of loading and unloading.

Elements without Hysteresis

Many materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example:

Linear Elastic Materials: Materials like steel or aluminum, which follow Hooke's Law (stress is directly proportional to strain), do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
Ideal Gases: Gases generally do not show hysteresis because their properties (like pressure) are directly proportional to temperature and volume, and do not depend on the history of changes.

Air

Air, being an ideal gas under most conditions, does not exhibit hysteresis. Its properties such as pressure and density are directly related to temperature and volume, and do not depend on the history of changes. However, in certain extreme conditions (e.g., phase transitions or very complex thermodynamic processes), the behavior of air could become more complex, but this is not typically considered hysteresis in the usual sense.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those that can undergo significant deformation or changes in state, while many other materials and systems do not exhibit this property.
