What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of applied magnetic fields.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases, such as air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any "memory" of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. When you apply a magnetic field to air, it will align with the field, but once the field is removed, the air will return to its original state without retaining any information about the previous field strength or direction.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in magnetic materials and some other specific materials, while most elements and substances, including air, do not exhibit this property.
