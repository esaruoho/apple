What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often creating a lagging curve when plotted over time.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the previous loading history.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can also exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most simple linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors and capacitors in electrical circuits typically do not show hysteresis.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's law strictly and does not show any memory effect, hence no hysteresis.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in an electric or magnetic field, respectively, but do not retain any "memory" of past states, so they do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of its past states. When you compress air, it will expand to its original volume when the pressure is removed, and there is no lag or retention of previous compression states. Therefore, air does not exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects like magnetic materials and some polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
