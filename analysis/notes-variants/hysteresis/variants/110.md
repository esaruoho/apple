Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of applied magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until a threshold is reached, exhibiting hysteresis in their phase transitions.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical properties under mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis

Most simple elements or materials do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. For example:
Metals: Pure metals generally do not show significant hysteresis unless they are part of a complex system or undergo phase transitions.
Non-magnetic Metals: Metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show magnetic hysteresis because they are not ferromagnetic.
Semiconductors: Semiconductors typically do not show hysteresis in their electronic properties under normal operating conditions.

Air

Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. It is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, air can exhibit some hysteretic behavior in specific contexts, such as:

Thermal Hysteresis: In very controlled environments, air might show slight thermal hysteresis due to the time it takes for heat to fully distribute within a system, but this is not a fundamental property of air.
Pressure Hysteresis: In elastic systems involving air (like balloons), there can be a form of hysteresis related to the elastic deformation, but this is not inherent to the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, and air does not inherently exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
