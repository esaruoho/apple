What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed, leading to a hysteresis loop.
Rubber and Elastomers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the energy required to deform them depends on the previous deformation history.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can also exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states once the external conditions change.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. The properties of air (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) respond directly to the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions. For instance, if you compress air and then release it, it will return to its original state without retaining any "memory" of the compression.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields or elastic deformation. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
