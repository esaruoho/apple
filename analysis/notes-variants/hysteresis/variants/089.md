Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of the magnetic field applied to them.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the previous deformations.
Some Ceramics and Glasses: Certain ceramics and glasses can also exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Linear Systems: In linear systems, the response is directly proportional to the input, and there is no memory of past inputs. Examples include simple electrical circuits with resistors, capacitors, and inductors (without hysteresis).
Ideal Springs and Masses: Idealized models of springs and masses do not exhibit hysteresis; they return to their original state after the force is removed.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in an electric or magnetic field, respectively, and do not retain any "memory" of past voltages or currents.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a fluid and does not retain a "memory" of past states in the same way as solid materials or certain types of mechanical systems. However, properties related to air can exhibit hysteresis under certain conditions:

Humidity: The relative humidity of air can exhibit some hysteresis, especially in porous materials or in the context of desiccation and hydration cycles.
Thermal Properties: The thermal capacity of air can show some hysteresis in very specific conditions, such as during rapid heating and cooling cycles, but this is not a common or significant effect.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and retain energy in a way that depends on their history. Air, being a fluid, does not typically exhibit hysteresis in the same sense as solid materials or certain mechanical systems.
