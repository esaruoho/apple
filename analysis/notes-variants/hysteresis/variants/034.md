Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. Examples include iron, nickel, cobalt, and some alloys like permalloy.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature and retain those changes, demonstrating hysteresis.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.
Thermocouples: Certain types of thermocouples can exhibit hysteresis in their temperature-to-voltage response.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Semiconductors: In general, semiconductors do not show hysteresis unless they are part of a specific device or system where it is induced.
Insulators: Pure insulators typically do not exhibit hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce it.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, certain properties of air can be described using hysteretic behavior under specific conditions:

Thermal Hysteresis: The thermal conductivity of air can show some hysteresis-like behavior depending on the rate of heating or cooling. For example, the thermal conductivity might change slightly as the air transitions from one phase of heating to another.
Humidity Hysteresis: In humid environments, the relationship between temperature and relative humidity can show hysteresis due to the time required for moisture to evaporate or condense.

In summary, while many materials and systems can exhibit hysteresis, air itself does not inherently possess this property.
