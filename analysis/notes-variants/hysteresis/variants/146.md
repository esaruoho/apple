Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well. Common examples of hysteresis include:

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt exhibit hysteresis when subjected to magnetic fields. The magnetization (or magnetic flux density) depends on the history of the applied magnetic field.
Elastic Materials: Some elastic materials can show hysteresis under stress, particularly if they undergo cyclic loading and unloading.
Thermal Systems: Certain thermal systems can exhibit hysteresis, especially in phase change processes like melting and freezing.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between inputs and outputs do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, Ohm's law for resistors (V = IR) describes a linear relationship and does not depend on the history of voltage or current.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal electrical components, the charge and current relationships are linear and do not depend on previous states.
Simple Springs: Ideal springs follow Hooke's law (F = -kx), which is a linear relationship and does not exhibit hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and follows ideal gas laws under normal conditions. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and volume) are generally linear and do not depend on the history of these properties. However, in specific applications involving complex interactions (like in certain atmospheric phenomena or in controlled environments), there might be indirect effects that could mimic hysteresis, but this would not be inherent to the material itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that involve nonlinear behavior or memory effects. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
