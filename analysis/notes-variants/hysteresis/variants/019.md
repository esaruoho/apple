What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains. Even after the external magnetic field is removed, the material retains some magnetization, which is described by the hysteresis loop.

Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to an electric field.

Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until a threshold temperature is reached. The process is reversible but can exhibit hysteresis due to the stress-strain relationship during phase transitions.

Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not have magnetic domains that can align and retain orientation.

Semiconductors: While semiconductors can exhibit some form of hysteresis in certain applications (like in memory devices), pure semiconducting materials generally do not show pronounced hysteresis under normal operating conditions.

Insulators: Pure insulating materials typically do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic or other forms of hysteresis.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-piezoelectric, and non-ferroelectric medium under normal conditions. Air does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, air can exhibit some forms of hysteresis-like behavior in certain specialized contexts, such as in the case of hysteresis loops in thermodynamic cycles involving air, but this is not inherent to air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic, ferroelectric, or shape-memory properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
