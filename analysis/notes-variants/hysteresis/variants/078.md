What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature and can remember their previous shapes, showing hysteresis in their mechanical properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases, when not subjected to strong external fields or changes in pressure that would induce hysteresis, do not show this effect.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, if you consider the behavior of air under different conditions (e.g., changes in temperature and pressure), it can exhibit some forms of hysteresis-like behavior in specific contexts, such as during phase transitions (like condensation and evaporation) or in complex systems involving feedback loops.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain types of materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects like magnetic materials and shape memory alloys. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
