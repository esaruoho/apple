What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states as well. Common examples of hysteresis include:

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. When these materials are exposed to a changing magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed, leading to a hysteresis loop.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the previous deformations.
Some Electronic Components: Certain types of capacitors and inductors can exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors, capacitors, and inductors in purely linear circuits do not show hysteresis.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's law perfectly, meaning it does not retain any deformation after the force is removed, thus showing no hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. The behavior of air is generally linear and reversible; for instance, when you compress air, it expands back to its original volume when the pressure is removed. There is no "memory" effect in the way that materials like magnets or certain polymers do.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and recall information about past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
