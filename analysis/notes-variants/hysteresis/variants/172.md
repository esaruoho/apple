What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and magnetic flux density (B) are related through the hysteresis loop.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol can undergo shape changes based on temperature, and their behavior can show hysteresis.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can exhibit hysteresis in their response to mechanical stress and electrical fields.
Thermal Systems: Certain thermal systems can show hysteresis in their response to temperature changes, especially if there are phase transitions involved.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
Pure gases, when not interacting with other materials or undergoing phase changes, typically do not show hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, air can participate in hysteresis loops in certain systems, such as in humidity control systems where the relationship between temperature and relative humidity can show hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific types of materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic properties, shape memory, and certain thermal responses. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
