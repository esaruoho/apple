What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) versus magnetic flux density (B) curve forms a loop, known as the hysteresis loop.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until a threshold is reached, exhibiting hysteresis in their phase transitions.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases, when not subjected to phase transitions or other complex behaviors, typically do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. Gases generally do not show memory effects related to past states unless they undergo phase transitions (like condensation or vaporization), which are rare in everyday situations involving air.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems that can store and recall information about their past states, such as magnetic materials and some alloys. Air, as a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under typical conditions.
