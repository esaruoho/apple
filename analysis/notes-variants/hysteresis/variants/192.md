What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly when they undergo repeated magnetization cycles. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, such as mechanical deformation or electrical polarization.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties after being demagnetized.
Semiconductors: While some semiconductors can show hysteretic behavior in certain applications (like in ferroelectric materials), most do not exhibit hysteresis under normal operating conditions.
Insulators: Pure insulators generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce it, such as in certain dielectric materials.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-polarizable medium under normal conditions. However, if you consider the behavior of air in terms of pressure and temperature changes over time, it can exhibit some memory-like behavior due to thermal inertia or other environmental factors, but this is not the same as hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials and systems that can store and release energy in a way that depends on their past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently possess hysteresis.
