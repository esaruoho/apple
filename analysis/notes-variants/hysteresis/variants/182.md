What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past exposure to magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their phase transformations.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions because they do not have internal states that depend on past conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in terms of its physical or chemical properties. Changes in air pressure, temperature, and volume are typically reversible and do not depend on the path taken to reach those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic or ferroelectric properties, shape memory alloys, and some piezoelectric materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
