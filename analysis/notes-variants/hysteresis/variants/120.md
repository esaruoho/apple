What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The relationship between the magnetic field strength (H) and the magnetization (M) is described by the hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, such as changes in temperature or mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), insulators, and gases.
Pure elements that do not form magnetic domains typically do not show hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a gas and does not retain memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. However, certain properties of air, such as its density or pressure, can change in response to external factors and can be described using hysteresis-like behavior in specific contexts (e.g., in throttling processes). But this is not due to inherent hysteresis but rather to the thermodynamic properties of the system.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with magnetic materials and some specific types of ceramics and polymers, while most other materials and gases do not exhibit this property.
