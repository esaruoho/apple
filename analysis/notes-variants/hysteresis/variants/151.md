What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys, like nitinol (nickel-titanium), can change shape in response to temperature changes, exhibiting hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to special treatments or conditions.
Pure gases generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states under normal conditions. However, certain properties of air, such as its density or pressure, can be influenced by historical conditions, but this is not hysteresis in the strict sense. For instance, if air is compressed and then allowed to expand, it will return to its original state without retaining any "memory" of the compression.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in magnetic materials and some specialized materials under specific conditions, while most other elements and substances do not exhibit this property.
