What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can show hysteresis, especially those used in electronic components like capacitors and inductors.
Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials can exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties, such as stress-strain relationships.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Semiconductors: While some semiconductors can exhibit hysteresis under certain conditions, many do not.
Insulators: Most insulating materials do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce it.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. For example, if you compress air and then release it, the air will return to its original state without retaining any "memory" of the compression.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or interactions. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
