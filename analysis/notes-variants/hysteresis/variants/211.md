What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have a memory effect related to their state changes.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it responds immediately and does not "remember" previous conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with internal memory effects, such as magnetic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
