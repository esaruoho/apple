Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions and affect its response to future conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains. As the magnetic field changes, the material's magnetization follows a loop known as the hysteresis loop.
Some Polymers: Certain types of polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.
Some Ceramics: Some ceramic materials can also exhibit hysteresis in their electrical or mechanical properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not have magnetic domains that can align in response to an external magnetic field.
Insulators: Pure insulating materials generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic behavior (e.g., through impurities or phase transitions).

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-crystalline substance that does not retain any memory of past states when it comes to physical or chemical properties. However, air can participate in processes that involve hysteresis, such as the hysteresis loops seen in the saturation vapor pressure of water at different temperatures and pressures.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials that can store and recall information about past states, such as magnetic materials and some polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
