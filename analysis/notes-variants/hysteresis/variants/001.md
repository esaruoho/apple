Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to a magnetic field, it will retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence and is a classic example of hysteresis.
Rubber Bands: When you stretch a rubber band, it will stay stretched until you release it. If you then try to compress it, it won't return to its original shape immediately; instead, it will take some force to compress it further.
Solenoids: In electrical circuits, solenoids can exhibit hysteresis when used as switches or relays.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis, especially when used in rapidly changing temperature environments.

Elements and Materials with Hysteresis:
Metals: Certain metals like iron, nickel, and cobalt show significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Polymers: Some polymers can exhibit hysteresis under mechanical stress.
Ceramics: Certain ceramics can show hysteresis in their electrical properties.

Elements and Materials without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Elements like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Non-piezoelectric Materials: Materials like glass and rubber do not show hysteresis in their piezoelectric properties.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between input and output do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that materials like magnets do. The behavior of air is generally linear and does not depend on its previous states. For example, if you heat air, it expands; cooling it back down does not require any additional energy beyond what would be needed to cool it directly from the heated state.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or memory effects. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
