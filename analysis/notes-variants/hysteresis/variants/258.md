What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature and retain those changes until the material is heated above a certain threshold.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties after the removal of an external magnetic field.
Semiconductors: While some semiconductors can show hysteresis in certain conditions (like in magnetic sensors), many do not exhibit this behavior under normal operating conditions.
Insulators: Pure insulators generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce it.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a fluid and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. For example, if you compress air and then release it, the air will return to its original state without retaining any residual effects from the compression cycle.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic properties or shape-memory capabilities. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
