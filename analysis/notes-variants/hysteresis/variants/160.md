What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states. Materials with magnetic properties, such as ferromagnetic materials, often exhibit hysteresis. Here are some examples:

Elements with Hysteresis:
Ferromagnetic Materials: These include iron, nickel, cobalt, and some alloys like steel. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed.
Shape Memory Alloys: Such as nitinol (NiTi), which can "remember" their shape and return to it after being deformed, depending on the temperature.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties.
Paramagnetic and Diamagnetic Materials: These materials do not have permanent magnetic properties and do not exhibit hysteresis.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is not a material that retains memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. However, certain properties of air, such as its pressure or temperature, can be described using hysteresis-like behavior under specific conditions, but this is not the same as the intrinsic hysteresis found in magnetic materials.

For example, in a refrigeration cycle, the pressure and temperature of the refrigerant might exhibit a hysteresis loop when the system cycles between heating and cooling phases. But this is due to the operational characteristics of the system rather than an inherent property of the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials that can retain a memory of past states, particularly magnetic materials. Air, as a substance, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
