What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until the temperature is changed again.
Piezoelectric Materials: These materials can convert mechanical stress into electrical energy and vice versa, and their behavior can be hysteresis-dependent.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties when the external magnetic field is removed.
Semiconductors: While some semiconductors can show hysteresis in certain conditions, many do not exhibit it under normal operating conditions.
Insulators: Most insulating materials do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce it.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of previous states or conditions. However, certain properties of air, such as its density or pressure, can change in response to environmental conditions, but these changes do not typically exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and retain information about past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
