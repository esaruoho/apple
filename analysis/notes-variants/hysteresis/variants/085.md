What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. Gases do not retain a "memory" of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, magnetic materials and certain other materials can exhibit hysteresis, while most non-magnetic materials and gases like air do not.
