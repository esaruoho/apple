Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can retain this shape memory, exhibiting hysteresis in their phase transitions.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties.
Non-Ferroelectric Materials: Most dielectric materials do not show hysteresis in their polarization.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between input and output (like resistors or capacitors) do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-hysteretic medium. However, certain properties related to air can exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions:

Humidity: The relative humidity of air can exhibit some hysteresis, especially in humidifiers and dehumidifiers, where the system's state depends on the history of moisture levels.
Thermal Properties: In very controlled environments, the thermal properties of air might show some hysteresis, but this is not a common or significant phenomenon.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic, ferroelectric, or shape-memory behaviors. Air, as a material, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
