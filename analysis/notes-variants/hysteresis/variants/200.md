Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it will retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. The magnetization (output) depends on the history of the magnetic field applied (input). This is known as the hysteresis loop.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the magnetic flux density depends on the number of times the coil has been energized or de-energized.
Rubber Bands: When a rubber band is stretched, it takes some time to return to its original shape, depending on how quickly it was stretched.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis due to the thermal history of the material.
Materials with Memory Effects: Certain polymers and other materials can show memory effects, where their properties depend on past mechanical deformations.

Elements That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a direct and proportional manner to their inputs. For example, resistors, capacitors, and inductors in linear circuits do not exhibit hysteresis.
Simple Mechanical Systems: Most simple mechanical systems, like springs without damping, do not exhibit hysteresis unless they are part of a more complex system that introduces non-linear behavior.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and follows ideal gas laws under normal conditions. However, certain properties of air, such as humidity, can exhibit hysteresis. For example, the moisture content in air can depend on the history of temperature and humidity changes, leading to a hysteresis loop in the psychrometric chart used in HVAC engineering.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects or non-linear behaviors. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis, but its properties can sometimes show hysteresis under specific conditions.
