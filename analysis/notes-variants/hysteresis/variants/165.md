Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape, demonstrating hysteresis.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of previous states.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions. However, certain properties of air, such as its pressure or temperature, can be described using hysteresis-like behavior in specific contexts, but this is not inherent to the air itself. For example, in a refrigeration cycle, the pressure-temperature relationship of the refrigerant might show hysteresis due to the phase transitions involved, but this is a property of the refrigerant and the system, not of the air.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields or phase transitions. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
