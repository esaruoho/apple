Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Common examples include magnetic materials, some types of springs, and certain types of memory devices.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis because their magnetic domains align in response to an external magnetic field, and this alignment can persist even after the field is removed.
Some Types of Springs: Certain mechanical springs can show hysteresis under stress, particularly if they are subjected to repeated cycles of loading and unloading.
Certain Ceramics and Polymers: Some materials used in electronics and other applications can also exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most simple linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors and capacitors in electronic circuits typically follow Ohm's law and do not remember past conditions.
Ideal Springs: In theory, an ideal spring follows Hooke's law and does not exhibit hysteresis.
Pure Fluids: Pure liquids and gases generally do not show hysteresis unless they are undergoing phase transitions or other complex processes.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. It is a simple gas that follows the ideal gas law under most circumstances. However, air can exhibit some behavior that might seem like hysteresis in specific contexts, such as:

Thermal Expansion: While air expands when heated, it does not retain a "memory" of previous temperature changes in the same way that a material with hysteresis would.
Pressure Changes: When air pressure changes, it tends to return to its original state once the external force (e.g., a pump) is removed, similar to how a linear spring behaves.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, and air does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
