What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of mechanical systems.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis because their magnetic domains align in response to an external magnetic field, and this alignment can persist even after the field is removed.
Rubber and Elastomers: These materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves due to the internal rearrangement of polymer chains under deformation.
Some Ceramics and Glasses: Certain ceramics and glasses can also exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most simple linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits behave linearly, meaning their output is directly proportional to their input.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's Law, which states that the force exerted by the spring is directly proportional to its displacement from the equilibrium position. There is no memory effect, so it does not exhibit hysteresis.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in electric and magnetic fields, respectively, without any memory effect of past conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that responds linearly to changes in pressure and temperature. The state of air (its pressure, density, etc.) at any given moment depends solely on the current conditions and not on past conditions. Therefore, air does not have hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving complex interactions like magnetic domains or polymer chains. Simple linear systems and gases like air do not exhibit hysteresis.
