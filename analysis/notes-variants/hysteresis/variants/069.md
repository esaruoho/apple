What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed, leading to a hysteresis loop when plotted as magnetization versus magnetic field strength.

Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, indicating that the material's deformation depends on its previous deformations.

Some Ceramics and Glasses: Certain ceramics and glasses can also exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to special treatments or conditions that induce magnetic behavior.
Insulators: Most insulating materials do not show hysteresis because they do not have the necessary internal structure to retain memory of past states.
Simple Dielectrics: Pure dielectric materials (insulators) generally do not exhibit hysteresis in their polarization characteristics.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, the behavior of air can be complex depending on factors such as temperature and pressure changes, but this complexity does not constitute hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with internal structures that allow them to "remember" past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
