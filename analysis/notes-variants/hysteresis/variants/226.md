What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and magnetic flux density (B) relationship in these materials forms a hysteresis loop.
Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves under cyclic loading.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can also exhibit hysteresis, particularly those used in piezoelectric applications.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-Magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties.
Insulators: Most insulating materials do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic behavior.
Semiconductors: Semiconductors generally do not exhibit hysteresis unless they are doped or subjected to specific conditions that create magnetic domains.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-piezoelectric medium and does not retain any memory of previous states in terms of stress or strain. However, air can be affected by external forces and can change its properties over time due to factors like temperature and pressure changes, but this is not hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials with specific magnetic or mechanical properties, while air and many other simple substances do not exhibit this behavior.
