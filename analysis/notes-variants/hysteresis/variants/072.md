What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of being exposed to magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their transformation behavior.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past conditions.

Air:
Air, as a gas, does not have hysteresis. It does not remember past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. For example, if you heat air and then cool it back down, the air will return to its original state without retaining any memory of the heating process.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects such as magnetic materials, shape memory alloys, and some ferroelectric materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
