What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and magnetic flux density (B) are related through a hysteresis loop.
Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, indicating energy loss during deformation and recovery.
Some Ceramics: Certain ceramic materials can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties under cyclic stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Materials: Most linear materials do not exhibit hysteresis; they respond directly to the applied force or field without retaining any "memory" of past conditions.
Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and most other non-magnetic metals do not show significant hysteresis.
Gases: Gases generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any "memory" of previous states when undergoing changes in pressure and volume.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis. It follows the ideal gas law and responds linearly to changes in temperature and pressure. There is no retained "memory" of past conditions in the behavior of air.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that are nonlinear or have magnetic properties. Air, as a gas, does not have hysteresis.
