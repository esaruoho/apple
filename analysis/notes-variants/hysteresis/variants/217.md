What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of magnetic field exposure.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature and can remember their original shape after being deformed, exhibiting hysteresis.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can show hysteresis in their electrical properties under varying conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of previous states.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. Gases do not retain a "memory" of previous states in the same way that magnetic materials or shape memory alloys do. The properties of air (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on past conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic or shape-memory properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
