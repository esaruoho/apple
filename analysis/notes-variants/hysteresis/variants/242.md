What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of soils.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetized material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it retains some magnetization even when the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber: The deformation of rubber under stress depends on the previous stress applied. It can "remember" the previous deformations.
Soils: Some types of soil exhibit hysteresis in their strength and deformation properties.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Elements and simple systems typically do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have complex internal structures or memory mechanisms. For example, a single element like copper or aluminum does not show hysteresis in its electrical resistance or mechanical properties.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas with no internal structure that would allow it to "remember" past conditions. Properties of air such as pressure, temperature, and density change based on current conditions and do not depend on past conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a property seen in certain materials and systems due to their internal structure or memory mechanisms, while simple elements and gases like air do not exhibit this behavior.
