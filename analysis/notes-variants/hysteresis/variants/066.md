What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The relationship between the applied magnetic field and the resulting magnetization is described by the hysteresis loop.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization properties when subjected to an electric field.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their phase transformations.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Elements like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties after the removal of an external magnetic field.
Insulators: Pure insulating materials generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic behavior (e.g., through impurities or doping).

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-ferroelectric, and non-piezoelectric medium under normal conditions. However, if you consider the behavior of air in terms of pressure and temperature (which can be described by thermodynamic processes), it does not exhibit hysteresis in the same sense as magnetic or ferroelectric materials.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic, ferroelectric, or shape memory properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
