What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states. Here are some examples and non-examples of hysteresis:

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence. The relationship between the magnetic field strength and the magnetization is hysteresis.
Rubber Bands: When you stretch a rubber band, it takes some force to start stretching it, and it requires more force to continue stretching. Releasing the tension doesn't immediately return it to its original shape; it needs to be released slowly for it to fully relax.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the current required to maintain a certain magnetic flux density is dependent on the previous history of the current through the coil.
Elastic Deformation: Some materials exhibit elastic hysteresis, where the stress-strain curve forms a loop due to the material's internal energy changes during loading and unloading.

Non-Examples of Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships (where the output is directly proportional to the input) do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's law, where the force is directly proportional to the displacement. There is no memory of past displacements, so it does not exhibit hysteresis.
Purely Resistive Components: Resistors, capacitors, and inductors in their ideal forms do not exhibit hysteresis because they respond linearly to the applied voltage or current.

Air:
Air does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way as materials or systems do. Air pressure, temperature, and volume changes are typically described by thermodynamic equations that do not depend on the history of these variables. However, if we consider the behavior of air in a compressible fluid system, such as in a compressor or turbine, there might be some hysteresis-like behavior due to the complex interactions within the system, but this is not inherent to the properties of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems where the response depends on the history of inputs, while linear systems and ideal components do not exhibit hysteresis. Air, being a gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis, though complex systems involving air might show some hysteresis-like behavior under specific conditions.
