What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of being exposed to magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature and can remember their previous shape even after returning to their original shape.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases and liquids typically do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change in response to current conditions and do not depend on past conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific types of materials, particularly magnetic and some ferroelectric materials, while most other elements and substances do not exhibit this property.
