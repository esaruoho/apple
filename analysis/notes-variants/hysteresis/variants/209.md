What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber Bands: When you stretch a rubber band, it will return to its original shape when released, but if you stretch it beyond its elastic limit, it may not fully return to its original shape.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the current required to maintain a certain magnetic field strength can be different from the current required to reach that same field strength initially.
Thermocouples: The voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history of the material.
Elastic Deformation: Some materials exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the strain does not return to zero when the stress is removed.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Simple elements like gases (e.g., air), pure metals, or single-phase liquids do not typically exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. They respond linearly to changes in external parameters such as pressure, temperature, or magnetic fields.

Air:
Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. It follows ideal gas laws and responds linearly to changes in temperature and pressure. However, in certain complex systems involving air (such as in weather patterns or in the behavior of air in confined spaces with changing boundary conditions), hysteresis might appear due to non-linear interactions or memory effects, but this is not inherent to the properties of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of more complex systems and materials, while simple elements like air do not exhibit this property under normal circumstances.
