What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of magnetic field exposure.
Some Polymers: Certain types of polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.
Metals and Alloys: Some metals and alloys, especially those used in engineering applications, can exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curves.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, most metals like copper or aluminum do not show significant hysteresis in their electrical resistance or thermal properties.
Pure elements that do not form complex magnetic structures typically do not show hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on past conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or histories, such as magnetic materials and some polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
