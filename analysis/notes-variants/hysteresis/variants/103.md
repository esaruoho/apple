Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains, and when the field is removed, the domains remain aligned for some time, leading to a hysteresis loop.

Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, such as mechanical deformation or electrical polarization.

Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature, and this process can be reversible, showing hysteresis.

Piezoelectric Materials: These materials can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not have permanent magnetic domains.
Semiconductors: While semiconductors can show some form of hysteresis in their electrical properties under certain conditions, it is generally less pronounced than in magnetic materials.
Insulators: Most insulating materials do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce temporary changes in their structure.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. For example, if you heat air and then cool it back down, the air will return to its original state without any lingering effects from the heating cycle.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields, mechanical deformation, or electrical polarization. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
