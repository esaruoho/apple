Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Some Polymers and Rubber: Certain types of polymers and rubbers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties, such as stress-strain curves.
Ceramic Materials: Some ceramic materials can also exhibit hysteresis in their electrical or magnetic properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, aluminum, copper, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure metals like gold, silver, and platinum generally do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, air can exhibit some behavior that might seem similar to hysteresis in certain contexts, such as changes in pressure and volume during compression and expansion cycles, but this is not true hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic or elastic properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
