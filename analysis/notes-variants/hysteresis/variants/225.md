Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. When these materials are exposed to a changing magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is why they show a hysteresis loop when plotted against the applied magnetic field strength.

Some Metals and Alloys: Certain metals and alloys, such as nickel, cobalt, and some types of steel, can also show hysteresis in their magnetic properties.

Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials can exhibit mechanical hysteresis, meaning that the energy lost during deformation is stored and released during subsequent cycles of loading and unloading.

Circuits and Devices: Certain electronic components, like hysteresis comparators or certain types of memory devices, can exhibit hysteresis in their behavior.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties because they do not retain magnetic domains.

Semiconductors: While semiconductors can exhibit some form of hysteresis in certain conditions (like in certain types of transistors), they generally do not show pronounced hysteresis under normal operating conditions.

Air: Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that does not retain any memory of previous states in terms of its physical or chemical properties. The behavior of air is typically described by thermodynamic equations and does not depend on its past states in the same way that materials with hysteresis do.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or memory-like properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not have hysteresis.
