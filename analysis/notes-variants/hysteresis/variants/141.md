What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past exposure to magnetic fields.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), insulators, and gases.
Pure liquids generally do not show hysteresis either, as they do not retain memory of previous states in the same way as solids or certain materials.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis. Gases do not retain a "memory" of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in magnetic materials and certain other specialized materials, while most elements and air do not exhibit this property.
