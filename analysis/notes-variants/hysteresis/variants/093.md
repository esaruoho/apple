What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The relationship between the applied magnetic field and the resulting magnetization is described by the hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, though it is less common than in metals.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, air, water, and many metals (like copper or aluminum) do not show significant hysteresis under normal conditions.
Pure gases like air do not have hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states when subjected to changes in pressure or temperature.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states under typical conditions. Changes in air pressure or temperature are reversible; once the conditions return to their original values, the air properties will also return to their initial states without any lingering effects.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials that can store and remember past states, such as certain magnetic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
