What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber Bands: When you stretch a rubber band, it takes some time for it to return to its original shape, depending on how much it was stretched.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the current required to maintain a certain magnetic flux density can be different from the current needed to reach that same flux density when increasing the current.
Plastic Deformation: In materials like rubber or some plastics, the deformation can be irreversible under certain conditions.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a direct and proportional manner to their inputs without any memory of past states. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's Law perfectly, meaning the force is directly proportional to the displacement, with no memory of past deformations.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in electric and magnetic fields, respectively, without any hysteresis effect.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. However, certain properties of air can show hysteresis under specific conditions:

Humidity: The amount of water vapor in the air can show some hysteresis effects during the condensation and evaporation processes. For instance, the dew point temperature can change based on the history of humidity changes.
Thermal Properties: While air itself doesn't show hysteresis, the thermal capacity of air can change based on its history of heating and cooling cycles, but this is more related to the kinetic energy of molecules rather than a hysteresis loop.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems that remember past conditions, while linear systems and ideal components do not exhibit hysteresis. Air, as a simple gas, does not show hysteresis, but some properties of air can exhibit hysteresis-like behavior under specific conditions.
