Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it has taken to get there, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape based on temperature changes, and their behavior can be hysteric.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of previous states in the same way as magnetic materials.

Air:
Air does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain a "memory" of its past states in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air, such as pressure and temperature, depend on the current conditions and not on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems that can store and recall information about their past states, such as magnetic materials and some shape memory alloys. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
