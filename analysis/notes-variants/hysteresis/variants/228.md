What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist. Hysteresis is commonly seen in magnetic materials, mechanical systems, and some electronic components.

Examples of Materials with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These include ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of the magnetic field applied to them.
Rubber and Elastic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the energy required to deform the material is not fully recoverable when the load is removed.
Some Electronic Components: Certain types of capacitors and inductors can show hysteresis under certain conditions.

Elements and Systems without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These are systems where the output is directly proportional to the input, and there is no memory of past inputs. Examples include simple resistors, capacitors, and inductors in an ideal circuit.
Ideal Springs: In an ideal spring, the force exerted is directly proportional to the displacement, and there is no hysteresis.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: Under ideal conditions, these components do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a fluid and does not retain any memory of past states. When you compress air, it expands again to its original volume when the pressure is released, without retaining any "memory" of the compression.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those that can store and remember energy or state information over time. Air, being a fluid, does not have hysteresis.
