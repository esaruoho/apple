What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains. Even after the external magnetic field is removed, the material retains some magnetization, which is known as remanence. The relationship between the magnetic field strength (H) and the magnetic flux density (B) is described by the hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, although it is less common than in metals.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals like aluminum, copper, and gold, as well as insulators like glass and rubber.
Pure gases generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states when pressure or temperature changes.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis. Gases do not retain any memory of past states; once an external condition (like pressure or temperature) changes and then returns to its original value, the gas will return to its initial state without any residual effect. Therefore, air does not have a hysteresis loop.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with magnetic materials and certain types of ceramics and polymers, while most other materials and gases do not exhibit this property.
