What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly when they undergo repeated magnetization cycles. The most common example is iron, which shows a distinct hysteresis loop in its magnetic properties.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, especially under cyclic loading.
Some Ceramics and Glasses: Certain types of ceramics and glasses can also exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical behavior.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits behave linearly, meaning their response is directly proportional to the input without any memory of past inputs.
Ideal Springs: In an ideal spring, the force is directly proportional to the displacement (Hooke's Law), and there is no hysteresis.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in electric and magnetic fields, respectively, and do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and behaves as a linear medium under normal conditions. The pressure-volume relationship for an ideal gas follows the ideal gas law, which is a linear relationship and does not depend on past states.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in complex materials and systems, such as magnetic materials and some polymers, while simpler systems like air and ideal electrical components do not exhibit this property.
