Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. When these materials are exposed to an external magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis in their mechanical or electrical properties under certain conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis in their basic properties. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis in their conductivity or thermal properties.
Pure gases, such as air, do not exhibit hysteresis in their fundamental physical properties under normal conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in terms of its basic physical properties (such as pressure, temperature, and density). However, when considering more complex systems involving air, such as air conditioning systems or weather patterns, hysteresis might be observed due to the interactions within those systems rather than the properties of the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis in its basic physical properties.
