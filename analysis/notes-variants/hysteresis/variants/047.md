What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of soils.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis because their magnetic domains align with an applied magnetic field, and this alignment can persist even after the field is removed. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves due to the internal rearrangement of polymer chains.
Some Soils: Certain types of soil can exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain behavior under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most Metals (excluding ferromagnetic ones): Pure metals like copper or aluminum do not show significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to extreme conditions or undergo phase changes.
Non-magnetic Materials: Materials that do not respond magnetically, such as wood or glass, do not exhibit hysteresis.
Simple Fluids: Pure liquids and gases generally do not show hysteresis in their basic properties.

Air:
Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis in its basic thermodynamic properties. The state of air (temperature, pressure, volume) depends only on the current conditions and not on its past history. However, if you consider more complex phenomena involving air, such as the behavior of air in a compressor or turbine where there are repeated cycles of compression and expansion, you might observe some hysteresis-like effects due to the internal energy storage and release within the system. But this is not inherent to the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those that involve memory effects or internal rearrangements. Simple elements and gases like air do not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
