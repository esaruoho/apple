What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, especially under cyclic loading.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can also exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their electrical properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties.
Non-polymeric Solids: Pure crystalline solids typically do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to complex mechanical or thermal treatments.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. For example, if you compress air and then release it, the air will return to its original state without retaining any "memory" of the compression.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic or elastic properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
