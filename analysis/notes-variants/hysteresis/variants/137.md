What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: The magnetization of ferromagnetic materials (like iron) depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them. This is why a magnet can retain its magnetic properties even after the external magnetic field is removed.
Rubber Bands: When stretching a rubber band, the force required to stretch it further increases as it stretches more, due to the elastic properties of the material.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis in their temperature response, meaning they may show different readings for the same temperature depending on whether the temperature was increasing or decreasing.
Solenoids: In some solenoids, the resistance changes with the direction of the current flow, leading to hysteresis in the current-voltage characteristics.

Elements and Materials Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits behave linearly, meaning their output is directly proportional to their input without any memory of past inputs.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's Law, where the force is directly proportional to the displacement, without any hysteresis.
Pure Resistors: Pure resistors do not exhibit hysteresis because the current through them is directly proportional to the voltage across them, regardless of the history of voltages.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that responds linearly to changes in pressure and temperature within the limits of its phase (e.g., liquid or gas). The properties of air such as density and viscosity do not depend on the history of pressure or temperature changes; they respond directly to the current conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or nonlinear responses. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
