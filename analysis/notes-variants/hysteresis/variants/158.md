What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. The relationship between the magnetic field strength and the magnetization is hysteresis.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the current required to maintain a certain magnetic flux can depend on the previous history of current changes.
Elastic Deformation: Some materials exhibit elastic hysteresis, meaning the stress-strain curve does not return to the origin when the load is removed. Rubber is an example of this.
Thermal Systems: Some thermal systems can exhibit hysteresis, such as phase transitions in materials like water freezing and melting.

Elements and Materials with Hysteresis:
Metals: Many metals, especially ferromagnetic ones, show hysteresis.
Polymers: Certain polymers can exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties.
Ceramics: Some ceramics can show hysteresis in their electrical or magnetic properties.

Elements and Materials without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper or aluminum do not show significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to magnetic fields.
Non-ferromagnetic Materials: Materials like aluminum, gold, and silver do not show hysteresis under normal conditions.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships (like Ohm's law for resistors) do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not have memory of past states in the way that more complex materials do. However, air can participate in hysteresis loops in certain systems, such as in the case of humidity in air, which can be described by hysteresis loops in psychrometric charts.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and remember past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis, but it can be part of hysteresis loops in specific contexts.
