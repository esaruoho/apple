What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions and affect its response to future conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties after the removal of an external magnetic field.
Semiconductors and Insulators: These materials generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic behavior (e.g., through doping or other treatments).

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-retentive medium. However, air can be used as a medium for carrying magnetic fields, and it can influence magnetic phenomena, but it does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way as magnetic materials do.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with magnetic materials and certain types of ceramics and polymers, while non-magnetic metals and semiconductors typically do not exhibit this property. Air, being a non-magnetic medium, does not have hysteresis.
