What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis in their electrical or thermal properties.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain a "memory" of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air, such as pressure, temperature, and density, change based on the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials and systems, especially those with complex internal structures or magnetic properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
