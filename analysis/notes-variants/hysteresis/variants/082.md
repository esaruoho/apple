What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.
Metals and Alloys: Some alloys, such as those used in magnetic recording media, exhibit hysteresis.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most simple materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example:
Non-magnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show hysteresis because they do not retain any magnetic properties.
Insulators: Most insulating materials do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic behavior (which is rare).

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is an inert gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, air can be affected by changes in temperature and pressure, which can lead to changes in its density and other physical properties, but these effects are not hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials with memory, particularly magnetic materials, while most simple elements and gases do not exhibit this property.
