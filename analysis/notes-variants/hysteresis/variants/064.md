Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions and affect its response to future ones.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most metals and non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure elements like gold, silver, and platinum typically do not show hysteresis unless they are part of a complex system or undergo phase changes.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, properties related to air, such as pressure and temperature, can be influenced by historical conditions, but this is not hysteresis in the strict sense. For example, if you cool air and then heat it back up, the final state might depend on the cooling rate and path taken, but this is more about thermodynamics than hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials that can store and recall information about past states, such as magnetic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way.
