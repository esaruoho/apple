Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like aluminum, copper), glass, and many plastics.
Pure gases like air do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have a memory effect related to their state changes.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states once those states have changed. For example, if you heat air and then cool it back down, the air will simply return to its initial state without any lingering effects from the heating process.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic properties or complex internal structures. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
