What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past exposure to magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape based on temperature changes and can remember their previous shape, exhibiting hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and gold, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states under normal conditions.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. Gases do not retain any "memory" of past states; their properties (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) depend only on the current conditions and not on the path taken to reach those conditions. Therefore, air does not exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific types of materials, particularly magnetic and some ferroelectric materials, while most other elements and gases do not exhibit this property.
