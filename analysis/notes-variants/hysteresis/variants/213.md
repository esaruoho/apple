What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until a threshold temperature is reached, exhibiting hysteresis.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic memory.
Semiconductors: While semiconductors can exhibit some form of hysteresis in their electrical properties, it is generally less pronounced than in magnetic materials.
Insulators: Pure insulating materials typically do not show hysteresis unless they undergo phase transitions or have specific structural properties.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in terms of its physical or chemical properties. However, air can participate in processes that involve hysteresis, such as the hysteresis loop seen in the adsorption of gases on surfaces, but this is not a property of the air itself but rather of the interaction between the air and the surface.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields or complex mechanical behaviors. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
