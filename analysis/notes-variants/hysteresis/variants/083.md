Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well. Common examples include magnetic materials, springs, and some types of memory devices.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis when they are exposed to varying magnetic fields. The magnetization (or magnetic flux density) of these materials depends on their past exposure to magnetic fields.
Rubber and Elastic Materials: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the previous deformations.
Some Types of Memory Devices: Certain types of non-volatile memory, like magnetic RAM, can exhibit hysteresis in their data retention characteristics.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a direct and proportional manner to their inputs. Examples include simple resistors or capacitors in electrical circuits.
Ideal Springs: In an ideal spring, the force is directly proportional to the displacement, and there is no memory of past displacements.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in electric and magnetic fields, respectively, without any hysteresis effect.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. However, certain properties related to air can show hysteresis under specific conditions:

Humidity in Air: The relative humidity of air can show some hysteresis effects due to the adsorption and desorption of water vapor on surfaces, especially in porous materials. This can be seen in the behavior of hygroscopic materials over time.
Thermal Properties: While air itself doesn't show hysteresis, the thermal capacity of air can change depending on its history, such as how it was heated or cooled over time.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and remember past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis, though related properties can show some form of hysteresis under specific conditions.
