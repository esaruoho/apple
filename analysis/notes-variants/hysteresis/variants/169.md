What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber and Springs: These materials exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the energy required to deform them is not fully recoverable.
Thermocouples: The voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history.
Saturable Inductors: In electrical circuits, the inductance of a saturable inductor can change depending on the previous current through it.

Elements and Materials Without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Elements like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties.
Non-saturable Materials: Materials that do not become permanently deformed or do not retain magnetic properties after the removal of the applied force or field.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in terms of its physical properties such as pressure, volume, or temperature. However, the behavior of air in certain systems (like in compressors or expansion valves) might show some hysteresis due to the mechanical aspects of the system rather than the intrinsic properties of air.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields, mechanical deformation, or thermal changes. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
