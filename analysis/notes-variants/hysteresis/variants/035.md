What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber Bands: When a rubber band is stretched, it takes some time to return to its original shape, depending on how much it was stretched previously.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the current required to maintain a certain magnetic flux can depend on the previous history of current changes.
Thermocouples: The temperature reading from a thermocouple can be affected by the temperature history, especially if there are rapid changes.

Elements and Systems with Hysteresis:
Materials with Memory Effects: Such as ferromagnetic materials, which exhibit hysteresis loops in their magnetization curves.
Mechanical Systems: Like springs or rubber bands, which show hysteresis in their deformation behavior.
Electronic Components: Certain types of transistors and capacitors can exhibit hysteresis under certain conditions.

Elements and Systems without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a direct and proportional manner to their inputs, without any memory effect.
Ideal Resistors: In an ideal resistor, the voltage across it is directly proportional to the current through it, with no hysteresis.
Capacitors and Inductors: Under ideal conditions, these components do not exhibit hysteresis; they store and release energy based solely on the current or voltage at the moment.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not have memory effects like those seen in magnetic materials or mechanical systems. The properties of air (such as pressure and temperature) change in response to current conditions and do not depend on past conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects, while linear systems and simple gases like air do not exhibit hysteresis.
