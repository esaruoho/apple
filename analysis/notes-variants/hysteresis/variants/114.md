What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) versus magnetic flux density (B) curve forms a loop, known as the hysteresis loop.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature, and their behavior can show hysteresis.
Piezoelectric Materials: These materials can convert mechanical stress into electrical energy and vice versa, and their response can be hysteresis-dependent.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties under varying conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics.
Pure metals that do not form magnetic domains typically do not show hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that materials with internal structures do. However, air can exhibit some hysteretic behavior in certain contexts, such as in the case of humidity. The relationship between temperature and relative humidity can show some hysteresis due to the time it takes for water vapor to equilibrate in the air.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with complex materials and systems that have internal states or memories, while simple substances like air do not inherently exhibit this property.
