What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Solenoids: The magnetic flux in a solenoid depends on whether it was previously magnetized or demagnetized.
Rubber Bands: When stretched, rubber bands exhibit hysteresis due to their elastic properties.
Thermocouples: In some cases, thermocouples can show hysteresis, especially if they experience rapid temperature changes.
Soil and Rocks: These can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves under cyclic loading.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most simple linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a resistor in an electrical circuit follows Ohm's law without memory of past voltages.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in a way that is directly proportional to the applied voltage or current, respectively, without any memory effect.
Purely Resistive Loads: Pure resistors do not exhibit hysteresis because their resistance is constant regardless of the history of the applied voltage or current.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis in the same sense as materials do. However, certain properties related to air can show behavior that might be loosely described as having "hysteresis" under specific conditions:

Humidity in Air: The relative humidity of air can show some hysteresis-like behavior when considering the process of condensation and evaporation. For instance, the amount of water vapor that can be held in air at a given temperature can change depending on the history of temperature and humidity changes.
Pressure and Temperature: In certain processes involving air, such as in the operation of compressors or in weather systems, the relationship between pressure and temperature can show some non-linear behavior that might be interpreted as hysteresis in certain contexts.

In summary, while many materials and systems exhibit hysteresis, air itself does not typically show this property in a straightforward manner.
