What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber and Elastic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the energy required to deform them is not fully recoverable when they return to their original shape.
Thermocouples: The voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history, not just the current temperature.
Saturable Inductors: In electrical circuits, inductors can exhibit hysteresis in their magnetic properties, affecting the current-voltage relationship.

Elements That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Materials: Materials like copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show hysteresis because they do not retain any magnetic properties.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between input and output do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal models, capacitors and inductors do not exhibit hysteresis; they simply store and release energy without retaining any "memory" of past conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a non-viscous, non-polar fluid. It does not retain any memory of past pressure or temperature changes in the same way that materials with hysteresis do. However, air can exhibit some behavior that might seem similar to hysteresis under certain conditions:

Thermal Expansion: While not exactly hysteresis, air can expand and contract based on temperature changes, which can be somewhat reversible but not in a hysteric manner.
Pressure Changes: In terms of pressure, air can compress and decompress, but this process is typically rapid and does not retain any "memory" of past pressures.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of specific materials and systems, particularly those that can retain some form of "memory" of past conditions. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
