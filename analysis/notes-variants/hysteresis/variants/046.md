What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber Bands: When you stretch a rubber band, it will return to its original shape when released, but if you stretch it beyond its elastic limit, it may not fully return to its original shape.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the magnetic flux density (B) depends not only on the current (I) but also on the previous history of current changes.
Thermocouples: The voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history of the junction.
Plastic Deformation: In materials that undergo plastic deformation, the stress-strain curve is not symmetric; the material deforms more easily in one direction than the other.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a way that is directly proportional to the input without any memory of past inputs. For example, a simple resistor or capacitor in an electrical circuit.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's law perfectly, where the force is directly proportional to the displacement, and there is no memory of past displacements.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components do not exhibit hysteresis because their behavior is purely dependent on the current or voltage at the moment, not on the history.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. However, certain properties of air can be influenced by past conditions:

Temperature: While air does not have hysteresis in the strict sense, the temperature of air can be influenced by its history. For example, if air is heated and then cooled, it might not return to its original state due to factors like humidity changes or heat retention in the container.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems that remember past conditions, while linear and ideal systems do not exhibit this behavior. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently show hysteresis, but its properties can be influenced by past conditions.
