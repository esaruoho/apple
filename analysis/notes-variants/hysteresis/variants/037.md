What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and the resulting magnetization (M) do not immediately return to their original values when the external magnetic field is removed; instead, they follow a characteristic hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials like copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not exhibit significant hysteresis.
Pure metals like gold or silver, which are not ferromagnetic, generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to special treatments or conditions that induce it.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is not a material that retains memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. However, properties related to air, such as pressure and temperature, can be influenced by historical conditions, but this is not hysteresis. For example, if you heat air and then cool it back down, the air will not retain any "memory" of the heating process in terms of its behavior.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials and systems, particularly magnetic ones, while air and other non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis.
