What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is why you can use a magnet to attract metal objects after it has been demagnetized.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases like air, which are not magnetic or do not have internal magnetic structures, do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is not a material that retains memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and the path taken to reach those conditions, but they do not "remember" past states in the same way that magnetic materials do.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with magnetic materials and some specific types of ceramics and polymers. Air, being a gas and not having magnetic properties, does not exhibit hysteresis.
