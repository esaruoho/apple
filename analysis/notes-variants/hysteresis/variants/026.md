What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) versus magnetic flux density (B) curve for these materials forms a loop, known as the hysteresis loop.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization properties when subjected to electric fields.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol can exhibit hysteresis in their shape changes when heated or cooled.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases, such as air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way as solid or liquid materials.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. Gases do not retain a "memory" of past conditions in the same way that solids or liquids do. When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it will respond immediately and directly to the new conditions without any lag or retention of past states.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain types of materials, particularly those that can store and recall information about past states, such as magnetic and ferroelectric materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
