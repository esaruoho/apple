What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature, and they exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can show hysteresis in their electrical properties under certain conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Pure gases, such as air, do not exhibit hysteresis in their physical properties under normal conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not have memory-like properties. The behavior of air is primarily governed by its pressure, temperature, and volume, which follow well-defined thermodynamic laws. These properties do not depend on the past states of the system; they only depend on the current conditions.

In summary, while many materials and systems can exhibit hysteresis, simple elements like air do not.
