Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is why magnetic tapes, hard disks, and transformers use such materials.

Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves. This means that the deformation of the material depends on how it was previously deformed.

Some Electronic Components: Certain types of electronic components, like certain types of capacitors or inductors, can exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Biological Systems: In some biological contexts, hysteresis can be observed in processes like gene expression, where the state of a gene can depend on its previous state.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Many materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example:

Linear Materials: Most linear elastic materials (like steel or aluminum) do not show hysteresis in their stress-strain behavior unless they undergo significant plastic deformation.
Non-Ferromagnetic Materials: Materials that do not become permanently magnetized when exposed to a magnetic field will not show hysteresis in their magnetic properties.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change in response to current conditions and do not depend on past conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and remember past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
