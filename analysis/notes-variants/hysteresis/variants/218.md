What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions and affect its response to future ones.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can show hysteresis under stress or strain.
Some Polymers: Some plastics and rubbers can exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain a permanent magnetic field.
Insulators: Pure insulating materials generally do not show hysteresis unless they undergo phase transitions or have complex internal structures.
Simple Gases: Pure gases typically do not show hysteresis in their physical properties.

Air (Gas) and Hysteresis:
Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis in its basic physical properties such as pressure, volume, and temperature. Simple gases follow ideal gas laws and do not retain any memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, if you consider more complex systems involving air, such as those involving phase changes (like condensation and evaporation), there might be some hysteresis-like behavior, but it is not inherent to the gas itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or magnetic properties. Air, as a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
