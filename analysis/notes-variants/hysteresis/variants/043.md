Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can also show hysteresis, particularly those used in piezoelectric applications.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, especially under cyclic loading.
Some Electronic Components: Certain types of capacitors and inductors can exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors, capacitors, and inductors in purely linear circuits do not show hysteresis.
Idealized Models: In idealized models or simulations, systems are often assumed to be non-hysteretic unless specified otherwise.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and behaves according to the laws of thermodynamics and fluid dynamics, which are generally linear and do not depend on the history of pressure or temperature changes. However, certain properties related to air, such as humidity in air, can exhibit hysteresis due to the adsorption and desorption processes of water vapor on surfaces, but this is more related to the material properties of the surfaces rather than the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields, polymers, and some electronic components. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
