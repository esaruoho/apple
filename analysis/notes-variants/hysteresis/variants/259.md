What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of springs.

Elements with Hysteresis

Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis because their magnetization depends on the history of the magnetic field applied to them. When the magnetic field changes direction or intensity, the material's magnetization lags behind, creating a hysteresis loop.
Rubber and Other Elastomers: These materials can deform under stress, and the deformation depends on the previous stresses applied. When the stress is removed, the material may not return exactly to its original shape, showing hysteresis in its strain.
Some Types of Springs: Certain types of springs, especially those made from materials like nickel-titanium (NiTi) alloys, can exhibit hysteresis due to their shape-memory properties.

Elements without Hysteresis

Many materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example:
Linear Elastic Materials: These materials behave linearly, meaning their response to an applied force is directly proportional to the force. Examples include most metals and ceramics under small deformations.
Ideal Fluids: Fluids that follow simple models like ideal gases or Newtonian fluids do not exhibit hysteresis because their behavior is consistent and does not depend on past conditions.

Air and Hysteresis

Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple fluid that follows basic physical laws such as the ideal gas law, which describes its behavior in terms of pressure, volume, and temperature. The properties of air change in a straightforward manner based on these variables, without any memory of past conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and remember past states. Air, being a simple fluid, does not exhibit hysteresis.
