Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past exposure to magnetic fields.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), insulators, and gases.
Pure elements that do not form complex magnetic structures typically do not show hysteresis.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is not a material that retains memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. However, properties related to air, such as its pressure or temperature, can exhibit hysteresis-like behavior under certain conditions. For example, in a closed system where air pressure is repeatedly cycled between two values, the system might show some form of hysteresis due to thermal inertia or other factors, but this is not inherent to air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials, especially those with complex magnetic properties, while most simple substances like air do not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
