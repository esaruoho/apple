What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to an external magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber Bands: When you stretch a rubber band, it will return to its original shape when the force is removed, but if you stretch it beyond its elastic limit, it may not fully return to its original shape.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the magnetic flux density (B) depends on the current (I) and the history of the current changes.
Spring-Mass Systems: In some spring-mass systems, the displacement depends on the history of forces applied.

Elements That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a direct and proportional manner to their inputs without any memory of past states.
Simple Resistors: In purely resistive circuits, the current through a resistor is directly proportional to the voltage across it, with no hysteresis.
Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal capacitors and inductors, the charge or current is directly related to the voltage or magnetic field, respectively, without any hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not have the complex internal structure or memory properties required for hysteresis. The behavior of air is generally linear and does not depend on past conditions in the same way that materials with hysteresis do.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or memory effects. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
