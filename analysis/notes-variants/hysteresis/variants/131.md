What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases, including air, do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials that can be magnetized, such as ferromagnetic metals, and in some specialized ceramics and polymers. Air and other simple gases do not exhibit hysteresis.
