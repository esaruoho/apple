What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains, and when the field is removed, the domains remain aligned to some extent, leading to a hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, though it is less common than in metals.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have the internal structure or memory that would allow for such behavior.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change in response to current conditions but do not depend on the history of those conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials, particularly magnetic ones, while air and other simple substances do not exhibit this property.
