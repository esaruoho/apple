What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. Air is a gas, and gases do not retain a memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do. When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it responds immediately to the new conditions without any "memory" of previous states.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials, particularly magnetic ones, while most other elements and substances do not exhibit this property. Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis.
