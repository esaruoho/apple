What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is why they show a hysteresis loop when plotted against applied magnetic field strength.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and iron (in their non-magnetic forms) do not exhibit hysteresis.
Non-Ferromagnetic Materials: Materials like glass, wood, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to special treatments or conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a gas and does not retain any memory of previous states in the way that magnetic materials do. However, air can exhibit some behavior that might seem similar to hysteresis in certain contexts, such as changes in pressure and temperature during phase transitions (like condensation and vaporization), but this is not true hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic properties, while air and many other materials do not exhibit hysteresis.
