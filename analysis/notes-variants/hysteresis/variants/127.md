What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases, including air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions. When you change the temperature or pressure of air, it responds immediately to the new conditions without any lag or dependence on previous conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic properties, while most elements and simple substances like air do not exhibit this behavior.
