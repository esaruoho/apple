Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Rubber and Elastomers: These materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the previous loading history.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Non-elastomeric Polymers: Most plastics and rigid polymers do not exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air, as a gas, does not typically exhibit hysteresis. The behavior of gases is generally described by equations of state (like the ideal gas law) which relate pressure, volume, and temperature. While there can be some complex behaviors in certain conditions (such as phase transitions or non-ideal gas effects), these do not generally involve hysteresis in the same way as magnetic materials or elastomers.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic properties or elastic deformation. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis in its standard physical properties.
