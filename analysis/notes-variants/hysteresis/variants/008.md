What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Hysteresis loops are often seen in magnetic materials, rubber, and some mechanical systems.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. These materials show a hysteresis loop when plotted against applied magnetic field strength and magnetic flux density.
Rubber and Elastomers: These materials can also show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, indicating energy loss during deformation and recovery.
Some Mechanical Systems: Certain mechanical systems, like springs with friction or dampers, can exhibit hysteresis in their response to external forces.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Materials: Materials like copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Ideal Springs: In an ideal spring, the force required to stretch or compress it is directly proportional to the displacement, and there is no hysteresis.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between input and output (like resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits) do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is not a material that retains memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials or other physical systems do. However, the behavior of air in certain contexts (such as changes in temperature and pressure) can be described using hysteresis-like phenomena, but this is more about the thermodynamic properties of air rather than intrinsic hysteresis.

For example:
The expansion and contraction of air in a closed system can show some behavior that resembles hysteresis due to factors like heat exchange with the surroundings, but this is not the same as the hysteresis seen in magnetic materials.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of specific materials and systems, and air does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
