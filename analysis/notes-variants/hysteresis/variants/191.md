What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed, leading to a hysteresis loop.
Shape Memory Alloys (SMAs): These alloys, like nitinol, can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until the temperature changes again. The process of heating and cooling can create a hysteresis loop.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.
Thermocouples: Certain types of thermocouples can exhibit hysteresis in their temperature-to-voltage response.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Linear systems, which respond directly and proportionally to their inputs, do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions. However, certain properties of air can be considered in terms of hysteresis-like behavior under specific circumstances:

Humidity: While not exactly hysteresis, the relationship between temperature and relative humidity can sometimes exhibit a behavior similar to hysteresis, especially in the context of dehumidification and humidification processes.
Pressure: In very specific contexts, such as the behavior of air in certain types of compressors or expanders, there might be a slight lag in response due to the thermal inertia of the air, but this is not true hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with complex materials and systems that can store and release energy or information over time. Simple elements like air do not exhibit hysteresis in the same way.
