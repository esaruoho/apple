What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way that materials with internal structures do.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any "memory" of past states. When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it responds immediately and does not depend on its previous state. The behavior of air is governed by thermodynamic principles rather than hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures like ferromagnetic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
