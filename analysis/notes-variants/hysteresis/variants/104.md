Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the changes in the input. Materials with magnetic properties, for example, exhibit hysteresis in their magnetization curves.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it becomes magnetized. If the magnetic field is then removed, the material retains some magnetization, which is called remanence. When the magnetic field is reapplied, the magnetization curve does not return to zero immediately but follows a path that depends on the previous history of the magnetic field. This is known as the hysteresis loop.
Elastic Deformation: Some materials, like rubber, can exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curves. The stress required to deform the material and the stress required to return it to its original shape are different, depending on the history of deformation.
Thermal Systems: In some thermal systems, the temperature response can depend on the past heating and cooling cycles, although this is less common and typically less pronounced than in magnetic systems.

Elements and Other Non-Material Systems:
Elements: Pure chemical elements do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have internal states that depend on past conditions. For example, pure iron will not show a hysteresis loop when tested for magnetic properties.
Non-Material Systems: Non-material systems such as mathematical models, digital circuits, and some types of software algorithms do not exhibit hysteresis unless specifically designed to do so.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that responds to pressure and temperature changes without retaining any "memory" of past conditions. The behavior of air is described by thermodynamic equations and does not follow a hysteresis loop.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with internal states that depend on past conditions. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
