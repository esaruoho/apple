Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of mechanical systems.

Elements with Hysteresis

Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis when they are exposed to changing magnetic fields. The magnetization of these materials depends on both the current magnetic field and the history of previous magnetic fields.
Rubber and Elastomers: These materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the history of loading and unloading cycles.
Some Mechanical Systems: Certain mechanical systems can exhibit hysteresis, particularly those involving friction or other non-linear behaviors.

Elements without Hysteresis

Many simple materials and systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example:

Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between inputs and outputs do not exhibit hysteresis.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal electrical components, the charge and current are directly proportional to the voltage and current, respectively, without any memory of past states.
Ideal Springs: Ideal springs follow Hooke's Law, where the force is directly proportional to the displacement, without any hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis

Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is an ideal gas under normal conditions. The properties of air (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) change in a straightforward, non-memory-dependent manner according to the ideal gas law. However, if you consider the behavior of air in a specific system, such as a compressor or an expansion valve, the system might exhibit hysteresis due to the non-linearities introduced by the mechanical components rather than the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, and it is not a characteristic of simple elements like air under normal conditions.
