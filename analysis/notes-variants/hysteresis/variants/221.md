What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

────────────

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well. Materials with hysteresis typically exhibit behaviors such as magnetic remanence or elastic deformation.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetized material is exposed to a changing magnetic field, its magnetic properties (such as magnetization) depend on the history of the applied field. This is why hysteresis loops are often used to describe the relationship between magnetic field strength and magnetization.
Elastic Materials: In some elastic materials, the stress-strain relationship can be hysteresic, meaning that the strain does not return to zero when the stress is removed, especially if the material has been cyclically loaded.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis in their temperature-to-electrical signal conversion, which can affect the accuracy of temperature measurements.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most simple linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits behave linearly without memory of past conditions.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's law perfectly, meaning it does not retain any deformation after the force is removed, thus showing no hysteresis.
Ohmic Conductors: Conductors with constant resistance do not show hysteresis because their resistance does not change based on past conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. The properties of air, such as pressure, volume, and temperature, follow simple relationships described by the ideal gas law under normal conditions. There is no inherent memory effect in the behavior of air that would cause hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and release energy in a way that depends on their past states. Air, being a simple fluid, does not exhibit hysteresis.
