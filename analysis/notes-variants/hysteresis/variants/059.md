What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they become magnetized, and when the field is removed, they retain some magnetization. The relationship between the magnetic field and the magnetization is described by the hysteresis loop.

Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to electric fields.

Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Materials: Materials that do not exhibit magnetic properties generally do not show hysteresis.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships (where the output is directly proportional to the input) do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include resistors, capacitors, and inductors in purely resistive circuits.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, certain properties of air, such as its density or pressure, can be affected by past conditions, but this is not hysteresis. For example, if you compress air and then release it, the air will return to its original state, showing no hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of specific materials and systems, particularly those that can store and remember past states, such as magnetic materials and shape memory alloys. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
