What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to an external magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanent magnetization.
Spring-Mass Systems: In some mechanical systems, the force required to stretch or compress a spring can depend on whether the spring was previously stretched or compressed.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis in their temperature-to-voltage response.
Plastic Deformation: In materials that undergo plastic deformation, the stress-strain curve may not be symmetric; the material may deform more under tension than it returns to under compression.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Elements: Pure elements like metals do not typically exhibit hysteresis unless they are part of a complex system or undergo phase changes that involve hysteresis (e.g., magnetic phase transitions).
Simple Mechanical Systems: Basic elements like springs or masses do not inherently show hysteresis unless they are part of a more complex system that introduces memory effects.

Things That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between inputs and outputs do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit.
Idealized Models: Idealized models of physical systems often do not account for hysteresis because these models assume perfect behavior without memory effects.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that follows ideal gas laws under normal conditions. The properties of air (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) change in a straightforward manner based on the current conditions, without retaining any "memory" of past states.

In summary, hysteresis is a property seen in specific materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects. Simple elements and basic systems generally do not exhibit hysteresis, and air is an example of a substance that does not show this behavior.
