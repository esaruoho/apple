What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not become magnetized easily.
Non-ferromagnetic Materials: Materials that do not retain magnetization, such as most plastics and many alloys, do not show hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-retentive medium. However, air can be affected by external forces (like pressure or temperature changes) which might cause it to behave in certain ways, but this is not hysteresis. For example, air can expand or contract based on temperature changes, but this is a reversible process and does not depend on the history of temperature changes.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials that can be magnetized and retain that magnetization, such as ferromagnetic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
