What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist. Materials with hysteresis typically exhibit behaviors such as magnetization, strain, or phase transitions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it becomes magnetized. If the magnetic field is then removed, the material may retain some magnetization due to hysteresis. This is why you can still feel the magnetism in an old refrigerator magnet after the external magnetic field is removed.
Elastic Strain: In some elastic materials, the stress-strain curve does not return to zero when the stress is removed; instead, it follows a different path, creating a hysteresis loop.
Phase Transitions: Some materials exhibit hysteresis during phase transitions, such as the transition between liquid and solid states, where the temperature may need to change significantly before the phase changes completely.

Elements and Simple Substances:
Simple elements like metals, gases, and pure liquids generally do not show significant hysteresis because they do not have complex internal structures or memory effects. However, some alloys and composite materials can exhibit hysteresis due to their microstructure and composition.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way as magnetic materials or other solids. Gases do not retain any "memory" of past conditions in the same manner. For example, if you compress air and then release it, the air will simply expand back to its original volume without retaining any residual effect from the compression.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or histories. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
