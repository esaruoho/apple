What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Materials with magnetic properties, for example, exhibit hysteresis in their magnetization curves.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt show hysteresis in their magnetic properties. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they become magnetized, and when the field is removed, they retain some magnetization (magnetic remanence). This behavior is described by the hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Composites: Certain types of ceramics and composite materials can also exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Materials: Materials that do not have magnetic properties, such as copper, aluminum, or glass, do not exhibit hysteresis.
Materials with Simple Elastic Behavior: Some materials that follow simple elastic behavior, like most metals under small deformations, do not show hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is not a material that retains memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, air can exhibit some forms of hysteresis-like behavior in certain contexts, such as in the case of phase transitions (e.g., condensation and evaporation) or in the context of air pressure changes over time, but this is not the same as the hysteresis seen in magnetic materials.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials with specific properties, particularly magnetic materials, while non-magnetic materials and simple elastic materials generally do not exhibit hysteresis.
