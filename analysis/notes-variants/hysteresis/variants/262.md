What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to a magnetic field, it will retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The magnetization (output) depends on the history of the applied magnetic field (input).
Solenoids and Springs: In mechanical systems, the force required to extend or compress a spring can depend on how much it has been extended or compressed in the past.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis due to the thermal history of the material.
Plastic Deformation: In materials science, the deformation of some materials can be hysteresis-dependent, meaning the material may deform more under stress than it returns to its original shape when the stress is removed.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Air: Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past conditions. However, the behavior of air in certain systems (like in a weather system or in a compressor) might show hysteresis depending on the context.

Things That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Simple Linear Systems: Systems that follow a linear relationship between input and output do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a resistor in an electrical circuit follows Ohm's law (V = IR), which is a linear relationship.
Idealized Models: Idealized models of physical systems often do not account for hysteresis because they assume perfect conditions without memory effects.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
No: Air does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way that materials or systems with memory effects do. Air pressure, temperature, and density can change in response to current conditions, but these changes do not depend on the history of those conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is a property seen in specific materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
