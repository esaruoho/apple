What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains, and when the field is removed, they do not immediately return to their original state but retain some magnetization (this is known as remanence).

Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature. The process of returning to the original shape after being deformed can be hysteresis-dependent.

Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under stress.

Capacitors: In some cases, capacitors can exhibit hysteresis in their capacitance under varying voltage conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic domains.

Semiconductors: Semiconductors generally do not show hysteresis unless they are part of a specific device or circuit configuration.

Pure Gases: Pure gases do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have internal structures or magnetic domains.

Air:

Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. It does not retain any state information from previous conditions. The properties of air (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on past conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields, shape memory alloys, and some polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
