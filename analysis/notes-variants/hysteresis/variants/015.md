What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Hysteresis loops are often seen in magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of memory devices.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetized material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it retains some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber: Rubber exhibits hysteresis in its stress-strain curve, meaning that the energy required to stretch the rubber depends on whether you are stretching or compressing it.
Saturable Inductors: These are used in power electronics and exhibit hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Some Types of Memory: Certain types of non-volatile memory (like magnetic RAM) can show hysteresis in their switching behavior.

Elements and Materials Without Hysteresis:
Many materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, most metals and simple dielectric materials do not show significant hysteresis under normal conditions. Pure elements like copper, gold, and silver typically do not exhibit hysteresis in their physical properties.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. The state of air (pressure, temperature, density) at any given moment depends solely on the current conditions and not on past conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields or certain types of mechanical deformation. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
