Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber bands, and some types of memory devices.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis when they are subjected to varying magnetic fields. The magnetization (or magnetic flux density) of these materials depends on both the current magnetic field and the history of previous magnetic fields.
Rubber Bands: When stretched, rubber bands show hysteresis in their stress-strain curve. The force required to stretch the band depends on how much it was previously stretched.
Some Types of Memory Devices: Certain types of non-volatile memory, like magnetic RAM, can exhibit hysteresis in their switching behavior.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors, capacitors, and simple springs behave linearly, meaning their response is directly proportional to the input and does not depend on past inputs.
Pure fluids (like water or air, under normal conditions) do not exhibit hysteresis because their properties (such as viscosity) do not depend on past conditions.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air, under normal conditions, does not exhibit hysteresis. Its properties, such as density and viscosity, do not depend on past conditions. However, in certain extreme conditions or with specific mixtures, there could be complex behaviors that might resemble hysteresis, but this is not a common or typical property of air.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving memory or deformation over time. Air, being a simple gas under normal conditions, does not exhibit hysteresis.
