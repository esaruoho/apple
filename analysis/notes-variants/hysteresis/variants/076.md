What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it becomes magnetized. If the field is then removed, the material may retain some magnetization, depending on the strength and direction of the field. This is known as remanence.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the magnetic flux density depends on the current flowing through it, but it can also depend on the previous states of the current.
Rubber Bands: When stretched, rubber bands exhibit hysteresis because the force required to stretch them further increases with each cycle of stretching and releasing.
Thermocouples: The voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history, not just the current temperature.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most simple linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a resistor in an electrical circuit follows Ohm's law without memory of past voltages or currents.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in electric fields and magnetic fields, respectively, but they do not retain any information about past conditions beyond their current state.
Pure Resistors: Pure resistors dissipate energy without storing it, so they do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that responds linearly to changes in pressure and temperature according to the ideal gas law. There is no memory effect in the behavior of air under normal conditions. However, if you consider more complex systems involving air, such as the behavior of air in a compressor or an expansion valve in a refrigeration cycle, these systems might exhibit hysteresis due to the mechanical and thermal processes involved.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and remember past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
