Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.

Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature. The process of returning to their original shape can be hysteresis-dependent, meaning the material may not return exactly to its original shape due to residual stresses.

Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties, especially under cyclic loading.

Capacitors and Inductors: In some circuits, capacitors and inductors can exhibit hysteresis under certain conditions, particularly in non-linear systems.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties when the external magnetic field is removed.

Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between input and output do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors and capacitors in simple RC circuits typically do not show hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a fluid and does not retain any memory of previous states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, certain properties of air, such as its density or pressure, can change in response to environmental factors and can be described using hysteresis-like behavior in specific contexts, such as in the case of cyclic loading in materials that contain air bubbles or in the context of phase transitions involving air.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that are non-linear or have memory effects. Air, being a simple fluid, does not exhibit hysteresis in the same sense as materials with complex internal structures.
