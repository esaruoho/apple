What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions and affect its response to future conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can change shape based on temperature changes and retain this shape memory, showing hysteresis in their phase transitions.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain a permanent magnetic field.
Insulators: Materials like glass, rubber, and many ceramics do not exhibit hysteresis in their magnetic properties.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in terms of its magnetic properties or other similar behaviors. However, air can be affected by external factors such as temperature and pressure, which can change its density and other physical properties, but these changes do not exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific types of materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic properties or phase transitions. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
