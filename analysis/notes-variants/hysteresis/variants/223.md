What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions and affect its response to future conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of being exposed to magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their transformation behavior.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic elements do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain a permanent magnetic field after an external magnetic field is removed.
Pure metals that are not ferromagnetic (like copper, aluminum, gold) do not show significant hysteresis.
Non-ferroelectric materials do not show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, properties related to air, such as pressure and temperature, can be described using thermodynamic cycles, but these are not examples of hysteresis. Instead, they describe how these properties change over time and under different conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific types of materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects like magnetic and ferroelectric materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
