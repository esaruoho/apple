What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well. Materials with magnetic properties, for example, exhibit hysteresis.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetized material is subjected to varying magnetic fields, it retains some of the previous field strength even when the external field is removed. This is why a magnet can stay magnetized.
Rubber Bands: When stretched, rubber bands do not return exactly to their original length when the force is removed; they retain some deformation.
Saturable Inductors: These are used in power electronics and exhibit hysteresis in their magnetic characteristics.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials show hysteresis in their polarization characteristics.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships (e.g., Ohm's law for resistors) do not exhibit hysteresis because their output is directly proportional to their input.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal electrical components, the charge or current is directly proportional to the voltage or magnetic flux, respectively, without any memory of past conditions.
Non-magnetic Materials: Materials that do not have magnetic properties will not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-saturable medium. The pressure and volume relationship in an ideal gas follows the ideal gas law, which is a linear relationship and does not depend on past states. Therefore, air does not have hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic properties or other forms of internal memory. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit this behavior.
