What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of mechanical systems.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetized material is exposed to a changing magnetic field, its magnetization will lag behind the applied field. This is known as the hysteresis loop.
Rubber: The deformation of rubber under stress depends on the previous stresses it has experienced. This is why rubber can "remember" how much it was stretched previously.
Mechanical Systems: Some mechanical systems, like springs with friction, exhibit hysteresis due to the energy dissipated during deformation.

Elements and Substances without Hysteresis:
Most metals and non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. Air is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past pressure or temperature changes in the same way that magnetic materials do.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
No, air does not have hysteresis. Air is a simple gas and its properties (like pressure and density) change in direct response to external conditions such as temperature and volume. There is no lag or dependency on past conditions for these properties.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields or complex mechanical interactions. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit this behavior.
