What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to an electric field.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until a threshold is reached, exhibiting hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, conductors like copper or aluminum do not show hysteresis in their electrical properties.
Pure gases, such as air, do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions because they do not have internal structures or memory-like behaviors.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, density, and temperature) respond directly to the current conditions and do not depend on past conditions in a hysteresis manner.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or memory-like behaviors. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
