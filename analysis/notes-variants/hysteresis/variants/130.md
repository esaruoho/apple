What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to arrive there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of applied magnetic fields.
Elastic Materials: Some elastic materials can show hysteresis under stress, particularly if they undergo cyclic loading.
Thermal Systems: Certain thermal systems can exhibit hysteresis, especially in phase change processes like melting and freezing.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: In linear systems, the response to an input is directly proportional to the input itself, and there is no memory of past inputs.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components do not exhibit hysteresis because their behavior is purely dependent on the current input (voltage for capacitors, current for inductors).

Air and Hysteresis:
Air does not typically exhibit hysteresis in the same way as some materials do. However, air pressure can be considered to have a form of hysteresis in certain contexts, such as in the behavior of air in a compressor or an expansion valve, where the pressure depends on the history of volume changes. But this is more about the mechanical behavior of the system containing the air rather than a property of the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those that are nonlinear or have memory effects. Air, while it can participate in hysteresis-like behaviors in certain mechanical systems, does not inherently possess hysteresis as a material property.
