What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Hysteresis loops are often seen in magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of mechanical systems.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetized material (like iron) is exposed to a changing magnetic field, its magnetization will lag behind the applied field. This behavior can be visualized as a hysteresis loop.
Rubber: The stress-strain relationship in rubber exhibits hysteresis, meaning that the deformation depends on the previous loading history.
Saturable Inductors: These are used in power electronics and exhibit hysteresis in their magnetic core, which affects the current-voltage characteristics.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis due to the temperature-dependent properties of the materials involved.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most simple linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors, capacitors, and inductors in an electrical circuit behave linearly and do not remember past conditions.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's Law without any memory of past deformations.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in electric and magnetic fields, respectively, but do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not have the complex internal structure or memory that would allow for such behavior. The pressure-volume relationship of an ideal gas (described by Boyle's Law) is linear and does not depend on past conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or nonlinear behaviors. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
