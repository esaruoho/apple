What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains. Even after the external magnetic field is removed, the material retains some magnetization, which is known as remanence. The relationship between the magnetic field strength and the resulting magnetization is described by the hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, though it is less common than in metals.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not have magnetic domains that can align and retain their orientation.
Semiconductors and Insulators: These materials generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic behavior (e.g., through doping or impurities).

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-polarizable medium under normal conditions. Air does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, air can exhibit other types of behavior that might seem similar to hysteresis in certain contexts, such as changes in pressure and volume during phase transitions (like condensation and vaporization), but this is not true hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with magnetic materials and certain specialized materials under specific conditions, while non-magnetic elements and air do not exhibit hysteresis.
