What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it has taken to arrive there, not just its current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber and Springs: These materials exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the energy required to stretch or compress them depends on the previous deformations.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples show hysteresis in their temperature readings due to the thermal history of the material.
Solenoids: The resistance of a solenoid can change depending on the number of times it has been energized and de-energized.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between inputs and outputs do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal capacitors and inductors, the charge or current is directly proportional to the voltage or current, respectively, without any memory of past states.
Purely Mechanical Systems: Simple mechanical systems like frictionless pulleys or ideal springs do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a homogeneous substance with no internal memory or state-dependent properties. However, when considering systems involving air, such as air conditioning systems or weather patterns, hysteresis can be observed. For example, the temperature of a room controlled by an HVAC system may show hysteresis in its response to changes in set points.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and recall information about their past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
