What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly when they are subjected to repeated cycles of magnetization and demagnetization. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until the temperature is changed again. An example is nitinol (nickel titanium).
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can show hysteresis in their electrical properties under cyclic stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Simple metals like copper or aluminum do not exhibit significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to very specific conditions.
Semiconductors: While some semiconductors can show hysteresis in their conductivity under certain conditions, many do not.
Dielectrics: Pure dielectric materials generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to very specific conditions or are part of a complex system.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, air can participate in systems that exhibit hysteresis. For example, in a refrigeration cycle, the state of the refrigerant (which is often a liquid or gas) can exhibit hysteresis as it undergoes phase transitions and cycles through different states.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly magnetic materials and shape memory alloys. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis on its own.
