What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Polymers: Certain types of polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.
Some Ceramics: Some ceramic materials can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties under cyclic voltage application.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain a permanent magnetic field after the removal of an external magnetic field.
Semiconductors: In general, semiconductors do not exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties under normal operating conditions.
Insulators: Pure insulators typically do not show hysteresis unless they undergo phase transitions or have specific defects.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states or conditions in the way that materials with hysteresis do. For example, if you compress air and then release it, the air will return to its original state without any lingering effects from previous compressions (assuming no heat exchange or other changes).

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex microstructures or magnetic properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
