What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well. Materials with magnetic properties, for example, exhibit hysteresis when they are exposed to varying magnetic fields.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetized material (like iron) is subjected to a changing magnetic field, it retains some of the magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the previous deformations.
Saturable Inductors: In electrical circuits, these components can exhibit hysteresis in their current-voltage characteristics.

Elements That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Materials: Materials like copper, aluminum, and most non-magnetic metals do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain a magnetic moment in the absence of an external magnetic field.
Air: Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-piezoelectric, and non-hysteresis material under normal conditions. However, air can be affected by other forms of hysteresis in specific contexts, such as in the case of humidity changes over time, but this is not related to the concept of hysteresis in materials science.

Summary:
Materials with Magnetic Properties: Exhibit hysteresis.
Non-magnetic Materials: Do not exhibit hysteresis.
Air: Does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
