Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is described by the hysteresis loop, which shows the relationship between the magnetic field strength (H) and the resulting magnetization (M).

Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to an electric field.

Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their response to mechanical stress and electrical fields.

Elements without Hysteresis

Most non-magnetic and non-ferroelectric materials do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. For example:

Non-Magnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis.
Insulators: Most insulating materials do not exhibit hysteresis unless they are specifically engineered or treated to do so.

Air and Hysteresis

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past states in the way that materials with internal structures do. The properties of air, such as pressure and temperature, respond directly to the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions. Therefore, air does not exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific types of materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or memory-like behaviors, such as magnetic and ferroelectric materials, shape memory alloys, and some piezoelectric materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
