What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber and Springs: These materials exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the energy required to deform them is not fully recoverable when they return to their original shape.
Thermocouples: The voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history, not just the current temperature.
Saturable Inductors: In electrical circuits, the inductance of a saturable inductor can change depending on the previous current through it.

Elements That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Materials: Materials like copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any magnetic properties after an external magnetic field is removed.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between inputs and outputs do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit behaves linearly and does not remember past conditions.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way as materials like magnets or springs. The state of air (pressure, volume, temperature) changes in response to current conditions and does not depend on its past states. However, there are some subtle effects that might be considered related to hysteresis in specific contexts:

Thermal Hysteresis: In very controlled environments, the thermal properties of air might show slight hysteresis due to the time it takes for heat to distribute evenly. But this is not a fundamental property of air itself.
Humidity: While not exactly hysteresis, the behavior of air in terms of humidity can sometimes show a kind of memory effect, especially in closed systems where the relative humidity can lag behind changes in temperature.

In summary, air does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way as magnetic materials or other hysteresis-prone materials.
