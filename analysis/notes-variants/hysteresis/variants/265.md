What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist. Common examples include magnetic materials, some types of springs, and certain types of memory devices.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis because their magnetization depends on the history of applied magnetic fields. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Some Types of Springs: Certain elastic materials can show hysteresis under stress, meaning the deformation depends on the previous loading history.
Memory Devices: Some types of memory, like magnetic storage devices, can exhibit hysteresis in their data retention properties.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most simple materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example:
Non-magnetic Metals: Like copper or aluminum, which do not retain magnetic properties after an external magnetic field is removed.
Linear Elastic Materials: Such as most plastics or rubber, which deform elastically without retaining any memory of the deformation once the load is removed.

Air:
Air does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas that expands or contracts in response to changes in pressure and temperature, without retaining any memory of past conditions. The behavior of air is described by simple thermodynamic equations and does not exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic properties or complex elastic behaviors. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
