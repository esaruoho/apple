What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed, leading to a hysteresis loop.
Some Polymers: Certain types of polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples can exhibit hysteresis in their response to temperature changes.
Sensors: Certain sensors, especially those based on magnetic or mechanical principles, can show hysteresis in their readings.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not store internal memory of past states in the way that materials with internal structures do.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. Gases do not retain any internal memory of past states in the same way that solids or certain liquids do. The state of an ideal gas (ignoring real gas effects) is determined solely by its current pressure, volume, and temperature, without any dependence on its past states.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or memory, such as magnetic materials and some polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
