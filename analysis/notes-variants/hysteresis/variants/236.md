What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence.

Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning that the deformation depends on the previous deformations.

Some Electronic Components: Certain types of electronic components, such as certain types of capacitors or inductors, can exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Thermal Systems: Some thermal systems can exhibit hysteresis, especially in phase transitions like melting and freezing.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis in their magnetic properties.

Semiconductors: Semiconductors generally do not show hysteresis unless they are part of a specific type of device (e.g., certain types of memory devices).

Air: Air does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any state information from previous conditions. The pressure and volume of air change directly in response to the current conditions, without any memory of past states.

Summary:
Has Hysteresis: Magnetic materials, some rubbers and polymers, certain electronic components, and some thermal systems.
Does Not Have Hysteresis: Non-ferrromagnetic metals, semiconductors, and air.

Air specifically does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas that responds linearly to changes in pressure and temperature, without retaining any memory of past states.
