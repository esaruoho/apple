Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the changes in the input. Common examples include magnetic materials, springs, and some types of memory devices.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis when they are subjected to varying magnetic fields. The magnetization (or magnetic flux density) of these materials depends on the history of the applied magnetic field.
Rubber and Springs: These can show hysteresis in their deformation under stress. The amount of deformation depends on the previous stresses applied.
Some Types of Memory Devices: Certain types of non-volatile memory, like magnetic RAM, can exhibit hysteresis in their switching behavior.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most simple resistors, capacitors, and inductors do not exhibit hysteresis because their behavior is linear and does not depend on past conditions.
Pure fluids and gases, under normal conditions, do not exhibit hysteresis in their properties.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air, as a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. Its properties (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) are generally linear and do not depend on past states. However, if you consider more complex systems involving air, such as those involving phase transitions (like condensation and evaporation), there might be some hysteresis-like behavior, but it is not typically referred to as hysteresis in the same way as seen in magnetic materials or other solid-state systems.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and release energy in a way that depends on their history. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
