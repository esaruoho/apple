What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and magnetic flux density (B) are related through a hysteresis loop.
Rubber and Elastomers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the previous loading history.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties under cyclic stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Materials: Most linear elastic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. They follow Hooke's Law, where stress is directly proportional to strain.
Ideal Fluids: Ideal fluids like water or air, when not considering viscosity effects, do not exhibit hysteresis in their flow characteristics.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is an ideal fluid at typical atmospheric pressures and temperatures. It follows the ideal gas law and does not retain any memory of past pressure or temperature changes in its behavior. However, if you consider the viscosity of air, which is very low, it might show some minor hysteresis-like behavior in very specific conditions, such as during rapid compression and expansion cycles, but this is not a common or significant phenomenon.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that are nonlinear or have memory effects. Air, being an ideal fluid, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
