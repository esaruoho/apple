What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can exhibit hysteresis in their shape-memory effect.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most other metals do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases generally do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. Gases do not retain memory of previous states in the same way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) depend on the current conditions rather than the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific types of materials, particularly those with magnetic or ferroelectric properties, and shape memory alloys. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
