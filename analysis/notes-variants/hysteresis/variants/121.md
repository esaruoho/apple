What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. The relationship between the magnetic field and the magnetization is described by the hysteresis loop.

Some Ferroelectric Materials: Similar to magnetic materials, some ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to an electric field.

Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and can "remember" their original shape. They exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Piezoelectric Materials: Certain piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Magnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties after the removal of an external magnetic field.

Insulators: Most insulating materials do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic or ferroelectric behavior.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-ferroelectric, and non-piezoelectric medium under normal conditions. Air does not retain any form of memory or state dependency based on past inputs in the way that materials with hysteresis do.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic, ferroelectric, or shape-memory behaviors. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
