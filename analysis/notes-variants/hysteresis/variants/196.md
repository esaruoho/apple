What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains, and when the field is removed, they do not immediately return to their original state but rather follow a path determined by their previous exposure to magnetic fields.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), insulators, and gases.
Pure gases generally do not show hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. Air is a gas and does not retain a "memory" of its previous states in the way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on past conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in magnetic materials and some specific types of ceramics and polymers, while most other materials and gases do not exhibit this property.
