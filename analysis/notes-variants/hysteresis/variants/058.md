Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to an external magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber Bands: When a rubber band is stretched, it requires more force to stretch it further, and it takes time for the band to return to its original length.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the current required to maintain a certain magnetic flux density depends on the previous history of the current.
Materials with Memory Effects: Some materials exhibit memory effects, such as shape-memory alloys (e.g., Nitinol), which can return to a pre-defined shape after being deformed.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most linear systems do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a simple resistor or capacitor in an electrical circuit does not remember past conditions.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's Law without any memory effect.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components do not exhibit hysteresis because they store energy in a way that is independent of their past states.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that responds linearly to changes in pressure and temperature according to the ideal gas law. There is no memory effect in the behavior of air under normal conditions. However, if you consider complex systems involving air, such as the behavior of air in a compressible fluid system with significant friction or heat exchange, there could be some hysteresis-like behavior due to the history of the system's state.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, while most elements and simple systems do not exhibit this property. Air, in its simplest form, does not have hysteresis.
