What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

────────────

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past exposure to magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization properties.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their transformation behavior.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions because they do not retain memory of past states.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. Its properties (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) depend only on the current conditions and not on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems that can store and remember past states, such as magnetic materials, shape memory alloys, and some ferroelectric materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
