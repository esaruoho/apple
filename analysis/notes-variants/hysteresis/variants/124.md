What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well. Materials with hysteresis typically exhibit a lag between the application and removal of an external field or force.

Examples of Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt show hysteresis when subjected to magnetic fields. The magnetization (or magnetic moment) of these materials lags behind the applied magnetic field, leading to a hysteresis loop in the magnetization vs. magnetic field strength curve.
Elastic Materials: Some elastic materials can exhibit hysteresis under stress. For example, rubber bands show a hysteresis loop in their stress-strain curves.
Thermal Systems: Some thermal systems can exhibit hysteresis, especially in phase transitions like melting and freezing. The temperature at which a material changes phase can depend on its previous history.

Elements and Other Non-Hysteresis Examples:

Non-Magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain a permanent magnetic moment.
Non-Elastic Materials: Most rigid materials like glass or ceramics do not show hysteresis under normal conditions unless they undergo plastic deformation.
Air: Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas that does not retain any memory of past pressure or temperature changes beyond the immediate effects of those changes. However, air can participate in hysteresis loops in certain systems, such as in the case of air conditioning cycles where the system's state can depend on its history.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those that can store and release energy in a way that depends on their past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis on its own.
