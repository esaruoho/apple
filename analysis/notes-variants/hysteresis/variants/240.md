What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed, leading to a hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like aluminum, copper, gold), glass, and many plastics.
Pure gases generally do not show hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states when pressure or temperature changes occur.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis. When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it will respond immediately and directly to those changes, without retaining any "memory" of previous states. The properties of air (such as density, pressure, and temperature) are determined solely by the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials and systems, particularly magnetic materials, while most other elements and substances do not exhibit this property.
