What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of applied magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to electric fields.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain a permanent magnetic field after an external magnetic field is removed.
Semiconductors: While some semiconductors can exhibit some form of hysteresis, it is generally much less pronounced than in ferromagnetic materials.
Insulators: Pure insulators typically do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic or electric behavior.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-ferroelectric, and non-piezoelectric medium under normal conditions. Air does not retain any memory of past magnetic or electric fields, nor does it exhibit phase transitions that would lead to hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic, ferroelectric, or shape-memory behaviors. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal circumstances.
