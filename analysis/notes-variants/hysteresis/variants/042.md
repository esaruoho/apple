What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), insulators, and gases.
Pure liquids typically do not show hysteresis unless they undergo phase transitions or have complex interactions.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. Gases generally do not show hysteresis because their properties (such as pressure and volume) are directly proportional to temperature and do not depend on the historical changes in those properties.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems with memory effects, such as magnetic materials and certain ceramics, while gases like air do not exhibit hysteresis under typical conditions.
