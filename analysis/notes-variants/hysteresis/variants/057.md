What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and the magnetization (M) of these materials are related through a hysteresis loop.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes depending on temperature, and they exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases, when not subjected to phase transitions or other complex behaviors, typically do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it will respond according to its thermodynamic properties, but it will not "remember" previous states in the way that materials with hysteresis do.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or phase transitions. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
