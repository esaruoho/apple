What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains, and when the field is removed, the domains remain aligned in a way that depends on the previous history of the magnetic field.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, though this is less common than in metals.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, air, water, and many other gases and liquids do not show hysteresis under normal conditions.
Pure metals and simple conductive materials generally do not exhibit hysteresis unless they are subjected to special conditions or are part of a complex system.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a gas and does not retain memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change in response to current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain materials and systems, particularly those with magnetic properties, while most elements and substances like air do not exhibit hysteresis.
