What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and the magnetic flux density (B) are related through the hysteresis loop, which shows how the material retains some magnetization even after the external magnetic field is removed.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to very specific conditions.
Pure gases, including air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of previous states in the same way that magnetic materials do.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. When you apply a pressure or temperature change to air, it will respond accordingly, but it will not "remember" this change in a way that affects future responses to similar changes.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials that can store and release energy in response to changing conditions, particularly magnetic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
