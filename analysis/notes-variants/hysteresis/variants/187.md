What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states as well.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to an external magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber and Springs: The deformation of rubber or other elastic materials depends on the previous deformations; they do not return to their original shape immediately when the force is removed.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis due to changes in their electrical resistance with temperature.
Soil and Rocks: In geology, the stress-strain relationship in some soils and rocks can be hysteretic, meaning the strain does not return to zero when the stress is removed.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a direct and proportional manner to their inputs without any memory of past inputs. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal capacitors and inductors, the voltage across a capacitor or the current through an inductor is directly proportional to the charge or magnetic flux, respectively, without any hysteresis.
Pure Resistors: Pure resistors do not exhibit hysteresis because their resistance is constant regardless of the history of the applied voltage or current.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas whose properties (such as pressure and volume) change linearly with temperature and external forces without retaining any "memory" of past conditions. However, in certain complex systems involving air, such as in weather patterns or atmospheric dynamics, there might be some indirect effects that could resemble hysteresis, but these are not inherent properties of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that are nonlinear or have some form of internal memory. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
