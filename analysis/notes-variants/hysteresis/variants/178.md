What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Solenoids: The current required to maintain a certain magnetic field strength in a solenoid can depend on the previous history of current changes.
Rubber Bands: When stretched, rubber bands exhibit hysteresis in their stress-strain curve, meaning the force required to stretch them further depends on how much they were previously stretched.
Thermocouples: In some cases, the voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history rather than just the current temperature.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Elements: Pure elements generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have complex internal structures or memory mechanisms.
Simple Mechanical Systems: Basic mechanical systems like springs (without damping or other nonlinear effects) typically do not show hysteresis unless they include components with hysteresis properties.

Things That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Most simple linear systems, such as resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits.
Pure gases under isothermal conditions (where temperature remains constant).

Air:
Air: Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not remember past pressure or temperature changes in a way that would cause a lag in its response to current conditions. However, if you consider the behavior of air in a closed system with significant thermal inertia (like a room), there could be some very minor hysteresis-like effects due to heat capacity and thermal lag, but these are negligible for most practical purposes.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in complex systems with internal memory or nonlinear responses, while simple elements and basic mechanical systems do not exhibit this property.
