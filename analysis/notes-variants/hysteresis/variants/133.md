What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the changes in the input. Common examples include magnetic materials, springs, and some types of memory devices.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: The magnetization of a material like iron depends on the history of applied magnetic fields. This is why a magnet can retain its magnetic properties even after the external field is removed.
Rubber Bands: When you stretch a rubber band, it will return to its original shape when you release it, but the amount it returns depends on how much it was stretched previously.
Solenoids: In a solenoid, the current required to achieve a certain magnetic field strength depends on the previous history of current through the coil.
Memory Devices: Some types of memory (like magnetic tapes) exhibit hysteresis in their ability to store data.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a direct and proportional manner to their inputs without any lag or dependency on past inputs.
Simple Resistors: In an electrical circuit, a resistor's resistance does not depend on the history of the voltage or current applied to it.
Capacitors and Inductors: These components store energy in electric and magnetic fields, respectively, but do not exhibit hysteresis in their basic operation.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas with no internal memory or state that depends on past conditions. However, certain properties of air, such as its density or viscosity, can be affected by temperature and pressure changes, but these effects are not hysteresis in the strict sense. For example, if you heat air and then cool it back to its original temperature, the air will not "remember" the path it took to reach that temperature.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic or mechanical properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
