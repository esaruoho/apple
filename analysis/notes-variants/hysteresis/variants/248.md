Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Common examples of hysteresis include:

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and the magnetization (M) of these materials are related through a hysteresis loop.
Elastic Materials: Some elastic materials can show hysteresis under stress, particularly if they undergo cyclic loading.
Thermal Systems: Certain thermal systems can exhibit hysteresis, especially in phase transitions like melting and freezing.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between input and output do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, Ohm's law for resistors (V = IR) describes a linear relationship and does not show hysteresis.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal electrical components, the voltage across a capacitor or the current through an inductor is directly proportional to the charge or magnetic flux, respectively, and does not depend on past conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of past states in the way that some materials do. However, certain properties of air can be influenced by past conditions, but this is not due to hysteresis. For example:
Humidity: While not exactly hysteresis, the moisture content in air can depend on past humidity levels and temperature changes, but this is more about equilibrium and thermodynamics rather than a hysteresis loop.
Pressure and Temperature: Changes in pressure and temperature of air can be tracked over time, but these are not hysteresis loops unless specific conditions are met that cause the system to remember past states.

In summary, hysteresis is a property seen in specific materials and systems, particularly those that can store and recall past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis in the same sense.
