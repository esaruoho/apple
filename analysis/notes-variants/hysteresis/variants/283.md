What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals like aluminum, copper, and gold, which do not retain magnetization when the external magnetic field is removed.
Pure gases, such as air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have the internal structure or properties necessary for this phenomenon.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
No, air does not have hysteresis. Air is a simple gas composed of molecules (primarily nitrogen and oxygen) and does not possess the complex internal structure or memory that would allow it to exhibit hysteresis. Changes in air pressure or temperature do not depend on the history of those changes in the same way that magnetic materials do.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with certain magnetic and other complex materials, while simple elements like air do not exhibit this property.
