What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of mechanical systems.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis when they are subjected to varying magnetic fields. The magnetization (or magnetic flux density) of these materials depends on the history of the applied magnetic field.
Rubber and Elastomers: These materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the deformation depends on the previous deformations.
Some Mechanical Systems: Certain mechanical systems can exhibit hysteresis, such as those involving friction or spring-mass systems under non-linear conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: In linear systems, the response is directly proportional to the input and does not depend on the history of inputs. Examples include simple resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits.
Ideal Springs and Masses: Idealized models of springs and masses do not exhibit hysteresis; they return to their original state immediately after the force is removed.
Purely Electrical Components: Pure resistors, capacitors, and inductors do not exhibit hysteresis because their behavior is linear and does not depend on past states.

Air:
Air does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way as materials like magnets or rubber. The properties of air, such as pressure and temperature, generally follow simple relationships with external forces and do not retain a "memory" of past states. However, in some complex systems involving air, such as in weather patterns or atmospheric circulation models, there might be some form of hysteresis-like behavior due to nonlinear interactions over time, but this is not a fundamental property of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that are nonlinear or involve memory effects. Air, being a fluid, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
