What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material is exposed to a magnetic field, it will retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence and is a classic example of hysteresis.
Solenoids: The magnetic flux in a solenoid depends on whether it was previously magnetized or not.
Rubber Bands: When stretched, rubber bands exhibit hysteresis because the force required to stretch them further increases with each cycle of stretching and relaxing.
Thermocouples: In some cases, thermocouples can show hysteresis due to changes in their internal resistance over temperature cycles.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Simple elements like metals, gases (such as air), and pure liquids do not typically exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. However, complex systems involving these elements can still show hysteresis if they include components that do.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of previous states in the way that magnetic materials do. However, if you consider a system involving air, such as a balloon that expands and contracts multiple times, the balloon's material might show hysteresis, but the air inside would not.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems that have memory effects, such as magnetic materials, certain types of mechanical systems, and some electronic components. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
