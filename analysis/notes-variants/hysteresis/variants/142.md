What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of applied magnetic fields.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) generally do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. The properties of air, such as pressure and temperature, change based on the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems that can store and remember past conditions, such as magnetic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
