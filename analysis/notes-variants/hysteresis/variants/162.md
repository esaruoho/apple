What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The relationship between the magnetic field strength and the resulting magnetization is described by the hysteresis loop.

Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to an electric field.

Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Magnetic Materials: Materials that do not have magnetic properties, such as copper or aluminum, do not exhibit hysteresis.
Materials Without Remanence: Materials that do not retain any magnetization after the external magnetic field is removed will not show hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is not a material that retains any state information based on its history. Air pressure, for example, will respond directly to the current conditions (such as temperature and volume) and will not remember past pressures. Therefore, air does not exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic or ferroelectric properties, while non-magnetic and non-retentive materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis.
