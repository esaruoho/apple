What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed, leading to a hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases, such as air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states when subjected to changes in pressure or temperature.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas that responds to changes in pressure and temperature according to well-defined thermodynamic laws (such as the ideal gas law). When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it will return to its initial state once the external conditions are removed, without retaining any "memory" of previous states.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in magnetic materials and certain other complex systems, while simple elements like air do not exhibit this property.
