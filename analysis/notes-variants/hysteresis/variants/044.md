What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until they are heated above a certain threshold. They exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials show hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic metals and insulators do not exhibit significant hysteresis in their basic physical properties.
Pure gases (like air) generally do not exhibit hysteresis in their fundamental properties under normal conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not have memory or persistent state changes based on past conditions. The properties of air (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) follow straightforward relationships described by the ideal gas law, and these properties do not depend on the history of changes.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic properties, phase transitions, and complex mechanical behaviors. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
