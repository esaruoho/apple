What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases (like air) do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past conditions once the conditions change.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not have hysteresis. Gases do not retain any memory of past conditions; their properties (such as pressure, volume, and temperature) change instantaneously and directly in response to external changes. There is no lag or retention of past states in the behavior of air.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems that can store and remember past conditions, such as magnetic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit this property.
