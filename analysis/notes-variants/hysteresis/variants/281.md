What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains. The relationship between the applied magnetic field and the resulting magnetization (the hysteresis loop) is a classic example of hysteresis.

Some Ferroelectric Materials: Certain ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to an electric field.

Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their phase transitions.

Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their response to mechanical stress and electrical fields.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Materials: Materials that do not have magnetic properties generally do not exhibit hysteresis.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between input and output do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, resistors and capacitors in simple circuits behave linearly and do not show hysteresis.

Air:

Air, being a non-magnetic and non-ferroelectric material, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. It does not have a memory effect related to its state based on past inputs. However, air can exhibit some form of hysteresis in certain specific contexts, such as in the case of humidity levels in air, which can depend on past temperature and moisture conditions. But this is more about the state of the air rather than a fundamental property of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems with memory effects, such as magnetic and ferroelectric materials, shape memory alloys, and certain piezoelectric materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
