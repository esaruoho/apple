What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past exposure to magnetic fields.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature and can remember their previous shape.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific treatments or conditions that induce magnetic properties.
Semiconductors: In their pure form, semiconductors do not show hysteresis. However, some semiconductor devices (like certain types of transistors) can exhibit hysteresis due to their operational characteristics.
Insulators: Pure insulators do not show hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. The properties of air, such as pressure and density, change in response to immediate conditions but do not "remember" past conditions in a hysteresis-like manner.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with internal memory or state-dependent responses. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
