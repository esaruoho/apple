What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains, and when the field is removed, the domains remain aligned to some extent, leading to a hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, such as mechanical deformation or electrical polarization.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because their magnetic domains do not align in a way that retains memory of past magnetic fields.
Semiconductors and Insulators: These materials generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic behavior (e.g., through doping or external fields).

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-polarizable medium under normal conditions. Air does not retain any memory of past magnetic fields or other stimuli in the way that magnetic materials do. Therefore, air does not exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with magnetic materials and some specific types of ceramics and polymers, while most elements and air do not exhibit this property.
