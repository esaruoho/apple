Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Rubber and Springs: These materials exhibit hysteresis due to their elastic properties. The stress-strain curve for these materials forms a loop, indicating energy loss during deformation and recovery.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples show hysteresis due to changes in their electrical resistance with temperature.
Plastic Deformation: In metals, plastic deformation can lead to hysteresis in stress-strain curves.

Elements and Simple Substances:
Simple elements or pure substances generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have complex internal structures or interactions that would cause memory effects. For example, a single element like copper or silver does not show hysteresis in its physical or chemical properties.

Things That Do Not Have Hysteresis:
Pure metals and simple compounds typically do not show hysteresis.
Liquids and gases, unless they undergo phase transitions that involve hysteresis (e.g., water freezing and melting).

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. However, certain properties of air can exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions:
Humidity: The relative humidity of air can exhibit hysteresis in the context of desiccation and hydration cycles.
Temperature: In very controlled environments, the thermal properties of air might show slight hysteresis due to heat capacity differences at different temperatures, but this is minimal and not typically significant.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in complex materials and systems with internal memory mechanisms, while simple elements and substances generally do not exhibit hysteresis.
