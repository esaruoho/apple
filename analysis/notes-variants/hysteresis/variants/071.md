What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions and affect its response to future conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The most common example is the magnetization curve of these materials.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature and can retain these changes, demonstrating hysteresis.
Some Polymers: Certain types of polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most gases and liquids do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions because they do not have a memory effect related to previous states.
Pure metals and simple compounds generally do not show significant hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce it.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis. Gases do not retain a "memory" of past conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, in certain complex systems involving air, such as in weather patterns or atmospheric circulation models, hysteresis-like behavior might be observed due to the interactions of various factors over time, but this is not a fundamental property of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials and systems that can store and recall information about past conditions, such as magnetic materials and shape memory alloys. Air, as a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal circumstances.
