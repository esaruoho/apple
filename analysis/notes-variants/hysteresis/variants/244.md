What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions and affect its response to future conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, including iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) and magnetic flux density (B) are related through a hysteresis loop.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, especially under cyclic loading.
Some Ceramics: Certain types of ceramics can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical properties under cyclic voltage application.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most metals and non-magnetic materials do not show significant hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics.
Pure water and gases generally do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air:
Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. The properties of air such as pressure, volume, and temperature follow simple thermodynamic relationships without retaining a memory of past conditions.

However, if you consider more complex systems involving air, such as those involving phase changes (like condensation and evaporation), these processes might show some form of hysteresis due to the lag between the input conditions and the resulting state changes. But this is not a fundamental property of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems that can store and retain information about past states, such as magnetic materials and certain polymers. Air, as a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal circumstances.
