Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their history of magnetic field exposure.
Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties, such as stress-strain relationships.
Some Ceramics: Certain ceramic materials can also exhibit hysteresis under specific conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis in their electrical resistance or other properties.
Pure gases (like air) generally do not exhibit hysteresis in their thermodynamic properties under normal conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and its properties (such as pressure, temperature, and density) depend only on the current conditions and not on past conditions. However, if you consider complex systems involving air, such as the behavior of air in a refrigeration cycle or in a compressor, you might observe some hysteresis-like effects due to the system's memory of past states, but this is not inherent to the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials and systems that have memory of past conditions, such as magnetic materials and certain polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
