What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it will retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The amount of magnetization that remains is called remanence, and this is an example of hysteresis.
Rubber and Springs: These materials exhibit hysteresis due to their elastic properties. When stretched or compressed, they do not return to their original state immediately; instead, they store energy and release it gradually.
Thermocouples: In some cases, thermocouples can show hysteresis due to changes in their electrical resistance with temperature, which can lag behind the actual temperature change.
Soil and Water Content: In soil mechanics, the relationship between water content and stress can be hysteresis-like, especially during loading and unloading cycles.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Most simple linear systems, such as resistors, capacitors, and inductors, do not exhibit hysteresis. Their behavior is directly proportional to the input without any lag or memory effect.
Idealized Models: Idealized models of materials and systems, which assume no internal friction or memory effects, do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and follows ideal gas laws under normal conditions. However, in certain complex scenarios involving phase transitions or non-linear interactions, air could potentially exhibit hysteresis-like behavior, but this would be highly unusual and not typical.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with internal friction or memory effects. Simple elements like air do not exhibit hysteresis under normal circumstances.
