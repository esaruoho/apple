What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they align their magnetic domains, and when the field is removed, they do not immediately return to their original state but retain some magnetization, forming a hysteresis loop.
Shape Memory Alloys (SMAs): These alloys, like nitinol, can change shape in response to temperature changes and retain this shape until the temperature changes again. The process of heating and cooling can create a hysteresis loop.
Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can exhibit hysteresis in their electrical response to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not have magnetic domains that can align and retain a magnetic state.
Semiconductors: While semiconductors can show some form of hysteresis in certain applications (like in ferroelectric materials), pure semiconductors generally do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions.
Insulators: Most insulating materials do not show hysteresis unless they are specifically engineered to have such properties.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that materials with hysteresis do. For example, if you heat air and then cool it back down, the air will simply return to its initial state without retaining any "memory" of the heating process.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those involving magnetic fields, shape memory, and certain types of electrical responses. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
