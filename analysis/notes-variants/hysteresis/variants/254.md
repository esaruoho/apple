What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by past states as well.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence and is a classic example of hysteresis.
Solenoids and Relays: These devices exhibit hysteresis due to the mechanical and magnetic properties involved in their operation.
Rubber and Polymers: These materials can deform under stress and return to different shapes depending on the history of deformation.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis due to the thermal history of the material.

Elements and Simple Substances:
Simple elements like metals, gases (such as air), and pure liquids do not typically exhibit hysteresis because they do not have complex internal structures or histories that would affect their current state based on past conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas with no internal structure that retains memory of past states. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and not on past conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is a property seen in complex systems and materials with internal memory, such as magnetic materials, solenoids, and certain polymers. Simple substances like air do not exhibit hysteresis.
