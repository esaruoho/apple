What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

────────────

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, not just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it will retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence and can be visualized as a hysteresis loop.
Elastic Materials: In some elastic materials, the stress-strain relationship may exhibit hysteresis, especially under cyclic loading.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis due to changes in their electrical resistance with temperature.
Solenoids: The magnetic flux in a solenoid can show hysteresis when the coil is repeatedly energized and de-energized.

Elements and Substances without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Materials: Most metals and non-metallic materials do not exhibit significant hysteresis unless they are specifically engineered to do so.
Air: Air does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. Its properties (such as pressure and volume) depend only on the current conditions and not on the history of those conditions.

Summary:
Has Hysteresis: Magnetic materials, some elastic materials, certain types of thermocouples, and solenoids.
Does Not Have Hysteresis: Non-magnetic materials, air, and most other simple substances.

Air, being a simple gas, does not have hysteresis. It responds to changes in pressure and temperature based solely on the current conditions, not on any previous conditions.
