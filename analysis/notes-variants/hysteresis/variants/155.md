What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: The magnetization of ferromagnetic materials like iron depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them. This is why a magnet can retain its magnetism after the external magnetic field is removed.
Solenoids: When a solenoid (a coil of wire) is energized, it creates a magnetic field. If you then de-energize the solenoid, it may take some time for the magnetic field to completely dissipate, depending on the material and design.
Rubber Bands: When a rubber band is stretched, it takes some energy to do so. If you then release the band, it will return to its original shape, but if you stretch it again, it will require more force than the first time due to the stored elastic energy.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples exhibit hysteresis in their temperature readings, especially when there are rapid changes in temperature.

Elements and Systems Without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond in a direct and proportional manner to their inputs without any memory of past conditions. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal capacitors and inductors, the voltage across or current through them directly corresponds to the charge or magnetic flux, respectively, without any dependence on past conditions.
Simple Springs: A spring behaves linearly; the force required to stretch it is directly proportional to the extension, and it returns to its original shape without retaining any "memory" of previous deformations.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a fluid and does not have a memory of past states in the same way as solid materials or magnetic systems. However, certain properties of air can show behavior that might be loosely described as having some form of hysteresis under specific conditions:

Humidity: The amount of water vapor in the air can depend on past conditions, such as the rate at which moisture was added or removed. However, this is more about the kinetics of water vapor absorption and desorption rather than true hysteresis.
Pressure and Temperature: In some cases, the pressure or temperature of air can depend on the history of changes, but this is generally not what we mean by hysteresis in materials science.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects, while linear systems and fluids like air do not exhibit hysteresis.
