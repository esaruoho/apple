What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetic field strength (H) versus magnetic flux density (B) curve for these materials forms a loop, known as the hysteresis loop.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel-titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature, and their behavior can show hysteresis.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), ceramics, and many plastics.
Pure gases, including air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. When you change the pressure or temperature of air, it responds immediately to the new conditions without remembering the previous ones.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with specific types of materials, particularly those with memory effects like magnetic materials and some alloys. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
