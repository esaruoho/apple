Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The relationship between the magnetic field strength (H) and the resulting magnetization (M) is described by the hysteresis loop.

Elastic Materials: Some elastic materials can show hysteresis under stress. For example, rubber or certain polymers may exhibit a hysteresis loop when subjected to cyclic loading.

Thermal Systems: Certain thermal systems can also exhibit hysteresis, especially those involving phase transitions. For instance, the heat capacity of a material can change depending on whether it is heating up or cooling down.

Elements without Hysteresis

Many materials do not exhibit hysteresis. These include:

Linear Materials: Materials that follow linear relationships (like Ohm's law for resistors) do not exhibit hysteresis because their response is directly proportional to the input.

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain magnetic properties once the external magnetic field is removed.

Simple Mechanical Systems: Most simple mechanical systems, such as springs operating within their linear range, do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air and Hysteresis

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and follows ideal gas laws under normal conditions. However, certain properties of air, such as its density or specific heat, can change depending on the history of temperature and pressure changes, but this is not hysteresis in the strict sense. It's more about the thermodynamic state of the air rather than a memory effect related to past states.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those that can store and release energy in a way that depends on their past states. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
