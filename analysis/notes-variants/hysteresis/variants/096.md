What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states. Common examples of hysteresis include magnetic materials, rubber, and some types of springs.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis when they are exposed to changing magnetic fields. The magnetization of these materials depends on both the current magnetic field and the history of previous magnetic fields.
Rubber and Elastic Materials: These materials show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves. When deformed, they do not return to their original state immediately but retain some deformation, which is released gradually as the stress is removed.
Some Types of Springs: Certain types of springs can exhibit hysteresis, especially if they are made from materials that undergo significant plastic deformation.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: In linear systems, the response is directly proportional to the input, and there is no memory of past inputs. Examples include simple resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits.
Ideal Springs and Masses: Idealized models of springs and masses do not exhibit hysteresis because they return to their original state immediately after being deformed or displaced.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is an ideal gas under normal conditions and behaves according to the laws of thermodynamics without retaining any memory of past states. However, in certain complex systems involving air (such as in weather patterns or atmospheric dynamics), hysteresis might be observed due to the interactions between different components of the system, but this is not a property of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those that can undergo significant changes in shape or state that are not fully reversible. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
