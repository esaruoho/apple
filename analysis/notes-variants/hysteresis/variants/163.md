What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Elastic Materials: Some elastic materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, especially under cyclic loading.
Thermocouples and Thermistors: These temperature sensors can exhibit hysteresis due to the thermal history of the material.
Capacitors and Inductors: In some cases, these electrical components can show hysteresis, particularly in non-ideal conditions or when subjected to rapid changes in voltage/current.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Ferromagnetic Metals: Materials like copper, aluminum, and gold do not exhibit significant hysteresis because they do not retain a permanent magnetic moment.
Semiconductors: Under normal operating conditions, semiconductors do not show hysteresis in their electrical properties.
Pure Gases: Pure gases do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have internal structures or magnetic properties that could lead to such behavior.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past pressure or temperature changes in the way that materials with hysteresis do. However, air can exhibit some hysteretic behavior in specific contexts, such as in the case of moisture content in air, which can depend on the air's past humidity levels. But this is not a fundamental property of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or magnetic properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
