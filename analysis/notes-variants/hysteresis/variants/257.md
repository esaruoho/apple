What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is known as remanence.
Solenoids: The magnetic flux density in a solenoid depends on whether it was previously magnetized or demagnetized.
Rubber Bands: When stretched, rubber bands exhibit hysteresis due to their elastic properties.
Thermocouples: The voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history.
Hysteresis Loops: These are often seen in materials like ferromagnets, where the relationship between magnetic field strength and magnetic flux density forms a loop rather than a straight line.

Elements and Systems with Hysteresis:
Materials with Memory: Such as ferromagnetic materials, shape-memory alloys, and certain polymers.
Mechanical Systems: Like springs and dampers, which can show hysteresis under cyclic loading.
Electrical Systems: Certain types of inductors and capacitors can exhibit hysteresis under varying conditions.

Elements and Systems without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: These systems respond linearly to inputs, meaning the output is directly proportional to the input, and there is no memory of past states.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: In ideal electrical components, the current or voltage depends only on the current input and not on past inputs.
Simple Resistors: Resistors do not exhibit hysteresis because they follow Ohm's law, which is a linear relationship between voltage and current.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that follows the ideal gas law under normal conditions, meaning its pressure, volume, and temperature are directly related and do not depend on past states. However, in certain complex systems involving air (such as in weather patterns or in the behavior of air in confined spaces with changing boundary conditions), hysteresis might be observed, but this would be due to the complexity of the system rather than a fundamental property of air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, while air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
