Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: The magnetization of ferromagnetic materials (like iron) depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them. This is why a magnet can retain its magnetic properties even after the external field is removed.
Solenoids: When a solenoid is energized and de-energized repeatedly, the amount of magnetic flux it can store changes depending on the previous states.
Rubber Bands: When stretched and released, rubber bands exhibit hysteresis due to their elastic properties.
Thermocouples: In some cases, the voltage generated by a thermocouple can depend on the temperature history of the material.

Elements and Systems with Hysteresis:
Materials with Memory Effects: Many materials, especially those with magnetic or elastic properties, show hysteresis.
Biological Systems: Some biological processes can exhibit hysteresis, such as enzyme kinetics under varying conditions.
Economic Models: Certain economic models can exhibit hysteresis, particularly in unemployment rates and other macroeconomic indicators.

Elements and Systems without Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between inputs and outputs do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring follows Hooke's Law, which is a linear relationship, and does not exhibit hysteresis.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components in electrical circuits do not exhibit hysteresis because they follow linear relationships with respect to voltage and current.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that follows the ideal gas law under normal conditions, which is a linear relationship between pressure, volume, and temperature. However, when considering more complex phenomena involving air, such as the behavior of air in a compressor or turbine, hysteresis might be observed due to the non-linear effects of compression and expansion cycles.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of certain materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects or non-linear behaviors. Air, being a simple gas under normal conditions, does not exhibit hysteresis.
