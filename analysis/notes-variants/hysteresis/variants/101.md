What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on the path it took to get there, not just its current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can change shape based on temperature changes, and their behavior can show hysteresis.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties under stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most gases and fluids do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain memory of past states in the same way as solid materials do.
Pure metals and simple compounds generally do not show significant hysteresis unless they undergo phase transitions or have complex microstructures.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a gas, and gases do not retain memory of past states in the same way as solids or some other materials do. However, the behavior of air in certain systems (like in compressors or in weather patterns) might exhibit some form of hysteresis due to the complex interactions within those systems, but this is not inherent to the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials that can store and recall information about their past states, such as magnetic materials and shape memory alloys. Air, being a gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
