What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they can retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. This is why you can use a magnet to attract metal objects after it has been demagnetized.

Some Ferroelectric Materials: Similar to magnetic materials, some ferroelectric materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior.

Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and can "remember" their original shape when heated.

Some Electronic Components: Certain types of memory devices and some types of sensors can exhibit hysteresis in their responses to external stimuli.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-magnetic Metals: Most metals do not exhibit significant hysteresis unless they are specifically treated or are part of a composite material.
Non-Ferroelectric Materials: Materials that do not become polarized in an electric field typically do not show hysteresis in their electrical properties.
Simple Dielectrics: Pure dielectric materials (insulators) generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to complex mechanical or electrical stress.

Air and Hysteresis:

Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any "memory" of past conditions. However, the properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) can change in response to external factors, but these changes are not hysteresis. For example, if you heat air, its volume will expand, but this expansion is not dependent on the history of heating; it simply responds to the current temperature.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of specific materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or histories, such as magnetic and ferroelectric materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
