What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on their past magnetic field exposure.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.
Metals: Some metals, particularly those used in alloys, can exhibit hysteresis under certain conditions.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. Examples include most metals (like copper, aluminum), insulators, and gases.
Pure elements like gold, silver, and platinum generally do not show significant hysteresis unless they are part of a complex system or alloy.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a gas and does not retain a memory of past states in the same way that magnetic materials do. The properties of air (such as pressure, temperature, and density) change based on the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials with memory effects, such as certain magnetic materials, while most other materials and gases do not exhibit this property.
