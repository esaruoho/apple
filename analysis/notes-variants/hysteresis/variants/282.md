What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the input, often forming a loop or memory effect.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed, leading to a hysteresis loop in their magnetization curve.

Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions, although it is less common than in metals.

Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can exhibit shape memory effects, which can be considered a form of hysteresis.

Piezoelectric Materials: Some piezoelectric materials can show hysteresis in their electrical response when subjected to mechanical stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Non-Magnetic Metals: Pure metals like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not retain any magnetization after the external magnetic field is removed.

Semiconductors: Most semiconductors do not exhibit hysteresis unless they are part of a specific device configuration or under certain conditions that induce magnetic behavior.

Insulators: Pure insulators generally do not show hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce magnetic behavior.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is an inert gas and does not retain any memory of past states or conditions in the way that magnetic materials do. However, air can participate in processes that involve hysteresis, such as the hysteresis loops seen in the magnetization of ferromagnetic materials when they are exposed to varying magnetic fields.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials with magnetic properties, certain ceramics, and shape memory alloys, while non-magnetic metals, semiconductors, and pure insulators typically do not exhibit hysteresis. Air, being a simple gas, does not have hysteresis.
