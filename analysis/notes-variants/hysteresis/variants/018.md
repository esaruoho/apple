What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Some Ceramics: Certain ceramic materials can also show hysteresis, especially those used in piezoelectric applications.
Rubber and Polymers: Some rubber and polymer materials can exhibit mechanical hysteresis, meaning they store energy during deformation and release it gradually as they return to their original shape.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, copper, aluminum, and most plastics do not show significant hysteresis.
Pure gases like air do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions because they do not retain any memory of past states.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past states. When you compress air, it will expand to its original volume when the pressure is released, without retaining any "memory" of the compression process.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or magnetic properties. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
