What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it can retain some magnetization even after the external field is removed. This is known as remanence and is a classic example of hysteresis.
Solenoids and Electromagnets: The magnetic flux density in a solenoid changes with the current, but the relationship is hysteresis due to the material properties.
Rubber Bands: When stretched, rubber bands exhibit hysteresis because the force required to stretch them further increases with each cycle of stretching and relaxing.
Thermocouples: In some cases, thermocouples can show hysteresis, particularly if they are subjected to rapid temperature changes.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Elements: Pure elements like metals do not typically exhibit hysteresis unless they are part of a more complex system or undergo phase transitions that involve hysteresis.
Simple Mechanical Systems: Basic mechanical systems such as springs or dampers may not show significant hysteresis under normal operating conditions.

Examples of Non-Hysteresis:
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between inputs and outputs do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, a simple resistor in an electrical circuit follows Ohm's law without memory of past conditions.
Ideal Springs: An ideal spring behaves linearly and does not retain any state from previous deformations.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis. It is a simple gas that follows the ideal gas law under most conditions, which is a linear relationship between pressure, volume, and temperature. However, in specific contexts involving phase changes (like condensation and evaporation), air can exhibit behavior that might be loosely described as having some form of hysteresis, but this is more related to the phase change process rather than the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a property seen in certain materials and systems, particularly those that involve memory of past states. Elements and simple systems generally do not exhibit hysteresis, while complex systems like magnetic materials and certain mechanical components can show this effect.
