What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state or response lags behind the changes in the input. Common examples include magnetic materials, some types of rubber, and certain mechanical systems.

Elements with Hysteresis:

Magnetic Materials: These materials exhibit hysteresis when they are exposed to a changing magnetic field. The magnetization (or magnetic moment per unit volume) of these materials depends on both the current magnetic field and the history of previous fields.
Rubber and Some Polymers: These materials can show hysteresis in their stress-strain curves, meaning the strain does not return to its original value when the stress is removed.
Some Mechanical Systems: Certain mechanical systems, like springs with friction or dampers, can exhibit hysteresis in their force-displacement characteristics.

Elements without Hysteresis:

Linear Systems: These are systems where the output is directly proportional to the input, and there is no memory of past inputs. Examples include simple resistors, capacitors, and inductors in electrical circuits.
Ideal Springs: In an ideal spring, the force is directly proportional to the displacement, and there is no hysteresis.
Ideal Capacitors and Inductors: These components do not store energy in a way that depends on past inputs, so they do not exhibit hysteresis.

Air:

Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past pressure or temperature changes in the same way as some materials do. However, certain properties of air, such as its density, can change in response to pressure and temperature, but this change is typically linear and does not exhibit hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with internal states that depend on past inputs. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
