What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even after those conditions have changed. Materials with hysteresis typically exhibit behaviors such as magnetization, elasticity, and certain types of phase transitions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it becomes magnetized. If the magnetic field is then removed, the material may retain some magnetization, depending on the strength and direction of the field. This is known as the hysteresis loop.
Elastic Materials: In some elastic materials, the stress-strain relationship can be hysteric. For example, if you stretch a rubber band and then release it, it might not return exactly to its original length due to internal stresses and deformations.
Phase Transitions: Some materials exhibit hysteresis during phase transitions, such as the transition between liquid and solid states. The temperature at which the phase change occurs can depend on whether the material is cooling or heating.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Simple elements like gases, liquids, and pure metals generally do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have complex internal structures or memory effects. However, when these elements form more complex systems (like alloys or composite materials), hysteresis can appear.

Things Without Hysteresis:
Pure elements and simple substances (like pure water or pure metals).
Ideal gases (though real gases can show some hysteresis under certain conditions).

Air:
Air, being a mixture of gases, does not exhibit hysteresis in the same way as materials with complex internal structures. The properties of air (such as density, pressure, and temperature) are primarily determined by the current conditions and do not "remember" past conditions. Therefore, air does not have hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is a property of certain materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or memory effects. Simple elements and gases like air do not exhibit hysteresis.
