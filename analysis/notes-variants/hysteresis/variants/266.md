Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly when they undergo repeated cycles of magnetization and demagnetization. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Elastic Materials: Some elastic materials can show hysteresis under cyclic loading, such as rubber or certain types of plastics.
Thermal Systems: Certain thermal systems can exhibit hysteresis, especially those involving phase transitions like melting and freezing.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Materials: Materials that do not have magnetic properties typically do not exhibit hysteresis.
Linear Systems: Systems that follow linear relationships between input and output do not show hysteresis. For example, resistors in electrical circuits behave linearly and do not exhibit hysteresis.

Does Air Have Hysteresis?
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis because it is a simple gas and follows ideal gas laws under normal conditions. The behavior of air is generally linear and does not depend on its previous states in the same way that magnetic materials do. However, if you consider more complex systems involving air (such as air conditioning systems or weather patterns), these can sometimes exhibit hysteresis due to the memory effects of the system as a whole.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in specific materials and systems, particularly those with memory effects like magnetic materials and some elastic materials. Air, being a simple gas, does not inherently exhibit hysteresis.
