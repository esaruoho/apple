What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many magnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. When these materials are exposed to a magnetic field, they retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The relationship between the magnetic field strength and the resulting magnetization is described by the hysteresis loop.
Some Ferroelectric Materials: These materials can also show hysteresis in their polarization behavior when subjected to an electric field.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and polymers can exhibit hysteresis in their mechanical properties under stress.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Non-magnetic Metals: Elements like copper, aluminum, and gold do not show significant hysteresis because they do not become magnetized in response to external magnetic fields.
Insulators: Pure insulating materials generally do not exhibit hysteresis unless they are subjected to specific conditions that induce a magnetic or ferroelectric response.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a non-magnetic, non-ferroelectric, and non-ferroelastic material under normal conditions. Air does not retain any memory of past magnetic or electric fields. However, air can be affected by changes in temperature and pressure, which can lead to changes in its density and other physical properties, but this is not hysteresis.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in materials that can be magnetized or polarized, while air and most other simple elements do not exhibit this property.
