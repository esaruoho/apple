What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials or systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state depends on the path taken to reach that state, rather than just the current conditions.

Examples of Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: When a magnetic material (like iron) is exposed to a magnetic field, it will retain some magnetization even after the field is removed. The amount of magnetization that remains is called remanence, and this can be described by a hysteresis loop.
Rubber and Springs: In mechanical systems, rubber and certain types of springs exhibit hysteresis due to their viscoelastic properties. The energy required to stretch or compress them depends on the history of deformation.
Thermocouples: Some thermocouples show hysteresis in their temperature response, meaning they may give different readings depending on whether the temperature is increasing or decreasing.
Plastic Deformation: In materials science, plastic deformation can lead to hysteresis in stress-strain curves.

Elements and Simple Systems:
Elements and simple systems typically do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not have complex internal structures or memory effects. For example, a pure element like copper or a simple gas like nitrogen does not show hysteresis under normal conditions.

Air:
Air itself does not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. It is a simple gas with no internal structure that would cause such behavior. However, in certain complex systems involving air, such as in weather patterns or atmospheric circulation models, there might be emergent behaviors that could be described as having some form of hysteresis, but this is not inherent to the air itself.

In summary, hysteresis is a property seen in more complex materials and systems, particularly those with internal memory or viscoelastic properties. Simple elements and gases like air do not exhibit hysteresis under normal circumstances.
