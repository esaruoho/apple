What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state will "remember" past conditions even when those conditions no longer exist.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt. The magnetization of these materials depends on the history of magnetic fields applied to them.
Shape Memory Alloys: Materials like nitinol (nickel titanium alloy) can undergo shape changes based on temperature, and they exhibit hysteresis in their response to heating and cooling cycles.
Piezoelectric Materials: These materials can change their electrical properties in response to mechanical stress, and this change can be hysteresis-dependent.
Some Polymers: Certain polymers can show hysteresis in their mechanical properties under cyclic loading.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis in their magnetic properties.
Pure gases, like air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not retain any memory of past states under normal conditions.

Air and Hysteresis:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain any memory of past conditions. When you compress air, it heats up, and when you release it, it cools down, but there is no persistent effect that depends on the history of compression and expansion cycles.

In summary, hysteresis is a characteristic of specific materials and systems, particularly those with complex internal structures or memory effects. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
