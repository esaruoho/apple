What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time depends on its past states as well.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly when they undergo repeated magnetization cycles. The most common example is iron, which shows a magnetic hysteresis loop.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis under specific conditions.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys can change shape in response to temperature changes and can exhibit hysteresis in their phase transformations.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most non-magnetic materials do not exhibit hysteresis. For example, metals like copper or aluminum, which are not ferromagnetic, do not show hysteresis.
Pure gases, such as air, do not exhibit hysteresis because they do not store memory of past states in the same way as magnetic materials do.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis. It is a simple gas and does not retain a "memory" of past states in the way that magnetic materials do. However, properties related to air, such as pressure and temperature, can be described using thermodynamic processes, but these do not exhibit hysteresis unless they are part of a more complex system (like a refrigeration cycle).

In summary, hysteresis is primarily associated with materials that can be magnetized, such as ferromagnetic metals, and certain types of ceramics and polymers. Air, being a simple gas, does not exhibit hysteresis.
