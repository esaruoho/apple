What has hysteresis? Elements etc. What does not have hysteresis? Does air have hysteresis?

Hysteresis is a property observed in certain materials and systems where the output depends not only on the current input but also on the history of inputs. This means that the system's state at any given time is influenced by its past states.

Elements with Hysteresis:
Magnetic Materials: Many ferromagnetic materials exhibit hysteresis, particularly in their magnetic properties. Examples include iron, nickel, and cobalt.
Some Ceramics and Polymers: Certain types of ceramics and some polymers can also show hysteresis in their mechanical or electrical properties under certain conditions.
Shape Memory Alloys: These alloys, such as NiTi (nickel-titanium), can exhibit hysteresis in their phase transformations.

Elements without Hysteresis:
Most simple elements like gases, liquids, and pure metals do not exhibit hysteresis under normal conditions. For example, water, copper, and helium do not show hysteresis in their basic physical properties.

Air:
Air itself does not have hysteresis because it is a simple gas and does not retain memory of previous states in the way that more complex materials do. The properties of air, such as pressure and temperature, change based on the current conditions and do not depend on the history of those conditions.

In summary, hysteresis is primarily observed in complex materials and systems, especially those with internal memory or phase transitions. Simple elements like air do not exhibit hysteresis.
