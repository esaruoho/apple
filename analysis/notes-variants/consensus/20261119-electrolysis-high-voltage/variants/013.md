## Answer

**Conventional electrolysis is current-driven (Faradaic).** Faraday's laws dictate that the moles of substance produced are proportional to total charge passed (coulombs). In practice, you apply a voltage sufficient to overcome the thermodynamic decomposition voltage (~1.23 V for water) plus overpotentials at the electrodes, and current flows — the current *is* the reaction rate. This is "brute force" in the sense that you push electrons through the electrolyte, and efficiency losses appear as heat (I²R) and overpotential waste.

**High-voltage electrolysis is a distinct, claimed paradigm** — not mainstream accepted, but with a patent lineage and experimental literature. The core claim: water acts as the dielectric of a capacitor; a high-voltage field across it (kilovolts to tens of kilovolts) stresses the molecular bonds directly, releasing hydrogen/oxygen with *minimal sustained current*. Key figures and patents:

- **Tesla, US 568,177 (1896)** — impulse DC ozone generator; high-voltage, low-current pulses. The electrical ancestor.
- **Stephen Horvath, US 3,980,053 (1976)** — first water-electrolysis patent explicitly using pulsed high-voltage DC.
- **Stanley Meyer, US 4,936,961 (1990) / US 5,149,407 (1992)** — "Water Fuel Cell": resonant charging choke circuit, water as dielectric in a capacitor, voltage intensifier circuit, current restricted to leakage levels. Meyer's own words: "voltage, not amperage, does the work" [Meyer-162]; "current is maintained constant at a very low level" [Meyer-33]; "inherent current limiting voltage source" [Meyer-39].
- **Andrija Puharich, US 4,394,230 (1983)** — resonant water splitting at specific frequencies.
- **Xogen / Paul Pendergrass / Warren Herwig / Charles Frazer / Stephen Chambers** — capacitive discharge / pulsed DC variants.

**Circuit topology commonalities**: high-Q resonant LC tank (Q-multiplication) driven at low voltage → kilovolt peaks across the water cell → ultrafast diode voltage doubler / rectifier captures the multiplied voltage as DC pulses across the cell → low duty cycle, low average current. The water cell *is* the capacitor in the resonant circuit (or in parallel with it). Voltage across the cell = Q × drive voltage.

**What distinguishes it from conventional**: In Faraday electrolysis, current *carries* the reducing/oxidizing equivalents. In the high-voltage claim, the electric *field* does the bond-breaking; current is a parasitic loss to be minimized. The "voltage does the work" assertion [Meyer-162] means the energy per molecule comes from the potential gradient, not from electron throughput.

**Status**: Conventional electrolysis is industrial standard (alkaline, PEM, SOEC). High-voltage pulsed / resonant approaches exist in patents and bench reports but lack independent, calibrated, long-duration efficiency validation (calorimetry + gas chromatography + full input metering) that would settle the COP question. The skeptical EE test: meter *all* input ports (drive, bias, pump, control) and output (gas enthalpy + heat) over hours; single clamp meters miss phase, harmonics, RF, common-mode. The step where this class of claim usually dies: sustained closed-loop calorimetry with all ports metered.
