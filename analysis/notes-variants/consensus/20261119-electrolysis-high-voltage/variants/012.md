## Answer

**Conventional electrolysis is current-driven (Faradaic).** Faraday's laws dictate that the moles of H₂ and O₂ produced are directly proportional to the total charge (coulombs) passed through the cell. In practice this means DC current at 1.8–2.4 V per cell (thermodynamic minimum 1.23 V plus overpotentials), with efficiencies of 60–80% HHV for modern alkaline/PEM electrolyzers. The "brute force" characterization fits: you push amps, you get gas linearly; energy cost is dominated by I²R losses and overvoltage.

**High-voltage / low-average-current electrolysis exists as a distinct patented and demonstrated approach.** The lineage runs:
- **Tesla (US 568,177, 1896)** — impulse DC / disruptive discharge for ozone; the electrical ancestor.
- **Horvath (US 3,980,053, 1974)** — first water-electrolysis patent explicitly using high-voltage pulsed DC.
- **Puharich (US 4,394,230, 1983)** — high-frequency AC / resonant dielectric breakdown.
- **Meyer (US 4,613,779, 1986; US 4,936,961, 1990)** — "Water Fuel Cell": resonant charging-choke circuit, water as dielectric of a capacitor, voltage intensified to 1–5 kV+ while average current is held to milliamps. Meyer's own statements (verified ledger): *"the voltage is increased and decreased but the current is maintained constant at a very low level"* [Meyer-33]; *"as you raise the voltage higher… more gas was being generated, not on a linear effect as to the electrolysis process"* [Meyer-162]; *"inherent current limiting voltage source"* [Meyer-39].
- **Xogen / Pendergrass / Herwig / Frazer / Chambers** — capacitive-discharge / pulsed variants.

**Mechanism difference:** In Faradaic electrolysis, current *is* the reaction carrier (electron transfer at electrodes). In the high-voltage pulsed/resonant paradigm, the cell is operated as a **voltage-driven capacitive load** — the water gap sees high dV/dt impulses; the claim is that the intense electric field (and/or field-emission / avalanche / plasma effects at the electrode interface) drives dissociation with far less net charge throughput. Whether this yields net energy gain (over-unity) is a separate, contested question; the *phenomenon* of high-voltage gas evolution at low average current is documented in the patent record and bench reports.

**Key practical markers:** nanosecond-to-microsecond rise-time pulses; low duty cycle (1–10%); resonant LC tank (charging choke + cell capacitance) to achieve Q-multiplied voltage; ultrafast recovery diodes (UF5408, etc.) to prevent ring-down loading the spike; stainless or platinized electrodes with narrow gap (1–3 mm); gas measurement by calibrated flow meter or gas chromatography, not just bubble observation.
