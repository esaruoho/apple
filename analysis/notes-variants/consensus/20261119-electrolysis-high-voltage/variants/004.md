## Answer

**Conventional electrolysis is current-driven (Faraday regime).** The amount of hydrogen produced is proportional to the total charge passed (coulombs = amperes × seconds). You apply a DC voltage high enough to overcome the decomposition voltage (~1.23 V thermodynamic + overpotentials), and the current that flows — limited by solution resistance, electrode kinetics, and bubble coverage — determines the gas yield. This is what Meyer calls "brute force": you push amps through the cell, most energy becomes heat (I²R losses and overpotential losses), and efficiency tops out around 70–80 % electrical-to-chemical in good alkaline cells.

**High-voltage / pulsed / resonant electrolysis is a different claimed regime.** The lineage in the archive runs: Tesla's impulse DC ozone apparatus (US 568,177, 1896) → Stephen Horvath's pulsed electrolysis cell (US 3,980,053, 1974) → Stanley Meyer's Water Fuel Cell (US 4,613,779, 1986; US 4,798,661, 1989; US 4,826,581, 1989) → Andrija Puharich, Xogen, and others. The common claim: **short, high-voltage pulses (kV–tens of kV) at low duty cycle** create a strong electric field across the water gap (treated as a dielectric capacitor) that polarises and stresses the H₂O molecules, releasing hydrogen with **minimal average current draw**. Meyer's verified statements:

- "the voltage is increased and decreased but the current is maintained constant at a very low level" [Meyer-33]
- "as you raise the voltage higher, then there were higher, more gas was being generated, not on a linear effect as to the electrolysis process" [Meyer-162]
- "hence the arrangement is an inherent current limiting voltage source" [Meyer-39]

His circuit uses a **resonant charging choke** (bifilar coil) that steps up voltage via Q-multiplication while the water cell's capacitance completes the tank; at resonance the current falls toward leakage while voltage rises. The claim is that the **voltage field itself** — not the current — does the bond-breaking, so gas production scales with peak voltage, not with coulombs passed.

**Other high-voltage approaches:**
- **Puharich**: tuned the cell to a mechanical/acoustic resonance of the water lattice (≈ 600 Hz + harmonics) with high-voltage pulses.
- **Horvath**: unipolar high-voltage pulses (µs width, kV amplitude) across a narrow gap; claimed Faradaic efficiency > 100 %.
- **Xogen / capacitive discharge**: dump a charged capacitor through the water gap; the fast dV/dt creates non-Faradaic field effects.
- **Plasma electrolysis / glow discharge** (Koldomasov, Mizuno, etc.): a plasma sheath forms at the electrode; the high-field region drives non-thermal dissociation.

**Mainstream view:** conventional electrochemistry treats any gas evolution as Faradaic (charge-coupled). Reported "excess" gas in high-voltage cells is usually attributed to measurement error (gas entrainment, temperature/pressure miscalculation, unmetered current paths, RF pickup on meters). No peer-reviewed replication has closed the energy balance with calorimetry + gas chromatography + full electrical metering (including HF/RF content) showing > 100 % Faradaic efficiency.

**What would settle it for a given device:** simultaneous measurement of (a) real input energy (calorimetry or four-quadrant power analyzer with bandwidth > 10× pulse repetition rate, all ports metered), (b) gas volume + composition (GC), (c) cell temperature, (d) voltage/current waveforms at the cell terminals with calibrated HV probe and current-viewing resistor. Run until steady state (1+ hour). Compare joules in vs. HHV of gas out. That is the single test the field rarely publishes.
