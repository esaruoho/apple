## Answer

**Conventional electrolysis is current-driven (Faradaic).** Faraday's laws dictate that the moles of hydrogen produced are directly proportional to the total charge passed (coulombs = amperes × seconds). The thermodynamic minimum cell voltage is ~1.23 V at 25 °C; practical cells run 1.8–2.2 V with overpotentials. You "brute-force" it by pushing current through the electrolyte — more current = more gas, linearly. Efficiency is limited by ohmic losses (I²R heating) and electrode overpotentials.

**High-voltage / pulsed DC electrolysis is a documented, distinct paradigm.** It uses short, high-voltage pulses (often kV–tens of kV) at low duty cycle so that *average* current and power remain small, while *peak* voltage stresses the water dielectric. Key historical/experimental lines:

**Mechanism differences:**
- **Conventional**: Continuous DC, electrode reactions limited by charge transfer kinetics and mass transport; gas evolves at electrodes.
- **High-voltage pulsed**: Nanosecond–microsecond pulses exceed water's dielectric strength locally, creating non-equilibrium plasma channels, shock waves, or field-emission effects at electrode tips. The *average* Faradaic current can be far below the gas yield predicted by Faraday — proponents argue for field-driven dissociation, electron impact dissociation in micro-plasmas, or coherent vibrational excitation. Critics attribute apparent excess to measurement artifacts (unmetered RF, capacitive displacement current, gas recombination, calorimetric errors).

**What would settle it for a given device:** Simultaneous, calibrated measurement of (a) real input energy (all ports: drive, bias, pump, control — integrated joules), (b) output gas volume/composition (GC or mass flow), (c) calorimetry of cell and exhaust, (d) high-bandwidth voltage/current waveforms at the cell terminals (to separate displacement from conduction current). Run until steady state; compare joules-in vs. HHV of gas out plus measured heat. That single integrated energy balance is the falsification test.
