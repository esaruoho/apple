## Answer

**Conventional electrolysis is current-driven (Faraday's laws).** The amount of substance liberated at each electrode is proportional to the total charge passed (coulombs). In practice this means you push amperes through the cell at a voltage just above the thermodynamic decomposition voltage (~1.23 V for water at STP) plus overpotentials. The energy cost is dominated by I²R losses in the electrolyte and electrodes, and by the overpotentials themselves. This is what the question calls "bruteforce" — you pay for every coulomb.

**High-voltage / pulsed-DC / resonant electrolysis is a different paradigm.** The claim — made by Horvath (US 3,980,053, 1974), Puharich, Meyer, Xogen, and others — is that very short, high-voltage pulses (often kilovolts, nanosecond to microsecond rise times, low duty cycle) can split water with far less *average* current and therefore less average power than Faraday would predict for the same gas volume. The proposed mechanisms vary:
- **Dielectric breakdown of the water layer** at the electrode surface, creating transient plasma channels that dissociate molecules non-thermally.
- **Resonant charging-choke circuits** (Meyer) where the water cell acts as a capacitor in a high-Q LC tank; voltage is multiplied by Q while current stays near leakage.
- **Impulse / radiant energy capture** (Bedini, Tesla lineage) where the sharp transient spike — "voltage without current" — does the work before the conduction current catches up.
- **Non-Faradaic charge injection** where the double layer is charged/discharged faster than ions can migrate, so the energy goes into bond breaking rather than heating.

**What the literature shows:**
- Horvath's 1974 patent (US 3,980,053) explicitly uses high-voltage pulses (1–50 kV, 0.1–10 µs) at low duty cycle and claims gas production exceeding Faraday predictions.
- Meyer's patents (US 4,613,779; US 4,798,661; US 4,826,581) describe a "voltage intensifier circuit" — a resonant charging choke with the water cell as the dielectric — and state "the voltage is increased and decreased but the current is maintained constant at a very low level" [Meyer-33].
- Puharich (US 4,394,230) used a tuned circuit at the water's dielectric relaxation frequency (~2.45 GHz in later work, lower in earlier) to achieve high voltage across the cell with minimal current.
- Xogen's capacitive-discharge patents (US 6,126,794 etc.) use fast MOSFET switching to dump capacitor energy into the cell in sub-microsecond pulses.

**No peer-reviewed, independently replicated study has confirmed >100% Faradaic efficiency under controlled calorimetry.** The claims persist in the open-source / independent researcher community. The critical measurement is **simultaneous, time-resolved electrical input (all ports: drive, bias, pump) and gas output (volume, composition, temperature) over a full thermal cycle**, with calorimetry to catch any unmetered energy paths. Until that dataset exists for a specific device, the question remains open but unproven.
