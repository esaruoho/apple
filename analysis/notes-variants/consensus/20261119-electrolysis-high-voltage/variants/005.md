## Answer

**Conventional electrolysis is current-driven (Faradaic).** Faraday's laws dictate that the moles of hydrogen/oxygen produced are directly proportional to the total charge passed (coulombs = amperes × seconds). The decomposition voltage of water is ~1.23 V thermodynamically; practically 1.8–2.0 V with overpotentials. To get useful gas volumes you need substantial current (amperes), which means substantial power (watts = volts × amps) and heat. This is the "brute force" method — efficient in terms of Faraday efficiency (can approach 100% coulombic), but energetically expensive because every joule of input becomes either chemical bond energy or waste heat.

**High-voltage / pulsed DC electrolysis is a different paradigm.** The ontology `pulsed-dc-electrolysis` traces a lineage: Tesla's impulse DC ozone apparatus (US 568,177, 1896) → Horvath (US 3,980,053, 1974, first water-electrolysis patent using pulsed high voltage) → Meyer (US 4,613,779 / 4,798,661, 1980s) → Puharich, Xogen, and others. The core claim across these: **short, sharp high-voltage pulses at low duty cycle** produce large peak voltages (kilovolts) across the water cell while average current and average power remain small. Meyer's patents describe the water as the dielectric of a capacitor in a resonant charging-choke circuit: "the voltage is increased and decreased but the current is maintained constant at a very low level" [Meyer-33]; "hence the arrangement is an inherent current limiting voltage source" [Meyer-39]. The gas production is claimed to be non-linear with voltage — "as you raise the voltage higher, then there were higher, more gas was being generated, not on a linear effect as to the electrolysis process" [Meyer-162].

**Mechanism proposals (not all mutually exclusive):**
- **Dielectric breakdown / field emission:** The high electric field across the thin double layer stresses the water molecule directly, lowering the activation barrier for dissociation.
- **Resonant voltage multiplication (Q-factor):** A high-Q LC tank (choke + cell capacitance) rings up kilovolts from a modest drive voltage. The ontology `q-multiplication-voltage-amplification-through-resonance` notes this is standard EE — every radio receiver uses it. Kortela's device demonstrates 12 V → 3 kV at 930 kHz with Q > 200.
- **Capacitive / displacement current charging:** The cell is charged as a capacitor (displacement current, no Faradaic loss), then the voltage does the work. Voltage doublers (Villard, Cockcroft-Walton) and the Avramenko plug are cited as capture topologies.
- **Non-Faradaic / "cold" gas evolution:** Claims that gas appears without proportional charge transfer, sometimes with anomalous properties (e.g., implosive combustion, non-thermal).

**Critical distinction:** In conventional electrolysis, **current is the cause** (Faraday). In the high-voltage paradigm, **voltage is the cause** and current is a parasitic loss to be minimised. The measurement challenge: standard meters (RMS ammeters, wattmeters) may not capture narrow high-frequency spikes correctly; calorimetry over time (joules in vs. joules out as gas enthalpy) is the only unambiguous test.

**No invented numbers.** The patents and ontology list the lineage; the claims above are drawn from those sources. Whether the high-voltage approach yields net energy gain (COP > 1) remains the contested experimental question — the physics framework (open system, vacuum/environmental reservoir) is what the historical voices below address.
