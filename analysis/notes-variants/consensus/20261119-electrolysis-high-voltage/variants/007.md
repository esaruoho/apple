## Answer

**Conventional (Faradaic) electrolysis** is current-driven: Faraday's laws dictate that the moles of H₂ and O₂ produced are proportional to the total charge passed (Q = I·t). You apply a DC voltage above the thermodynamic decomposition voltage (~1.23 V at STP) plus overpotentials, and current flows through the electrolyte. The energy input is V·I·t; efficiency is limited by ohmic losses, activation overpotentials at the electrodes, and concentration polarization. This is what the question calls "brute force" — you push amperes through the cell.

**High-voltage / pulsed / resonant electrolysis** represents a different paradigm. The water cell is treated as a **capacitive dielectric** (water's εᵣ ≈ 80) in a resonant charging-choke circuit. Key claims from the lineage:

- **Tesla (1896, US 568,177)**: Impulse DC / disruptive discharge — sharp, unidirectional voltage spikes with minimal current.
- **Horvath (1974, US 3,980,053)**: First water-electrolysis patent using high-voltage pulsed DC.
- **Meyer (1980s, e.g., US 4,613,779; EP 0 122 472)**: "Voltage, not amperage, does the work" [Meyer-162]. The cell is a **voltage intensifier** — a resonant charging-choke circuit where "at resonance the current falls toward leakage while voltage rises" [Meyer-33, Meyer-39]. Gas production "exceeds 100% although quantal energy comes down against time" [Seike-44, in the same voltage-not-current vein]. Meyer states: "the hydrogen is released, not generated" — the energy comes from the water's own binding energy triggered by the field, not from the input current.
- **Puharich**: High-frequency AC (MHz range) to match water's molecular resonances.
- **Xogen / capacitive discharge**: Short, high-voltage pulses into the cell's capacitance.
- **Don Smith**: Ambient background voltage (hundreds of kV) tapped via resonance; "all useful electricity over-unity between ambient and ground" [DonSmith-209 context]; wattless energy transfer to decoupled secondaries.

**Mechanism difference**: In the high-voltage paradigm, the input power supply charges a resonant tank (inductor + cell capacitance) at high Q. The voltage across the cell is Q-multiplied (V_cell ≈ Q × V_drive). The sharp voltage gradient across the double layer / dielectric is posited to cleave H-O bonds **field-driven** rather than **current-driven**. Current is kept near leakage levels; the "work" is done by the electric field across the water dielectric, not by Faradaic charge transfer. Whether this yields net energy gain (COP > 1) is the contested claim; the **engineering distinction** — voltage-driven vs. current-driven — is real and documented in the patents.
