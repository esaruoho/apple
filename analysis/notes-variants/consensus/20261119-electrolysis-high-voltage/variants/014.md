## Answer

**Conventional electrolysis is current-driven (Faradaic).** Faraday's laws dictate that the moles of hydrogen produced are proportional to the total charge passed (coulombs = amperes × seconds). In practice this means you push substantial DC current (amperes) through an electrolyte; the voltage needed is modest (1.23 V thermodynamic minimum, typically 1.8–2.5 V practical with overpotentials), and the energy cost is dominated by I²R losses in the solution and electrodes. This is what the question calls "bruteforce" — you pay for every electron that crosses the cell.

**High-voltage / pulsed-voltage electrolysis is a different paradigm.** The claim — made by Horvath (US 3,980,053, 1974), Meyer (US 4,613,779, 1986; US 4,798,661, 1989), Puharich, Xogen, and others — is that water can be treated as the dielectric of a capacitor in a resonant charging-choke circuit. The drive is high voltage (kilovolts) at low average current; the cell is driven at its electrical resonance so that Q-multiplication raises the voltage across the water gap while the source current falls toward leakage. Meyer's patent language states: "the voltage is increased and decreased but the current is maintained constant at a very low level" (Meyer-33) and "as you raise the voltage higher, then there were higher, more gas was being generated, not on a linear effect as to the electrolysis process" (Meyer-162). Horvath's 1974 patent uses short, sharp high-voltage pulses at low duty cycle. Tesla's 1896 ozone apparatus (US 568,177) is the electrical ancestor: impulse DC, not continuous current.

**Key circuit elements in the high-voltage approach:**
- Resonant LC tank (charging choke + cell capacitance) → Q-multiplication of voltage
- Ultrafast recovery diodes (UF5408, 75 ns) in voltage-doubler / Cockcroft-Walton topology to capture the multiplied voltage as DC pulses
- Water gap as a voltage-dependent dielectric; breakdown / field-emission / non-Faradaic dissociation mechanisms proposed
- Measurement claim: gas volume exceeds Faraday prediction for the measured average input power (COP > 1 claimed)

**Mainstream status:** Standard electrochemistry treats high-voltage breakdown in water as parasitic (corrosion, heating, ozone). No peer-reviewed replication has confirmed Faradaic efficiency > 100% under controlled calorimetry. The burden of proof remains: simultaneous measurement of real input power (all ports, including drive/bias/standby), gas volume/composition, and cell temperature over hour-scale runs.
