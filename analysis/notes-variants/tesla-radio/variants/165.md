Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and disagreement with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others, including Heinrich Hertz. Tesla's views on the nature of wireless communication were quite different from those of his contemporaries.

Tesla believed that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that did not rely solely on electromagnetic waves propagating through space. He argued that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth's ionosphere or through the ground. This concept is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonant antenna systems."

In contrast, the electromagnetic radiation theory proposed by Hertz suggested that radio waves could propagate through free space without needing a physical medium. While this theory laid the groundwork for modern radio technology, Tesla felt it did not fully capture the essence of what he believed was happening in wireless communication.

Tesla's claims and theories were not widely accepted during his lifetime, and much of the early development of radio technology was based on the electromagnetic radiation theory. However, Tesla's ideas have since gained more recognition, especially in the context of alternative methods of wireless communication and in certain fringe scientific communities.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories about earth resonance and wireless transmission have been studied and tested, they do not replace the established principles of electromagnetic radiation. Modern radio technology still relies heavily on the principles discovered by Hertz and further developed by many other scientists and engineers.
