Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's views on the nature of wireless communication were quite different from those of his contemporaries who were developing the field based on Heinrich Hertz's discoveries.

Tesla believed that radio waves were not the best way to transmit information over long distances. He argued that the ionosphere could be used as a natural conductor for high-frequency currents, which would then travel along the Earth's surface without the need for a line-of-sight transmission. This concept is now known as ground wave propagation, and it forms the basis for much of modern radio communication.

In contrast, the electromagnetic radiation theory proposed by Hertz suggested that radio waves could propagate through free space, which was a significant breakthrough but did not align with Tesla's vision of wireless communication. Tesla's approach emphasized the use of resonant circuits and the Earth itself as a medium for transmitting signals, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla's claims were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, as the technology of his day was still developing. However, his ideas laid the groundwork for many of the principles used in modern radio and telecommunications. Today, we recognize both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions to the field of wireless communication, although their methods and theories differ significantly.
