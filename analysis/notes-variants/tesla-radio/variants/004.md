Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others, including Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that his own work on wireless transmission was fundamentally different from what was being described by the electromagnetic radiation theory.

Tesla's concerns stemmed from several aspects:

Interference and Noise: Tesla believed that the wireless signals he transmitted were not subject to the same interference and noise that affected the electromagnetic waves described by Hertz. He thought that his signals could travel through the Earth itself, which would provide a more stable and less noisy medium for communication.

Range and Power: Tesla argued that his system did not require the high power levels or large antennas that were necessary for the transmission of electromagnetic waves according to the then-prevailing theories. He claimed that his methods could achieve long-range communication with minimal power.

Signal Quality: Tesla maintained that his signals were clearer and more reliable than those described by the electromagnetic radiation theory. He believed that his methods could transmit information without the distortion and loss of signal quality that was observed with electromagnetic waves.

Practical Applications: Tesla saw practical applications for his wireless technology that went beyond simple communication. He envisioned a global network of wireless power transmission, which would be impossible with the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood at the time.

Despite these criticisms, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were significant. His experiments and patents laid the groundwork for modern radio and wireless technology. However, his ideas about the nature of wireless transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime and only gained recognition much later.

It's important to note that while Tesla's views were innovative and sometimes controversial, they also contributed to the ongoing development of wireless technology. The debate between Tesla's approach and the electromagnetic radiation theory continued well into the 20th century and influenced the evolution of radio and telecommunications.
