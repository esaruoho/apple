Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it.

Tesla's views on this topic can be summarized as follows:

Wireless Transmission vs. Radio Waves: Tesla argued that the term "radio" was misleading because it implied that the signals were transmitted through the air as waves, similar to those described by Hertz. Tesla believed that his system of wireless transmission involved a different mechanism, one that did not rely solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space.

Coherers and Wireless Signals: Tesla emphasized the role of coherers (a type of detector used in early radio receivers) in his experiments. He suggested that these devices could detect signals that were not simply waves but rather a form of energy that could be transmitted without the need for a physical medium like air or water.

Earth as a Conductor: Tesla proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for wireless signals. He believed that signals could be transmitted through the Earth's conductive properties, rather than through the air as waves. This idea is often referred to as "earth currents."

Controversy with Marconi: There was significant controversy between Tesla and Guglielmo Marconi, another pioneer in wireless communication. Marconi's system relied more heavily on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air, while Tesla's system emphasized the use of the Earth as a conductor.

Claims and Patents: Tesla filed numerous patents related to his ideas about wireless transmission, including systems that did not require the transmission of waves through the air. However, his claims were often met with skepticism and legal challenges from other inventors and patent holders.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted at the time. The widespread adoption of radio technology eventually came to rely more on the principles of electromagnetic wave propagation, as described by Hertz and further developed by others.

Today, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are recognized, but his specific claims about the nature of wireless signals have largely been superseded by modern understanding and technology.
