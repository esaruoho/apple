Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a simple propagation of waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept now known as "earth resonance" or "Tesla coil" technology. According to Tesla, this approach would allow for more efficient and longer-range transmission without the need for high-frequency oscillations that were characteristic of the Hertzian wave theory.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was overly focused on the propagation of waves through space, which he felt was not the primary mechanism of wireless communication. Instead, he emphasized the role of the Earth and the ionosphere in facilitating long-distance communication. This perspective led him to develop and patent various devices and methods for wireless communication that differed from the prevailing scientific understanding of the time.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. Modern science has largely validated the principles of electromagnetic radiation, but it also acknowledges the contributions of Tesla to the development of practical wireless technologies.
