Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that his discoveries and inventions were based on a different principle than what was being described by the Hertzian wave theory.

Tesla's work with wireless transmission was rooted in his understanding of the Earth's ionosphere and the propagation of electrical currents through the ground and the atmosphere. He proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through the use of resonant circuits and the natural properties of the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than through the transmission of electromagnetic waves as described by Heinrich Hertz.

In his view, the Hertzian wave theory, which describes the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. Tesla argued that the concept of "radio waves" as understood at the time was too limited and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication.

This disagreement between Tesla and the prevailing scientific understanding of the time led to some controversy and misunderstanding. However, it is important to note that modern science has largely validated Tesla's approach to wireless communication, particularly in the context of long-distance and ground-based systems.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in the field of wireless communication. Tesla's contributions have been recognized for their significance in developing practical applications for wireless technology, even though his ideas about the nature of wireless communication differed from those of his contemporaries.
