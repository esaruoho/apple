Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being described by the then-current scientific understanding. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through space was an oversimplification and did not accurately represent the phenomena he observed and utilized in his experiments.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of what he called "resonance" or "sympathetic vibration," suggesting that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth's natural resonant frequency. This concept was quite different from the wave theory, which suggested that signals traveled through the air as waves.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, but they have since gained recognition as precursors to modern concepts such as ground wave propagation and the use of the Earth's ionosphere for radio communication. Today, many researchers acknowledge that Tesla's ideas about resonance and the Earth's role in wireless communication were ahead of their time and have contributed to our understanding of wireless technology.

It's important to note that while Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, the electromagnetic radiation theory still forms the basis of much of our current understanding and application of wireless technology.
