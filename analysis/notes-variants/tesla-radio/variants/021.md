Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to what he called "radiant energy" or "ether," rather than just electromagnetic waves propagating through space. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which involved the transmission of energy through space without a physical medium, was incomplete and potentially misleading.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of the Earth itself as a conductor and resonator in wireless transmission. He believed that the Earth could act as a conductor for the transmission of signals, and that this could be used to transmit information over long distances without the need for a line-of-sight or a physical medium like air or vacuum.

Tesla's views were controversial at the time and have been subject to much debate among scientists and historians. While modern physics has largely validated the concept of electromagnetic waves as a means of wireless communication, Tesla's ideas about the role of the Earth and the nature of the medium for wireless transmission continue to be studied and debated.

It's important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, even if his specific theories about the nature of the medium for transmission have not been fully supported by subsequent scientific research. His work laid the groundwork for many of the technologies we use today, including radio and wireless communication systems.
