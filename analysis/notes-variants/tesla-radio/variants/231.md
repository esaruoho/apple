Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being proposed by the prevailing scientific community at the time. He argued that the idea of sending signals through the air as waves was too indirect and inefficient for practical applications. Instead, Tesla advocated for a system where energy could be transmitted directly from one point to another without the need for a physical medium or wave propagation.

One of Tesla's key points was that the wireless transmission of information and energy should be possible without relying on the concept of waves traveling through space. He proposed that there could be a direct connection between transmitter and receiver, much like a conductor would transmit electricity. This idea was revolutionary at the time and contributed to the development of his patents and inventions, including the Tesla coil, which is still used in various electronic devices today.

Despite Tesla's assertions, the concept of electromagnetic waves as described by James Clerk Maxwell and later experimentally confirmed by Hertz became the foundation for modern radio and wireless technology. However, Tesla's ideas and contributions continue to be recognized and studied, particularly in the context of alternative approaches to wireless communication and the history of electrical engineering.
