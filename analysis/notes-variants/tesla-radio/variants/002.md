Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his understanding of the wireless transmission of energy and information. He emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the ability to transmit energy without the need for a physical medium, such as wires. According to Tesla, the idea of Hertzian waves, which involved the transmission of energy through space via oscillating electric and magnetic fields, was too limited and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication.

In particular, Tesla argued that the concept of Hertzian waves could not explain phenomena such as the long-distance transmission of energy and the reception of signals without a direct line of sight or physical connection. He proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through the resonance of the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" and "space currents."

While Tesla's ideas were groundbreaking and have since been validated to some extent, the scientific community at the time was more inclined to accept the established theories of electromagnetism. It took many years for the broader scientific community to recognize the validity of Tesla's concepts, particularly regarding the use of resonant circuits and the transmission of energy over long distances without wires.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in our understanding of wireless communication. Tesla's contributions to the field include the development of resonant circuits, which are still used in various applications today, including radio broadcasting and wireless charging.
