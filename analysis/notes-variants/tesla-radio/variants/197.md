Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory, in fully explaining his conception of wireless technology. Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of energy and information could be achieved through aether or some form of non-material medium, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments and theories, which suggested that wireless communication could be achieved without the need for a physical medium such as wires. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the Earth's natural resonant frequency. This concept is often referred to as "resonance" or "Earth resonance," and it was one of the key ideas behind Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower project.

However, it's important to note that Tesla's ideas were not widely accepted at the time, and the scientific community largely embraced the Hertzian wave theory, which posited that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space and be received by antennas. The development of radio technology was based on this theory, and it became the foundation for modern wireless communication.

Despite the skepticism from the scientific community, Tesla continued to advocate for his theories and conducted experiments to demonstrate the potential of wireless transmission. His work laid the groundwork for many future developments in wireless technology, even if his specific ideas about aether and Earth resonance were not fully validated during his lifetime.

In summary, while Tesla did claim that the Hertzian wave theory was insufficient for understanding his vision of wireless communication, the broader scientific community adopted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for radio technology. Tesla's contributions remain significant, both in terms of his experimental work and his theoretical insights into the nature of wireless communication.
