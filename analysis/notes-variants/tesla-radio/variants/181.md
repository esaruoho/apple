Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a mere propagation of waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept now known as "earth resonance" or "Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower." According to Tesla, this method would allow for much greater range and efficiency in wireless communication compared to the then-prevailing view of radio waves as simply propagating through the air.

In his writings and public demonstrations, Tesla often criticized what he saw as the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory, arguing that it did not adequately explain the phenomena he observed and experienced with his own inventions. He believed that the theory was overly simplistic and did not account for the complex interactions between the transmitter, receiver, and the Earth.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were not universally accepted at the time. The scientific community largely adopted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for radio technology, and it is this theory that forms the foundation of modern radio communications. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers exploring new methods of wireless communication.
