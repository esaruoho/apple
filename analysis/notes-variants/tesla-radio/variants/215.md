Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or radio waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments and observations, particularly those conducted at Wardenclyffe, where he attempted to transmit electrical energy without wires over long distances. He argued that the use of high frequencies and resonant circuits could achieve more efficient and effective wireless transmission than the lower frequencies used in Hertzian wave theory.

Some key points of Tesla's criticism include:

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that using resonant circuits could achieve much greater efficiency and range in wireless communication.

Frequency: He argued that using higher frequencies would be more effective for long-distance transmission. Lower frequencies, as proposed by Hertzian wave theory, were less suitable for practical applications.

Earth's Ionosphere: Tesla believed that the Earth's ionosphere played a crucial role in wireless transmission. He thought that signals could be reflected off the ionosphere, allowing for global communication.

Interference: Tesla was concerned about the interference caused by Hertzian waves, which he believed could disrupt the intended signal. He sought to develop methods to minimize such interference.

Despite these differences, it is important to note that Tesla's work laid the foundation for modern radio technology. While his ideas about wireless transmission were not entirely accurate, they inspired further research and experimentation that eventually led to the development of practical radio systems.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories have their merits and limitations. Modern wireless communication systems often combine elements from both approaches, using resonant circuits and higher frequencies to achieve efficient and reliable transmission.
