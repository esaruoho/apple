Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as short-range, line-of-sight radio waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were based on his own experiments and theories, particularly those related to long-distance wireless transmission. He argued that the idea of Hertzian waves being limited to direct line-of-sight transmission was too restrictive for practical applications. Tesla believed that wireless signals could travel through the Earth and air, and he conducted experiments to demonstrate this, such as his Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to transmit electrical energy without wires over vast distances.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which was later refined into what we now understand as radio waves. Tesla's ideas were more aligned with what is now known as ground wave propagation and ionospheric reflection, concepts that allow signals to travel around the curvature of the Earth.

It's important to note that Tesla's views were not universally accepted during his time, and the scientific community largely adopted the Hertzian wave theory as the foundation for radio technology. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to be studied and debated in the context of modern wireless technologies.
