Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (radio waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments and theories about wireless transmission, which he believed could be achieved through aether or some form of invisible medium that could carry energy without the need for physical waves. He proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through aether currents, which would allow for instantaneous transmission over vast distances without the need for a line-of-sight or physical medium like air or water.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was widely accepted at the time, suggested that radio waves traveled through space as electromagnetic waves. Tesla argued that this theory was incomplete and that it did not account for the phenomena he observed and experimented with, such as the transmission of energy and information without wires.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism and criticism from the scientific community of his time. However, his work laid the foundation for modern wireless technology, and many of his ideas have since been validated and incorporated into our understanding of electromagnetism and wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and influential, they also contained elements that were later found to be incorrect or incomplete. Nonetheless, his contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his legacy continues to inspire researchers and engineers working on advanced wireless technologies today.
