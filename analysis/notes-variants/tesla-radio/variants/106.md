Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that his own discoveries and inventions involved principles that went beyond what was understood by the electromagnetic radiation theory alone.

Tesla's claims were based on his extensive work with high-frequency alternating currents and his belief in the existence of "wireless" or "long-distance" transmission of energy without the need for physical conductors. He argued that the propagation of signals through the air could be achieved through a different mechanism than what Hertz had described.

Some key points of Tesla's perspective include:

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that the proper tuning of these devices was crucial for efficient communication, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

Earth as a Conductor: Tesla proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for wireless signals. This idea was part of his concept of "earth resonance," where the Earth's natural frequency could be used to transmit and receive signals over vast distances.

Coherer and Other Devices: Tesla developed devices like the coherer, which he believed could detect and amplify signals more effectively than simple electromagnetic waves.

Wireless Power Transmission: Beyond just communication, Tesla also believed in the possibility of transmitting power wirelessly, an idea that was ahead of its time and not fully understood at the time.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. Modern understanding of radio waves and wireless communication has largely validated many aspects of Hertz's theory, though Tesla's contributions to the field remain highly respected and continue to inspire research and innovation.

It's important to note that Tesla's skepticism did not negate the validity of Hertz's work but rather highlighted the limitations of the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. Tesla's approach to wireless communication was more holistic, encompassing both the physical and the resonant properties of the medium.
