Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the theory as it stood at the time did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's concerns were rooted in several aspects:

Wave Theory vs. Particle Theory: Tesla preferred a particle-like model of energy transmission over the wave theory. He believed that energy could be transmitted through space without the need for a medium, akin to how he conceptualized his "radiant energy" or "cosmic rays."

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in wireless transmission. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a resonant medium, allowing for more efficient and powerful transmission of energy. This concept was different from the idea of radio waves propagating through space like ripples on a pond.

Coherence: Tesla argued that the coherence of the signals was crucial for effective communication. He believed that the signals should be coherent and not just random waves, which aligns with his ideas about using specific frequencies and resonant circuits.

Interference: Tesla was concerned about the interference that could occur with the wave theory, especially with multiple transmitters operating in close proximity. He believed that this would lead to signal degradation and interference, which he sought to avoid.

Practical Applications: Tesla saw practical applications of his ideas in various technologies, including lighting, power distribution, and wireless communication. His inventions, such as the Tesla coil, were designed to work within these principles.

While Tesla's theories and inventions have had a profound impact on the field of wireless communication, the wave theory eventually became the dominant framework for understanding radio waves. However, Tesla's contributions and criticisms continue to be studied and debated by historians and scientists alike.
