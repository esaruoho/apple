Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates the widespread acceptance of Heinrich Hertz's discoveries, which laid the foundation for the modern understanding of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it. He argued that the idea of sending signals through space via these waves was too simplistic and did not account for the complex interactions between the transmitter, receiver, and the medium itself. Tesla's approach emphasized the role of the Earth and the ionosphere in transmitting and receiving signals, which he referred to as "earth currents" or "resonant circuits."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused more on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, without the need for a conductive medium. This difference in perspective led to some tension between Tesla and other scientists of his era who were more aligned with the Hertzian wave theory.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about wireless communication were innovative and ahead of their time, they were not universally accepted at the time. It took several decades for the scientific community to fully embrace the principles of electromagnetic radiation as described by Hertz and later refined by others. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his work continues to inspire researchers and engineers today.
