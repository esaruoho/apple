Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and a resonant medium for wireless signals, a concept he referred to as "earth currents" or "resonance."

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time held that wireless signals could be transmitted through the air using electromagnetic waves. This theory suggested that the waves would propagate through space and could be received by antennas tuned to the same frequency. However, Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and that the true nature of wireless transmission involved a deeper interaction with the Earth's natural electrical properties.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism and controversy during his lifetime. Nevertheless, his work laid the foundation for many modern technologies, including radio, and his ideas continue to influence discussions in the field of wireless communication and electromagnetics.
