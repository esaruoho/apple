Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory, in explaining his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the theory did not fully capture the essence of what he referred to as "radiant energy," which he thought was more akin to a form of energy transmission that could be used for long-distance communication without the need for wires.

Tesla's views were based on his experimental work and theoretical insights. He conducted extensive experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and developed devices such as the Tesla coil, which he believed could transmit energy through the air without the need for a physical conductor. Tesla argued that the Hertzian waves, as described by Heinrich Hertz, were too limited in their ability to carry information over long distances without significant loss or distortion.

In contrast, Tesla proposed that radiant energy could be modulated and transmitted in a way that allowed for clear and efficient communication. He believed that this form of energy transmission was more akin to what he called "resonance," where the transmitter and receiver would resonate at the same frequency, allowing for the transfer of information without the need for a physical medium.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not fully supported by contemporary scientific understanding. The development of practical radio technology came later, and it was based on the principles of electromagnetic radiation rather than Tesla's concept of radiant energy. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire discussions in the realm of alternative theories of energy transmission and communication.
