Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being described by the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. He argued that the transmission of information through the air was not simply a matter of radiating energy in all directions, but rather involved a more focused and coherent form of energy transfer.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the idea of tuning to specific frequencies, which he believed could achieve more efficient and targeted communication. This resonant approach was different from the broader, omnidirectional radiation suggested by the Hertzian wave theory.

It's worth noting that Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, his contributions to the field of wireless communication laid the groundwork for many modern technologies, including radio and telecommunications.

While Tesla's claims were not immediately validated or understood by the scientific community of his era, they have since been recognized as prescient and influential in the development of wireless technology.
