Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as being transmitted through space via oscillating electric and magnetic fields, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a vacuum. He proposed that wireless signals could travel through the Earth itself, a concept he referred to as "earth currents" or "terrestrial currents." This idea was based on his observations of the effects of electrical discharges on the Earth and the ionosphere.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory suggested that electromagnetic waves could propagate through free space without requiring a medium. Tesla argued that this theory was incomplete and did not account for the true nature of wireless communication. He believed that the Earth played a crucial role in the transmission and reception of these signals, and that the concept of Hertzian waves alone was insufficient to explain the phenomena he observed and utilized in his experiments and inventions.

Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories have elements of truth, and that wireless communication involves a combination of both ground waves and atmospheric waves. Nonetheless, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his skepticism towards the Hertzian wave theory reflects his innovative approach to the subject.
