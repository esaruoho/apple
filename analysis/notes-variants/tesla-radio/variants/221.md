Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication should be based on a different principle than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, which he referred to as "earth currents" or "resonant circuits." This idea was encapsulated in his patents and described in his writings, where he discussed methods for transmitting information without relying solely on the air as a medium for electromagnetic waves.

In contrast, the prevailing scientific understanding at the time was that electromagnetic waves could propagate through the air and that this was sufficient for wireless communication. Tesla's claims and demonstrations, such as his famous Wardenclyffe Tower project, aimed to prove that wireless communication could be achieved using different principles, including the use of the Earth as a conductor.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved to include both air-based electromagnetic waves and ground-based currents, but Tesla's contributions remain significant in the history of wireless technology.
