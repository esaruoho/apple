Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to what he called "radiant energy" or "cosmic energy," which he thought could travel through the Earth and air without the need for a physical medium like the vacuum. He proposed that this energy could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept he referred to as "earth resonance."

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time, based on Hertz's experiments, suggested that electromagnetic waves required a vacuum to propagate and that they could be detected using antennas. Tesla argued that this approach was limited and did not fully exploit the potential of wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about wireless transmission were innovative and influential, they also faced significant challenges and were not immediately accepted by the scientific community. However, his work laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology, including radio, television, and cellular networks.
