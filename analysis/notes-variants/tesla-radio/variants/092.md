Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (radio waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what Hertzian waves suggested. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself or through the ionosphere, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air. This idea was encapsulated in his theories of "earth resonance" and "wireless transmission without wires."

In his view, the traditional Hertzian wave theory was overly simplistic and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication. Tesla argued that the proper understanding of wireless transmission required a more holistic approach, taking into account the Earth's natural properties and the ionosphere's role in signal propagation.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and prescient, they were also ahead of their time. The technology and scientific understanding necessary to fully implement his theories only became available much later, with advancements in electronics and telecommunications.

Despite these differences, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are undeniable. His work laid the groundwork for modern radio and wireless technologies, even if his specific theories about the nature of wireless transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime.
