Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla's work on radio and wireless technology was groundbreaking, but his views on the electromagnetic radiation theory were somewhat controversial.

Tesla believed that the electromagnetic waves described by Heinrich Hertz were not the same as the waves used in wireless transmission. He argued that the Hertzian waves were too weak and did not carry information over long distances, which was essential for practical wireless communication. Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself or through the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" or "space currents."

Tesla's ideas were based on his observations and experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and resonant circuits. He believed that these currents could be used to transmit information without relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air. This concept was different from the prevailing view at the time, which was that radio waves could be transmitted through the air using antennas.

While Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, including the development of the Tesla coil and the design of early radio transmitters, his ideas about the nature of electromagnetic radiation were not widely accepted during his lifetime. It wasn't until later that other scientists and engineers developed the technology to transmit radio waves effectively, which is now the basis of modern radio and telecommunications.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in wireless communication. Hertzian waves are used in practical radio and television broadcasting, while Tesla's ideas about earth currents and space currents have influenced the development of technologies such as ground-penetrating radar and certain types of wireless communication systems.
