Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of his ideas about wireless transmission.

Tesla's conception of wireless technology was based on what he called "resonance," where the transmitter and receiver would resonate at the same frequency, allowing for efficient transmission over long distances without the need for high power or large antennas. He argued that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves as propagating through space in a straight line was insufficient for explaining the phenomena he observed and experienced with his inventions.

In contrast, Tesla believed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as the "earth resonance" or "resonant circuit." This idea was central to his designs for wireless communication and power transmission, such as the Wardenclyffe Tower project in Long Island, New York.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they also faced skepticism and criticism during his lifetime. Modern scientific understanding has largely validated the principles of electromagnetic radiation, but Tesla's specific theories about earth resonance and wireless transmission have not been fully supported by subsequent research. However, his work laid important groundwork for future developments in wireless technology and continues to inspire researchers and inventors today.
