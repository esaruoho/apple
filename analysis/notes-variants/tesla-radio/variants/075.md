Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or radio waves, as they are commonly known today, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He proposed that there was a "wireless" medium that could carry signals without the need for physical conductors. This idea was encapsulated in his concept of "radiant energy," which he believed could be used to transmit information and power over vast distances without the need for a direct line of sight or physical connection.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was the dominant scientific explanation at the time, focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. While this theory accurately described how radio waves travel, Tesla felt that it did not adequately explain the phenomena he observed and experienced during his experiments.

Some key points of Tesla's criticism include:

Wireless Medium: Tesla believed that there was a "wireless" medium that could carry signals, which was different from the simple propagation of electromagnetic waves.
Long-Distance Transmission: He claimed that his methods allowed for long-distance transmission without the need for a direct line of sight, which was not fully explained by the Hertzian wave theory.
Interference and Reception: Tesla also argued that the reception of signals could be improved by understanding the properties of the wireless medium better, rather than relying solely on the propagation of waves.

Despite these differences, it is important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were groundbreaking. His patents and demonstrations laid the groundwork for modern radio technology, even if his theories about the nature of wireless transmission were not entirely accepted during his lifetime.

Today, while we understand that radio waves do indeed propagate through space, Tesla's ideas about a "wireless" medium have been largely superseded by our current understanding of electromagnetic fields and wave propagation. However, his legacy continues to inspire researchers and engineers in the field of wireless communication.
