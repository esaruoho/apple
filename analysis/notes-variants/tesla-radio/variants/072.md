Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's views on this matter were rooted in his own conceptualization of wireless communication and energy transmission.

Tesla believed that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space, as proposed by Heinrich Hertz, did not fully capture the essence of how wireless signals could be transmitted and received. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through a vacuum was an oversimplification and did not align with his observations and experiments.

Tesla's approach to wireless communication was more focused on the manipulation of electrical currents and the creation of resonant circuits. He believed that by tuning these circuits to specific frequencies, one could achieve efficient and long-range communication without relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves. This is reflected in his work on resonant inductive coupling, which forms the basis for modern RFID technology and some aspects of wireless charging.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized what he saw as the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory, suggesting that it did not adequately explain phenomena such as the ability to transmit signals over vast distances without significant loss of power. He maintained that his methods of wireless transmission were superior because they involved direct energy transfer between resonant systems rather than relying on the propagation of waves.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, the scientific community at the time was still developing a full understanding of electromagnetic phenomena. The Hertzian wave theory eventually became widely accepted as a fundamental part of electromagnetism, but Tesla's contributions to the field continue to be recognized and studied.
