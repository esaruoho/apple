Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views can be summarized as follows:

Wireless Transmission vs. Radiation: Tesla argued that the transmission of information through space without wires was fundamentally different from the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He believed that the wireless process involved a more direct form of energy transmission, which he referred to as "radiant energy," rather than just the propagation of waves.

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in wireless communication. He proposed that the Earth could act as a resonant medium for wireless signals, allowing for the transmission of information over vast distances without the need for physical conductors. This idea was based on his experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and his belief in the Earth's natural resonant properties.

Interference and Reception: Tesla also criticized the notion that wireless signals would interfere with each other or require complex apparatus for reception. He believed that simpler devices could effectively receive and transmit signals, which aligned with his practical demonstrations of wireless communication.

Philosophical Differences: Beyond technical arguments, Tesla's philosophical stance on science and technology may have influenced his views. He often spoke about the spiritual and metaphysical aspects of scientific discovery, which set him apart from many of his contemporaries who focused solely on empirical evidence.

Despite these criticisms, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are undeniable. His work laid the groundwork for modern radio and wireless technology, even if his specific theories about the nature of wireless transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime. The development of radio and wireless communication continued to evolve, eventually incorporating elements of both Tesla's ideas and the electromagnetic radiation theory developed by others.
