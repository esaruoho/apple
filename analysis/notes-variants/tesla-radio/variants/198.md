Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla's work on radio and wireless technology was groundbreaking, but his views on the electromagnetic radiation theory were somewhat controversial.

Tesla believed that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space as described by Heinrich Hertz did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through a vacuum could not explain how signals could be transmitted over long distances without a line-of-sight path or some form of medium. Tesla proposed that wireless signals traveled through the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" or "space currents."

In his view, the electromagnetic radiation theory was incomplete because it did not account for the way signals could penetrate buildings and other obstacles, which was a key aspect of practical wireless communication. Tesla's approach emphasized the role of the Earth itself as a conductor and resonator, which could carry and transmit signals over vast distances.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of modern radio, they were not universally accepted at the time. The scientific community generally adopted Hertz's wave theory as the basis for understanding electromagnetic radiation and wireless communication. However, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to be studied and debated in the context of wireless technology and the history of science.
