Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's views on this matter were rooted in his own discoveries and theories regarding wireless transmission.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he understood it. He argued that the idea of waves propagating through space was an oversimplification and did not accurately represent the phenomena he observed and utilized.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of what he called "resonance" or "resonant coupling," which he believed was a more accurate description of how signals could be transmitted without the need for a physical medium like air or vacuum. He thought that resonant circuits could be tuned to specific frequencies, allowing for efficient energy transfer over distances, which he saw as a more fundamental aspect of wireless communication than simply transmitting waves.

Tesla's claims were often met with skepticism from other scientists and engineers of the time, who were more aligned with the emerging electromagnetic wave theory. However, his work and ideas have since been recognized as foundational to the development of modern wireless technology, including radio, television, and cellular networks.

It's important to note that while Tesla's views were innovative and influential, they also reflected a certain level of controversy and debate within the scientific community of his era. The full implications and accuracy of his theories continue to be subjects of study and discussion among historians and scientists today.
