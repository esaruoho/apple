Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct form of energy transfer between the transmitter and receiver, often referred to as "resonance" or "resonant coupling." He argued that the key to effective wireless communication was not just the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space but also the resonance between the transmitter and receiver circuits. This resonance allowed for the efficient transfer of energy without relying solely on the propagation of waves.

In contrast, the electromagnetic radiation theory proposed by Hertz focused on the propagation of waves through space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete explanation. Tesla believed that the waves alone could not account for the observed phenomena of wireless communication, such as the ability to transmit signals over long distances with relatively low power.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology, including radio, television, and cellular communications. However, his theories were not widely accepted during his lifetime due to the established scientific consensus around Hertzian waves. It wasn't until later that researchers began to understand and validate some of Tesla's concepts, particularly in the realm of resonant coupling and wireless energy transfer.

Today, while we recognize the importance of both Hertzian waves and resonant coupling in wireless communication, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire research into new forms of wireless technology.
