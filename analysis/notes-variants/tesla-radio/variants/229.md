Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was different from the electromagnetic waves described by Hertz. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" or "space currents." This idea was part of his broader concept of a wireless world where information and energy could be transmitted without the need for physical wires.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which was seen as a more direct method of transmitting signals. Tesla argued that this approach was limited and did not fully exploit the potential of wireless technology.

Some key points of Tesla's critique include:

Earth Currents: Tesla believed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for wireless signals, allowing for the transmission of information through the ground rather than through the air.
Space Currents: He also proposed that signals could be transmitted through the ionosphere, which is the upper layer of the Earth's atmosphere.
Wireless Power Transmission: Tesla was particularly interested in the possibility of transmitting electrical power wirelessly, which he demonstrated with his Wardenclyffe Tower project.
Interference and Reception: Tesla argued that the reception of wireless signals would be more reliable if they were transmitted through these mediums rather than through the air, which could be subject to interference.

Despite these differences, it's important to note that modern science has largely validated the principles of electromagnetic radiation, and Hertzian waves are now understood to be an accurate description of how electromagnetic signals propagate. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless technology, including his patents and demonstrations, have been recognized and continue to influence the development of wireless communication systems today.
