Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and disagreement with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others, such as Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that his own discoveries and methods were fundamentally different from what was being described by the electromagnetic radiation theory.

Tesla's claims and criticisms can be summarized as follows:

Wireless Transmission vs. Electromagnetic Radiation: Tesla argued that his wireless transmission methods did not rely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. Instead, he believed that wireless communication could be achieved through aetheric or radiant energy, which he thought was more efficient and direct.

Coherers and Reception: Tesla criticized the use of coherers (a type of detector used in early radio receivers) and the reliance on electromagnetic waves for reception. He believed that these devices were not necessary and that the signals could be detected without them.

Interference and Interception: Tesla was concerned about the potential for interference and interception of radio signals using electromagnetic radiation theory. He believed that his methods were less susceptible to such issues.

Innovation and Originality: Tesla often emphasized his original contributions to wireless technology, distinguishing himself from those who he felt were merely building upon his work without fully understanding its principles.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not universally accepted at the time. The electromagnetic radiation theory eventually became widely accepted as the basis for modern radio technology, although Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant and continue to be recognized.

Today, historians and scientists generally agree that Tesla's work laid the groundwork for many aspects of modern wireless communication, even if some of his specific theories and methods were not entirely accurate according to contemporary scientific understanding.
