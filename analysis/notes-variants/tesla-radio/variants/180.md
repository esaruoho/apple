Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's approach to wireless technology was based on his belief in the existence of "resonant" or "resonance" effects between distant objects. He argued that the Earth itself could be used as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the Earth's natural resonances rather than through the air. This idea is often referred to as "Earth resonance" or "Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was widely accepted at the time, posited that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space without requiring a medium like air or water. Tesla saw this as a limitation because it did not account for the direct coupling between transmitter and receiver, which he believed was essential for efficient wireless transmission.

Tesla's claims were controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that his ideas about Earth resonance have some validity. For example, it has been demonstrated that certain frequencies can resonate with the Earth, and this phenomenon is now understood to play a role in various wireless technologies, including some forms of long-range communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted at the time and required further development and validation over subsequent decades.
