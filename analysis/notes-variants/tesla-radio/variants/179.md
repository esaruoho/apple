Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as short, localized bursts of electromagnetic energy, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's conception of wireless transmission was rooted in his belief that it could be achieved through the Earth itself and the ionosphere, rather than through the emission of discrete waves. He proposed that the Earth could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it without the need for the air or space to be filled with discrete waves. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which became the foundation for modern radio technology, emphasized the transmission of electromagnetic waves through space. Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and potentially harmful because it implied that the air or space between the transmitter and receiver was being disturbed by these waves, which could interfere with other communications and cause other issues.

Tesla's views on this matter were detailed in various writings and speeches throughout his career. For example, in his 1903 lecture "On Light and Other High Frequency Phenomena," Tesla discussed his ideas about wireless transmission and criticized the prevailing theories of the time, including those of Hertz.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not widely accepted at the time. Modern understanding of radio waves and wireless communication has evolved significantly since Tesla's time, incorporating both the principles of Hertzian waves and Tesla's concepts of earth resonance and wireless transmission.
