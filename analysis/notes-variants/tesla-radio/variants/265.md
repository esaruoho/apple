Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates the widespread acceptance of Heinrich Hertz's discoveries, which laid the groundwork for the modern understanding of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (electromagnetic waves) was not sufficient to explain the phenomena of wireless transmission as he understood it. He argued that the idea of waves propagating through space was an oversimplification and did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication. Tesla emphasized the role of the Earth and the ionosphere in transmitting signals, which is now known as ground wave propagation.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space. Tesla's approach was more holistic, incorporating the Earth's conductive properties and the ionosphere, which he believed were crucial for long-distance wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not widely accepted at the time. It wasn't until later that scientists and engineers began to understand and incorporate the full range of phenomena involved in wireless transmission, including the role of the Earth and ionosphere.

Today, we recognize that both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions are essential to our understanding of wireless technology. Tesla's work on resonant circuits and his experiments with wireless transmission laid the groundwork for many of the principles used in modern radio and telecommunications.
