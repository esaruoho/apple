Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others, including Heinrich Hertz. Tesla's views on this matter were complex and often expressed in a way that highlighted his own contributions and inventions.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves as understood at the time did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that these waves were too diffuse and spread out, making them unsuitable for practical wireless transmission over long distances. Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept he referred to as "earth currents."

In his patents and writings, Tesla described methods for transmitting electrical energy without wires, which he believed could be more efficient and effective than relying solely on electromagnetic radiation. He emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the use of specific frequencies to achieve better transmission and reception of signals.

Tesla's claims and theories were controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, his work laid the groundwork for many modern wireless technologies, including radio, television, and cellular communications. The debate between Tesla's ideas and the established electromagnetic theory continued well into the 20th century and beyond.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also ahead of their time in some respects. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved significantly since Tesla's era, incorporating both his concepts and those of other scientists who built upon his work.
