Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others, including Heinrich Hertz. Tesla's views on this matter were complex and multifaceted.

Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (which refers to the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the transmission of information over long distances could be achieved without the need for a line-of-sight transmission medium, which is a key aspect of Hertzian wave theory. Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could travel through the Earth and the ionosphere, a concept sometimes referred to as "earth resonance."

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized what he saw as the limitations of Hertzian wave theory. He believed that the theory was overly focused on the idea of waves propagating through space and did not adequately account for the role of the Earth and other conductive media in transmitting signals. This perspective led him to develop his own theories and methods for wireless communication, which included the use of resonant circuits and the concept of "wireless" or "long-distance" transmission.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas about wireless communication were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, many of his concepts have since been validated and incorporated into modern wireless technology. For example, the idea of using the Earth as a conductor for radio waves has become a fundamental principle in groundwave propagation and long-distance radio communication.

In summary, Tesla's claims that the electromagnetic radiation theory was inimical to the proper understanding of the wireless process as he conceived it reflect his belief in the importance of the Earth and other conductive media in facilitating long-range wireless communication, which differed from the prevailing Hertzian wave theory of his time.
