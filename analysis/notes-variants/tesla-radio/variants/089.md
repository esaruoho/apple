Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory, in explaining his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the theory did not fully capture the essence of what he considered to be true wireless transmission.

Tesla's views were rooted in his experiments and observations. He conducted extensive research on high-frequency alternating currents and developed devices such as the Tesla coil, which could produce high-voltage, high-frequency currents. Tesla argued that these high-frequency currents could propagate through the air without the need for a physical conductor, which he saw as a more efficient and natural method of transmitting energy and information.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was based on the work of Heinrich Hertz, primarily described the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space using a physical medium like a wire antenna. Tesla felt that this approach was too limited and did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and experimented with.

One of Tesla's key points was that his methods could transmit signals over much greater distances and with less power than the Hertzian wave theory would predict. He also believed that his methods could achieve better signal clarity and reduce interference. These claims were controversial at the time and remain subjects of debate among historians and scientists today.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also ahead of their time in many respects. The technology and understanding of wireless communication have evolved significantly since Tesla's era, and modern theories and practices have largely incorporated elements from both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions.
