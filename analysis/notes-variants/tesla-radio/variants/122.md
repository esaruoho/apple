Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the nature of radio waves were quite different from those of Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with the discovery of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that radio waves could be transmitted without the need for a line-of-sight connection, which was a key aspect of his vision for wireless communication. He argued that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be sent through the ground or the air without the need for a physical medium like a wire. This concept was part of what he called "earth currents" or "wireless transmission."

In contrast, the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Hertz suggested that radio waves required a line-of-sight path between the transmitter and receiver. Tesla felt that this theory was incomplete and did not fully capture the potential of wireless communication.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments and observations, which led him to believe that radio waves could travel much farther and more efficiently than what the Hertzian wave theory suggested. He also believed that the Earth's ionosphere played a crucial role in reflecting and guiding these waves, allowing them to travel vast distances.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were not universally accepted at the time. The scientific community largely adopted the Hertzian wave theory, which eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication cannot be overstated, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers to this day.
