Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's conception of wireless technology was rooted in his belief that energy could be transmitted without wires through the medium of the Earth itself, a concept he referred to as "earth currents" or "resonant circuits." He argued that the transmission of information and energy could be achieved through these resonant circuits, which would allow for more efficient and widespread communication.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which was seen as a more direct method of transmitting signals. Tesla criticized this approach, arguing that it was not as efficient or practical for long-distance communication as he believed it should be.

One of the key points of contention between Tesla and the proponents of Hertzian waves was the idea of using high frequencies for wireless communication. Tesla favored lower frequencies, which he believed could travel greater distances and penetrate the Earth more effectively. This belief led him to develop systems that utilized lower frequency waves, such as his Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to demonstrate the feasibility of long-distance wireless transmission.

Despite Tesla's claims and demonstrations, the scientific community at the time largely embraced the Hertzian wave theory, which eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology. However, Tesla's ideas about earth currents and resonant circuits have since gained renewed interest, particularly in the context of modern research into wireless technologies and the exploration of alternative methods of energy and information transmission.

It's important to note that while Tesla's views were influential and contributed to the development of wireless technology, the Hertzian wave theory remains the basis for much of our current understanding and application of radio waves.
