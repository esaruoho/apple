Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than simply radiating energy through space. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself or through the ionosphere, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described methods for transmitting information without the need for a line-of-sight transmission, which was a key aspect of the Hertzian wave theory. He argued that the Earth could act as a conductor and a medium for wireless communication, a concept that is now known as ground wave propagation.

Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, they laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology, including radio, television, and mobile communications. Today, we understand that both ground wave propagation and Hertzian wave propagation play roles in wireless communication, depending on the frequency and other factors.

It's important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were significant, even if his theories about the nature of wireless transmission were not entirely accurate according to contemporary scientific understanding. His work inspired many others and paved the way for the development of modern telecommunications.
