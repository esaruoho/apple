Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his work with resonant circuits and his belief in the ability to transmit energy through the Earth itself without relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air. He argued that the idea of Hertzian waves being the primary means of wireless communication was overly simplistic and did not account for the potential of resonant systems to achieve long-distance communication.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described methods for wireless transmission that involved resonant circuits tuned to specific frequencies, which could then resonate with similar circuits at the receiving end. This approach allowed for more efficient and longer-range communication compared to what he perceived as the limitations of Hertzian wave theory.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they also faced skepticism and criticism during his lifetime. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved to incorporate both resonant circuit principles and the propagation of electromagnetic waves, acknowledging the contributions of various scientists and inventors, including Tesla himself.
