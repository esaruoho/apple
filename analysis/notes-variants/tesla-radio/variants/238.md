Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication should be based on a system of resonant circuits rather than the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the idea of Hertzian waves being transmitted through the air was an oversimplification and did not accurately describe the mechanism of wireless communication.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described his system as involving the resonance between transmitter and receiver circuits, which would allow for more efficient and targeted communication. This approach contrasted with the prevailing view that electromagnetic waves could propagate through the air without needing a direct line of sight or resonant circuitry.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not widely accepted at the time. The development of radio technology eventually relied more heavily on the principles of electromagnetic wave propagation, although Tesla's contributions to the field remain highly regarded.
