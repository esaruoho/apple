Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates many of the developments attributed to Guglielmo Marconi, although both contributed to the field.

Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it. He argued that the idea of propagating waves through space was insufficient for practical wireless communication. Instead, Tesla emphasized the role of resonant circuits and the Earth's ionosphere in transmitting signals.

In his view, the key to effective wireless communication lay in the resonance between the transmitter and receiver, rather than simply radiating energy into space. This resonant approach allowed for more efficient and targeted transmission of information. Tesla's patents and demonstrations often focused on this principle, which he believed could achieve much greater range and clarity in wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they also faced skepticism and criticism from other scientists and engineers of his time. The widespread adoption of Marconi's methods, which were based more directly on Hertzian wave theory, eventually led to the standardization of radio technology as we know it today.

Despite these differences, both Tesla and Marconi contributed significantly to the development of radio technology, and their work laid the foundation for modern telecommunications.
