Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's experiments and findings. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or radio waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and a resonant medium for wireless signals, a concept he referred to as "earth resonance." This idea was encapsulated in his notion of "wireless" transmission, where signals could travel without the need for physical wires.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which was seen as a more direct and measurable form of wireless communication. Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and that it did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and utilized in his experiments.

One of the key points of contention between Tesla and the Hertzian wave theory was the idea of long-distance wireless transmission. Tesla demonstrated the ability to transmit and receive signals over vast distances using his resonant circuits and earth as a conductor, which he believed supported his theories better than the Hertzian wave model. However, the scientific community at the time largely accepted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about earth resonance and wireless transmission were innovative and have since been validated in part (for example, in the use of the Earth as a ground plane in antenna design), they were not widely accepted during his lifetime due to the limitations of the technology and the prevailing scientific consensus of the time.
