Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates many of the theories and experiments associated with the Hertzian wave theory, named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of information could be achieved through aetheric or radiant energy, which he thought was different from the electromagnetic waves described by Hertz. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he understood it. Tesla's views were based on his extensive work with high-frequency alternating currents and his belief in the existence of a medium (aether) that could transmit these currents without the need for wires.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which was later refined into what we now understand as radio waves. Tesla's approach emphasized the use of resonant circuits and the manipulation of aetheric energy, which he believed could achieve more efficient and longer-range communication.

It is important to note that Tesla's ideas were not widely accepted at the time, and his claims about the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory were often met with skepticism. However, his contributions to the field of wireless communication are now recognized, and much of modern radio technology can be traced back to his pioneering work.
