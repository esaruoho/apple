Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as localized disturbances in the electric and magnetic fields, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more fundamental interaction between matter and energy. He argued that the propagation of signals through space was not merely a matter of localized disturbances but rather a manifestation of a more pervasive and continuous field. This perspective led him to propose that wireless communication could be achieved through a form of "radiant energy" that permeated the entire universe, rather than through discrete waves.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often emphasized the importance of the Earth's ionosphere and the atmosphere in facilitating wireless communication. He believed that the Earth itself played a crucial role in the transmission and reception of signals, which was not fully accounted for in the Hertzian wave theory. This view was encapsulated in his concept of "earth resonance," where he proposed that the Earth could be used as a resonant medium to transmit and receive signals over vast distances.

Despite these differences, it is important to note that Tesla's work laid the foundation for modern radio technology. While his ideas about the nature of wireless communication were innovative and sometimes controversial, they contributed significantly to the development of practical applications in radio and telecommunications.
