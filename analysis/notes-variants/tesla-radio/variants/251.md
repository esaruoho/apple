Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding what is now known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that the concept of radio waves propagating through space as electromagnetic waves was not fully aligned with his own understanding of wireless transmission.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments and theories involving resonant circuits and the Earth's ionosphere. He proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through the natural resonance of the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as the "earth's resonant circuit." This idea was different from the prevailing view at the time, which held that radio waves traveled through the air as electromagnetic waves.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory did not adequately explain the phenomena he observed and experienced in his experiments. He believed that the true nature of wireless transmission involved a more complex interaction between the transmitter, the receiver, and the Earth itself.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted or understood at the time. It wasn't until later that scientists and engineers developed a more comprehensive understanding of electromagnetic waves and their role in wireless communication.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in wireless communication. Tesla's work laid the groundwork for many aspects of modern radio technology, including the use of resonant circuits and the exploitation of natural resonances in the Earth-ionosphere system. However, the practical implementation of wireless communication relies heavily on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air, as described by Hertz's theory.
