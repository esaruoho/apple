Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to what he called "radiant energy" or "ether," rather than just electromagnetic waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could travel through the Earth's ionosphere and the ground, which he referred to as a "wireless earth." This idea was based on his observations and experiments with resonant circuits and his belief in the existence of a universal ether that could carry energy without the need for a physical medium like wires.

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time was that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space without a medium, a concept that contradicted the classical understanding of physics but was supported by experimental evidence. Tesla's ideas were often seen as controversial and ahead of their time, and they were not widely accepted until later developments in technology and science confirmed some of his theories.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about wireless transmission were innovative and influential, the modern understanding of radio waves and wireless communication is based on the principles of electromagnetic radiation, as described by James Clerk Maxwell and experimentally verified by Hertz. However, Tesla's contributions to the field, including his work on resonant circuits and his demonstrations of wireless power transmission, have had a lasting impact on the development of wireless technology.
