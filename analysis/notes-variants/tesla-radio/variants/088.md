Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's concerns stemmed from several aspects:

Wireless Transmission: Tesla argued that his methods of wireless transmission were fundamentally different from what Hertzian waves implied. He believed that his systems could transmit signals without the need for a line-of-sight or direct path between the transmitter and receiver, which was a key limitation of Hertzian waves at the time.

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in his wireless systems. He believed that by tuning the frequency of the transmitter and receiver to resonate with each other, he could achieve more efficient and longer-range communication. This concept was not fully captured by the Hertzian wave theory, which focused more on the propagation of waves through space.

Energy Distribution: Tesla also contended that his methods allowed for the distribution of energy over vast distances without significant loss, which was a challenge with the existing Hertzian wave theory. He believed that his systems could transmit energy in a manner that was more efficient and less dependent on physical infrastructure.

Interference and Noise: Tesla was concerned about the interference and noise that could affect Hertzian waves, especially in crowded urban environments. He believed that his methods could mitigate these issues, allowing for clearer and more reliable communication.

Despite these claims, it is important to note that Tesla's ideas were often ahead of their time and not fully understood by his contemporaries. The development of radio technology and the understanding of electromagnetic waves continued to evolve, eventually incorporating many of Tesla's concepts and overcoming some of the limitations he identified.

Today, we recognize that both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions were crucial to the development of modern radio and telecommunications. While Tesla's methods and theories were innovative and influential, they were also complex and sometimes difficult to implement with the technology available during his lifetime.
