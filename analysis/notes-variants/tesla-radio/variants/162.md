Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being proposed by the prevailing scientific community at the time. He argued that the idea of transmitting signals through the air as waves was too indirect and inefficient for practical applications. Instead, Tesla advocated for a system where the energy was directly coupled between the transmitter and receiver without the need for a medium like air or space.

One of Tesla's key points was that the wireless transmission of information should be possible over long distances without the need for a line-of-sight path, as would be required for radio waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor, allowing for the transmission of signals through the ground and the atmosphere. This concept is now known as Earth resonance or Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to demonstrate this principle on a large scale.

Despite Tesla's innovative ideas and demonstrations, such as his famous performances of wireless lighting and communication, the scientific community at the time did not fully accept his theories. The widespread adoption of radio technology was based more on the work of Guglielmo Marconi, who used radio waves for long-distance communication, although he acknowledged Tesla's contributions.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in modern wireless communication. Tesla's work laid the groundwork for many aspects of wireless technology, including the use of resonant circuits and the concept of earth-ionosphere coupling, while Hertz's discoveries provided the theoretical foundation for the propagation of electromagnetic waves.
