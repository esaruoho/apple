Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that there was a "wireless" or "etheric" medium that could transmit energy without the need for physical conductors. This idea was based on his observations and experiments with resonant circuits and his belief in the existence of a universal ether that could carry electrical energy.

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time was that radio waves could travel through space without a physical medium, which was a significant departure from the earlier theories of sound and light waves that required a medium like air or water. Tesla's skepticism towards this view was partly due to his desire to find a more efficient and less energy-intensive method of transmitting information and power wirelessly.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also ahead of their time in many respects. The technology and understanding of physics necessary to fully realize his vision did not exist during his lifetime. However, his work laid the groundwork for future developments in wireless communication and continues to inspire researchers and engineers today.
