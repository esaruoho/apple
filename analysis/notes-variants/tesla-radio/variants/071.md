Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves, as described by Heinrich Hertz, did not fully capture the essence of what he considered to be true wireless transmission.

Tesla's views were rooted in his experimental work and theoretical insights. He conducted extensive experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and developed devices such as the Tesla coil, which allowed for the generation of high-voltage, high-frequency currents. Tesla argued that these high-frequency currents could propagate through the Earth and air without the need for a line-of-sight transmission medium, which was the prevailing understanding at the time based on Hertz's work.

In contrast, Hertzian waves, or electromagnetic waves, were understood to require a direct path between the transmitter and receiver, similar to how sound waves travel. Tesla contended that this model was incomplete and that there was more to wireless communication than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary and ahead of their time. His work laid the foundation for modern radio technology, but his theories about the nature of wireless transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime. It wasn't until later that other scientists and engineers built upon Tesla's work to develop more comprehensive models of electromagnetic wave propagation.

It's important to note that while Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, the full understanding of electromagnetic waves and their behavior in various media came later, through the work of many scientists and the development of quantum mechanics and other advanced fields of physics.
