Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as localized disturbances in the electromagnetic field, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's view was that wireless transmission involved a more fundamental interaction with the Earth's ionosphere and the atmosphere, rather than just localized disturbances. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the Earth's natural resonant frequencies. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower project."

In his writings and public statements, Tesla argued that the theoretical framework of Hertzian waves was insufficient for explaining the long-range and efficient wireless communication that he demonstrated. He believed that the true nature of wireless transmission involved a form of energy propagation that extended beyond the simple wave-like behavior described by Hertz.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about earth resonance and wireless transmission were innovative and have been subject to modern scientific investigation, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. The prevailing scientific understanding at the time, and even today, is based on the electromagnetic radiation theory as developed by James Clerk Maxwell and later confirmed by experiments such as those conducted by Hertz.

Despite the controversy and differing views, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant and have had lasting impacts on technology. His work laid the groundwork for many of the principles used in modern radio and telecommunications systems.
