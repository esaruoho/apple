Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication could be achieved through a different mechanism, one that involved a more direct and efficient method of transmitting energy without relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves over long distances. He proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through the Earth itself, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In his patents and writings, Tesla described methods for wireless transmission that involved creating resonant circuits with the Earth and the ionosphere, which he believed would allow for more efficient and longer-range communication. He argued that the traditional Hertzian wave theory, which relied on the transmission of electromagnetic waves through the air, was less effective and more prone to interference.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted at the time due to the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. It wasn't until later that advancements in technology and further research confirmed some aspects of Tesla's theories, particularly in the realm of very low frequency (VLF) and extremely low frequency (ELF) communications.

Today, while we understand that electromagnetic waves play a crucial role in wireless communication, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the development of new technologies.
