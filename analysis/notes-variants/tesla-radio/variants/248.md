Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others, such as Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that his system of wireless transmission was fundamentally different from what was being described by the Hertzian wave theory.

Tesla's conception of wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method of transmitting information and energy through the air without the need for a physical medium like wires. He argued that the Hertzian wave theory, which describes electromagnetic waves propagating through space, did not fully capture the essence of his wireless communication and power transmission systems.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the Earth's ionosphere in his wireless transmission methods. He believed that these resonant systems could be tuned to specific frequencies, allowing for more efficient and targeted transmission of signals and energy. This approach contrasted with the Hertzian wave theory, which focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves over distances without the need for a resonant circuit or specific tuning.

Tesla's claims were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, but modern research has shown that some of his ideas about resonant systems and wireless transmission have merit. For example, the concept of resonant antennas and the use of the Earth's ionosphere as a conductor for radio waves are now well-established principles in radio engineering.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and sometimes ahead of their time, they also faced challenges in terms of scientific validation and practical implementation. Nonetheless, his contributions to the field of wireless communication and electrical engineering remain significant and continue to influence modern technology.
