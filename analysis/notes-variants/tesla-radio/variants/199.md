Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a mere propagation of waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonance principle." According to Tesla, this method would allow for more efficient and longer-range transmission without the need for high-frequency oscillations that were characteristic of the Hertzian wave theory.

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time was that radio waves required a line-of-sight transmission and could not travel around obstacles or through the ground effectively. Tesla argued that this approach was limiting and did not fully exploit the potential of wireless communication.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas about earth resonance were innovative and contributed to the development of modern radio technology, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. The practical implementation of radio communication largely relied on the Hertzian wave theory until later developments confirmed some of Tesla's concepts.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories have their merits and applications. Modern radio technology combines elements from both approaches, using antennas to transmit and receive signals while also taking advantage of the Earth's conductive properties for certain types of communication.
