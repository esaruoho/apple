Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's experiments and findings. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or radio waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through the Earth itself and the ionosphere, rather than through the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He proposed that the Earth could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonant antenna."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was gaining widespread acceptance at the time, suggested that radio waves could travel through the air without needing a medium. This theory was supported by experimental evidence and became the foundation for much of early radio technology.

Tesla's claims that the Hertzian wave theory was inimical to the proper understanding of wireless processes were based on his belief that it oversimplified the nature of wireless communication. He argued that the theory did not account for the resonant properties of the Earth and the ionosphere, which he believed were crucial for efficient wireless transmission.

Despite these differences, both Tesla and Hertz contributed significantly to the development of radio technology. Tesla's work laid the groundwork for many modern applications of wireless communication, while Hertz's experiments provided the theoretical basis for understanding how electromagnetic waves propagate.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas about earth resonance and resonant antennas were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, they have since been validated by modern research, particularly in the field of long-range wireless communication and the use of the Earth's natural resonances for signal transmission.
