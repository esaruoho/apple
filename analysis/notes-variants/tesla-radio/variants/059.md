Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments and theories about the propagation of electrical currents through the Earth and the ionosphere. He proposed that wireless transmission could be achieved through the ground or the ionosphere itself, rather than through the air via electromagnetic waves. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In his view, the traditional Hertzian wave theory was too focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air, which he believed was inefficient and impractical for long-distance communication. Tesla argued that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it, effectively creating a global network of wireless communication.

Some key points of Tesla's critique include:

Efficiency: Tesla believed that transmitting signals through the Earth would be more efficient and less susceptible to interference compared to sending signals through the air.
Range: He thought that the range of transmission could be extended significantly by utilizing the Earth's natural properties.
Interference: Tesla was concerned about the interference that could occur with air-based transmission methods, especially in crowded urban areas.

Despite these beliefs, Tesla's ideas were not widely accepted during his lifetime. The practical implementation of radio technology largely followed the principles of Hertzian waves, as developed further by Guglielmo Marconi and others. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to be studied and debated in the scientific community.
