Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory, in explaining his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of energy and information could be achieved through aether or some form of non-radiative field, rather than through the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla's views were rooted in his experimental work and theoretical insights. He conducted extensive experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and developed devices such as the Tesla coil, which demonstrated the generation and manipulation of high-voltage, high-frequency currents. Tesla argued that these high-frequency currents could be used for wireless communication without relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves over long distances.

In his writings and public lectures, Tesla criticized what he saw as the oversimplification of the wireless process by the Hertzian wave theory. He believed that the theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he understood it. Tesla's skepticism towards the Hertzian wave theory was partly due to his belief that it did not account for the non-radiative aspects of wireless transmission that he observed in his experiments.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not widely accepted at the time. The scientific community eventually came to accept the Hertzian wave theory as a valid explanation for wireless communication, although Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant. His work laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers today.
