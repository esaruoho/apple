Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of his wireless transmission methods and that it could be misleading.

Tesla's work on radio communication was groundbreaking, but it was based on a different conceptual framework than the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. He emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the ability of antennas to resonate with specific frequencies, rather than simply transmitting waves through space.

In his view, the Hertzian wave theory focused too much on the propagation of electromagnetic waves as a continuous stream, without adequately considering the role of resonance and the selective nature of antennas. Tesla argued that the theory did not account for the fact that antennas could selectively receive and transmit signals at certain frequencies, which was crucial for efficient wireless communication.

This difference in perspective led to some tension between Tesla and other scientists of his time who were more aligned with the Hertzian wave theory. However, it is important to note that Tesla's contributions to radio technology were significant, even if they were not always recognized or understood within the context of the prevailing scientific theories of the day.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and the Hertzian wave theories play important roles in our understanding of radio communication. Tesla's emphasis on resonance and selective reception has influenced modern antenna design, while the Hertzian wave theory provides a fundamental framework for understanding electromagnetic wave propagation.
