Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to aether or some form of invisible matter that permeated space. He proposed that wireless signals could travel through the Earth and air without the need for a line-of-sight transmission, as suggested by the then-popular Hertzian wave theory.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was too simplistic and did not account for the true nature of wireless communication. He emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the ability of the Earth to act as a conductor and transmitter of electrical energy. Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and contributed significantly to the development of modern wireless technology, although they were not widely accepted during his lifetime.

It's worth noting that while Tesla's theories were innovative and influential, the scientific community at the time was still developing a full understanding of electromagnetic phenomena. The Hertzian wave theory eventually became the foundation for much of modern radio and telecommunications, but Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant and continue to inspire researchers and engineers today.
