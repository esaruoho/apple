Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or what we now call radio waves, did not fully capture his vision of wireless transmission.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless communication could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that there was a "wireless" or "etheric" medium that could transmit energy and information without the need for physical conductors. This idea was based on his observations and experiments, particularly those involving resonant circuits and the Earth's ionosphere.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which required antennas and other conductive elements to transmit and receive signals. Tesla argued that this approach was limited and did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and experimented with.

Some key points of Tesla's critique include:

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in wireless transmission. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a resonant medium, allowing for the transmission of energy and information over vast distances without the need for physical conductors.

Earth's Ionosphere: Tesla recognized the role of the Earth's ionosphere in reflecting and transmitting radio waves. He developed methods to use the ionosphere as a natural conductor for wireless communication.

Long-distance Communication: Tesla claimed that his methods allowed for long-distance communication without the need for complex apparatuses like those required by Hertzian wave theory. He demonstrated this with his Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to establish a global network of wireless communication.

Interference and Noise: Tesla also criticized the Hertzian wave theory for its susceptibility to interference and noise, which he believed could be minimized or eliminated through his methods.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and often misunderstood during his lifetime. Modern technology has largely validated the practical applications of Hertzian waves for wireless communication, but Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, particularly in the realm of resonant circuits and the exploration of the Earth's ionosphere as a medium for wireless transmission.
