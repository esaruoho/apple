Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his work on wireless technology, particularly his experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and his claims of having developed a system for wireless transmission of power and information without wires. He argued that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves as propagating through space in a manner similar to sound waves or ripples in water was inadequate for explaining the phenomena he observed.

In his view, the wireless transmission of energy and information involved a more direct and efficient method than simply relying on the propagation of waves. Tesla suggested that his methods involved a form of resonance between the transmitter and receiver, which allowed for the transfer of energy over long distances without the need for the waves to travel through the air in the conventional sense.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they also diverged from the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. Modern physics has since refined our understanding of electromagnetic radiation and wireless communication, incorporating both wave theory and quantum mechanics. However, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire discussions about the nature of wireless technology.
