Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of his conception of wireless communication and energy transmission.

Tesla's work on radio and wireless technology was groundbreaking, but he had significant disagreements with the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. The Hertzian wave theory, named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, was based on the idea that these waves were propagated through space in a manner similar to light or sound waves.

Tesla argued that the concept of "waves" traveling through space was an oversimplification and that the true nature of wireless communication involved a more direct form of energy transfer. He believed that the energy could be transmitted without the need for a medium like air or a vacuum, and that the waves were not simply propagating through space but were instead being directed and focused by the transmitter and receiver.

One of Tesla's key points was that the Hertzian wave theory did not account for the phenomenon of resonance, which Tesla believed was crucial to the efficient transmission and reception of wireless signals. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and resonator, allowing for the transmission of energy over vast distances without the need for a physical medium.

Despite these differences, it is important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were immense. His patents and demonstrations laid the groundwork for modern radio and wireless technology. However, his ideas about the nature of wireless communication were not widely accepted during his lifetime and continue to be subjects of debate among historians and scientists today.

In summary, while Tesla acknowledged the importance of the Hertzian wave theory, he believed it was incomplete and did not fully describe the mechanisms of wireless communication as he understood them.
