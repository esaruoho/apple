Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his own experiments and theories. He proposed that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that he called "radiant energy," which was different from the electromagnetic waves described by Hertz. Tesla believed that this radiant energy could travel through the Earth itself, a concept now known as Earth resonance or longitudinal waves, rather than through the air as transverse waves.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was incomplete and that it did not account for the true nature of wireless communication. He suggested that the propagation of signals could be more efficient if they were transmitted through the Earth's natural resonances rather than through the air. This idea was revolutionary at the time and contributed to the development of what is now known as ground wave propagation.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The prevailing scientific consensus at the time was based on the Hertzian wave theory, which eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in wireless communication. Modern radio technology utilizes both transverse waves (electromagnetic waves) and ground waves (longitudinal waves) to transmit signals effectively.
