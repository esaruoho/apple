Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of energy and information could be achieved through a different mechanism than what was being described by the then-popular Hertzian wave theory.

Tesla's views were based on his own experiments and theories, particularly his concept of "resonance" and "radiant energy." He argued that the transmission of energy and information could be more effectively achieved through resonant circuits rather than through the propagation of waves. This idea was rooted in his belief that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it without the need for the air or space to carry the waves.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, as the scientific community at the time was heavily influenced by the Hertzian wave theory. However, modern research has shown that Tesla's ideas about resonance and the Earth's ionosphere as a medium for wireless communication have some validity. For example, the concept of using the Earth's ionosphere as a reflector for radio waves (a technique known as skywave propagation) is now widely accepted and used in radio communications.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted during his time. The development of practical radio communication systems did not fully align with Tesla's theories but instead relied more heavily on the principles of electromagnetic waves as described by Hertz and later refined by others.
