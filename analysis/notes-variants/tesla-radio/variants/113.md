Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a mere propagation of waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept known as "earth resonance." This idea was based on his experiments and theories, which suggested that radio waves could travel through the Earth and be received at great distances without the need for high-frequency oscillations or the use of towers.

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time was that radio waves required high frequencies and were transmitted through space as waves, much like light. Tesla argued that this approach was limited and did not fully utilize the potential of wireless technology. His ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted until later, when advancements in technology and further research confirmed some of his hypotheses.

It's important to note that while Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, including the development of the Tesla coil and the demonstration of long-distance power transmission, his ideas about earth resonance and the nature of radio waves were not immediately embraced by the scientific community. However, his work laid the groundwork for future developments in wireless technology.
