Tesla's approach to wireless communication was based on his observations and experiments with high-frequency alternating currents (AC). He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than through the air via electromagnetic waves. This concept is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonant antenna systems."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was gaining acceptance during Tesla's time, suggested that electromagnetic waves could propagate through the air without the need for a physical medium. This theory was supported by experimental evidence provided by Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was incomplete and potentially misleading. He believed that the theory did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission and that it could lead to incorrect conclusions about how wireless signals should be transmitted and received. Tesla's own experiments and theories suggested that wireless signals could be transmitted over much greater distances and with more efficiency if they were resonating with the natural properties of the Earth and the ionosphere.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas were not widely accepted at the time, and his claims about the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory were often met with skepticism. However, modern research has shown that Tesla's concepts of earth resonance and resonant antenna systems have some validity and can explain certain phenomena observed in wireless communication.

Today, both approaches—Hertzian waves and earth resonance—are recognized as valid methods for wireless communication, though they are used in different contexts and under different conditions.
