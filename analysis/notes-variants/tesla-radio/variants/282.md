Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the nature of radio waves were quite different from those of Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with proving the existence of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (electromagnetic waves) as discrete particles or waves propagating through space was not fully capturing the essence of wireless communication. He argued that the true mechanism of wireless transmission involved a form of energy that could travel through the Earth itself, rather than through the air as Hertzian waves suggested. This idea is sometimes referred to as "Earth resonance" or "resonant antenna systems."

In his patents and public statements, Tesla described a system where the Earth acts as a conductor and resonates at specific frequencies, allowing for the transmission of information without the need for the air to carry the signal. This concept was revolutionary at the time and was based on his observations and experiments with high-frequency currents and resonant circuits.

Tesla's views on this matter were controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that the Earth can indeed resonate at certain frequencies, which aligns with some aspects of Tesla's theories. Nonetheless, the practical implementation of radio technology largely followed the principles of Hertzian waves, leading to the development of the radio as we know it today.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they did not replace the established theory of electromagnetic radiation. Instead, they contributed to a broader understanding of how wireless communication could be achieved, and they continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the field of wireless technology.
