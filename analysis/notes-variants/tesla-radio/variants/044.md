Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as short-range, line-of-sight radio waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas about wireless transmission were rooted in his belief that energy could be transmitted through the Earth and the ionosphere without the need for a direct line of sight or physical antennas. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that energy could be transmitted through it, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the transmission of electromagnetic waves through space, which required line-of-sight propagation and physical antennas to receive and transmit signals. Tesla argued that this approach was limited and did not fully exploit the potential of wireless communication.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, but they have since gained recognition as precursors to modern wireless technology, particularly in the development of long-distance radio transmission and the understanding of the ionosphere's role in radio wave propagation.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, the practical implementation of wireless communication has largely relied on the principles of electromagnetic radiation as described by Hertz and further developed by other scientists and engineers.
