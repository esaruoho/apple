Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his own experimental and theoretical work, particularly his experiments with high-frequency currents and resonant circuits. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which described the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, was too narrow and did not account for the true nature of wireless transmission.

In Tesla's view, wireless communication involved a more direct and efficient method of transmitting energy and information without relying solely on the propagation of waves through the air. He emphasized the importance of resonant systems and the ability to transmit energy over long distances without the need for a line-of-sight or physical medium.

While Tesla's claims were met with skepticism at the time, modern research has shown that his ideas about resonant systems and wireless transmission have significant relevance. For example, the development of resonant coupling in wireless charging technologies and the use of resonant antennas in various applications can be seen as extensions of Tesla's concepts.

It's important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were groundbreaking, even if they were not fully recognized during his lifetime. His patents and demonstrations laid the groundwork for much of the technology we use today, including radio and wireless communication systems.
