Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that his own discoveries and inventions, particularly those related to wireless transmission, were fundamentally different from what he saw as the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory.

Tesla's claims and criticisms can be summarized as follows:

Wireless Transmission vs. Radio Waves: Tesla argued that his methods of wireless transmission were based on a more comprehensive understanding of the principles of electromagnetism. He believed that his systems could transmit information without the need for a line-of-sight or physical medium, which he saw as a more advanced form of communication than the radio waves described by Hertz.

Coherence and Directionality: Tesla criticized the idea that radio waves were coherent and directional, suggesting instead that his systems could transmit signals over much greater distances and in a more efficient manner. He believed that his methods could achieve point-to-point communication without the need for a direct line of sight, which was a significant departure from the prevailing understanding of radio waves at the time.

Energy Distribution: Tesla also argued that his methods involved a different approach to energy distribution. He believed that his systems could distribute electrical energy more efficiently and with less loss over long distances, which he saw as an advantage over the Hertzian wave theory.

Infrasound and Earth's Ionosphere: Tesla proposed that his wireless systems could utilize infrasound (very low-frequency sound waves) and the ionosphere (the upper part of the Earth's atmosphere) to transmit signals. This was seen as a more natural and efficient way of transmitting information compared to the radio waves described by Hertz.

Patent Disputes and Scientific Debate: Tesla's claims often led to patent disputes and scientific debates. His ideas were sometimes seen as conflicting with the established theories of the time, leading to a divide between Tesla's followers and those who adhered strictly to the Hertzian wave theory.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and groundbreaking, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The scientific community at the time was still developing a full understanding of electromagnetic phenomena, and Tesla's theories and inventions contributed significantly to this ongoing exploration.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions are integral to our modern understanding of wireless communication. Tesla's work laid the groundwork for many of the technologies we use today, including radio, television, and wireless internet.
