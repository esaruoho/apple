Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being proposed by the prevailing theories of the time. He argued that the transmission of signals through the air was not simply a matter of radiating energy in all directions, but rather involved a more focused and directed form of energy transfer.

In his experiments and writings, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the idea of "resonance" between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that this resonance was key to achieving efficient and long-range wireless communication without the need for vast amounts of power or the broad emission of energy that the Hertzian wave theory suggested.

Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and contributed significantly to the development of modern radio technology. However, at the time, many scientists and engineers were still working within the framework of Hertzian waves, which led to some tension between Tesla and the scientific community.

It's worth noting that while Tesla's views on the nature of wireless communication were innovative and influential, they were also controversial. The scientific consensus eventually aligned with the principles of electromagnetic radiation, although Tesla's contributions to the field remain highly respected.
