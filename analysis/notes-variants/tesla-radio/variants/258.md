Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication should be based on a system of resonant circuits rather than the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through the air was an oversimplification and that the true mechanism involved resonating with the Earth itself.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described his wireless systems as involving the transmission of energy through the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than through the air. This approach was based on his experiments with high-frequency currents and resonant circuits, which he believed could transmit information without the need for a physical medium like waves.

While modern science has largely validated the concept of electromagnetic waves as the basis for wireless communication, Tesla's ideas have also influenced the development of resonant circuit theory and other aspects of electrical engineering. His work continues to be studied and appreciated for its innovative contributions to the field of wireless technology.
