Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's experiments and findings. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or radio waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth's natural resonant frequency, a concept he referred to as "earth resonance."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the transmission of electromagnetic waves through space, which was later developed into radio technology. Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and that it did not account for the full potential of wireless communication.

Some key points of Tesla's critique include:

Earth Resonance: Tesla believed that the Earth could be used as a conductor for wireless signals, and that these signals could travel through the Earth's natural resonant frequency. This idea was different from the Hertzian wave theory, which relied solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air.

Wireless Power Transmission: Tesla also believed that it was possible to transmit power wirelessly, which was a concept that went beyond simple communication. He demonstrated this with his Wardenclyffe Tower project, where he aimed to transmit electrical energy across distances without wires.

Interference and Reception: Tesla argued that the reception of wireless signals could be improved by using the Earth as a conductor, reducing interference and improving signal strength.

Despite these differences, it's important to note that Tesla's work laid the foundation for modern radio technology. While his theories about earth resonance and wireless power transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime, they have been revisited and studied in recent years, particularly in the context of new technologies like wireless charging and resonant coupling.

Today, while we use the term "radio waves" to describe the electromagnetic waves used in wireless communication, the principles underlying Tesla's ideas continue to inspire research in fields such as wireless power transmission and advanced antenna design.
