Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves as understood at the time did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that there was a subtle, invisible medium that could carry information without the need for physical waves as described by Hertz. This medium, which Tesla sometimes referred to as a "ether," was thought to be a more efficient and direct means of transmitting energy and information.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves as discrete packets of energy (Hertzian waves) was overly simplistic and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication. He suggested that the true mechanism of wireless transmission involved a more continuous and pervasive interaction with this hypothetical medium.

It's important to note that Tesla's views were ahead of his time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. The scientific community at the turn of the 20th century generally supported the wave theory of electromagnetic radiation, and it wasn't until later that many of Tesla's ideas about wireless transmission became more widely recognized and understood.

Today, we understand that Tesla's concepts align more closely with modern theories of electromagnetic fields and wireless communication, although the exact nature of the medium he referred to remains speculative. His contributions to the field of wireless technology continue to be celebrated, even if some of his specific theories have not been fully validated by contemporary science.
