Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to what he called "radiant energy" or "ether," rather than just electromagnetic waves. He argued that the propagation of signals through space was not simply a matter of waves traveling through a vacuum but involved a more complex interaction with the surrounding environment.

Some key points of Tesla's critique include:

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in wireless transmission. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a resonant medium for wireless signals, which would allow for much longer-range communication without the need for high-frequency waves. This idea was based on his experiments with tuned circuits and resonant tanks.

Frequency: Tesla contended that using very low frequencies (VLF) could achieve better results for long-distance communication. He believed that higher frequencies, as proposed by others, were less effective and potentially harmful.

Interference: Tesla was concerned about the interference that high-frequency waves might cause, especially in the atmosphere. He felt that lower frequencies would be less susceptible to such interference.

Energy Efficiency: Tesla argued that his methods were more efficient in terms of energy use. He believed that his systems could transmit signals over vast distances with less power compared to those relying solely on high-frequency waves.

Despite these beliefs, Tesla's ideas were not widely accepted at the time. The scientific community largely embraced the Hertzian wave theory, which laid the foundation for modern radio technology. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers to this day.

It's worth noting that Tesla's theories and inventions have been re-evaluated in recent years, and some aspects of his work are now seen as prescient, particularly in the realm of wireless communication and the properties of the Earth's ionosphere.
