Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (radio waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through aetheric or longitudinal waves, which he thought were different from the transverse waves described by Hertz. He argued that the transverse waves (Hertzian waves) were not sufficient for long-distance wireless communication and that they required a medium to propagate, which contradicted his ideas about wireless transmission being possible without a medium.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the Hertzian wave theory, stating that it was incomplete and that it did not accurately describe the phenomena of wireless communication. He proposed that wireless signals could travel through the Earth itself and that the atmosphere could act as a conductor for these signals, rather than relying solely on air as a medium.

Despite these criticisms, Hertzian waves eventually became the accepted scientific basis for radio communication, and Tesla's ideas about longitudinal waves remained speculative and largely unproven during his lifetime. However, his contributions to the field of wireless communication are still recognized today, and his work laid the groundwork for many modern technologies.
