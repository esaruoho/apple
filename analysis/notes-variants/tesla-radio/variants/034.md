Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and disagreement with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the nature of radio waves were quite different from what was being proposed by other scientists of the era, such as Heinrich Hertz.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation as understood at the time did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication. He argued that the idea of sending signals through the air as waves was insufficient for achieving true wireless transmission. Tesla's vision involved a more direct form of energy transmission, which he referred to as "wireless" but which differed significantly from the electromagnetic wave theory.

In particular, Tesla contended that the use of high-frequency currents could achieve wireless communication without the need for a physical medium like air or space. He believed that the Earth itself could be used as a conductor for these currents, and that this method would be more efficient and effective than relying solely on electromagnetic waves.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime due to the limitations of the scientific understanding and experimental capabilities of the time. It wasn't until later that many of Tesla's concepts were validated and incorporated into modern wireless technology.

It's important to note that Tesla's views on the nature of radio waves were part of a broader debate in the late 19th and early 20th centuries about the mechanisms of wireless communication. The electromagnetic wave theory eventually became the standard model, but Tesla's contributions and criticisms remain significant in the history of science and technology.
