Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his own experiments and theories regarding wireless transmission. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept known as "earth resonance." This idea was based on his observations that certain frequencies could resonate with the Earth, allowing for the transmission of information without the need for a line-of-sight or physical medium like air or space.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on electromagnetic waves propagating through space. Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete because it did not account for the full potential of wireless communication. He believed that the Earth could act as a conductor and a resonant medium, enabling the transmission of signals over vast distances without the need for towers or antennas.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that earth resonance and other forms of non-Hertzian wave propagation do play a role in wireless communication, validating some aspects of Tesla's theories.

Overall, Tesla's claims reflect his innovative thinking and his desire to push the boundaries of what was then understood about wireless technology. His contributions to the field continue to influence discussions on wireless communication today.
