Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as being transmitted through space without any physical medium, did not align with his understanding of wireless transmission.

Tesla's view was that wireless communication involved a more direct and efficient method than simply relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, using the Earth as a conductor. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "Tesla coil" technology, although it is important to note that Tesla's theories and methods have been subject to much debate and scrutiny over the years.

Tesla's claims and theories were innovative for his time but have since been largely superseded by modern understandings of radio wave propagation and wireless communication. However, his work laid the groundwork for many of the technologies we use today, including radio, television, and radar.
