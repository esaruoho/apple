Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of how wireless transmission could work.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that allowed for the propagation of energy without the need for a physical medium like air or space. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could travel through the Earth's ionosphere and the ground, a concept now known as Earth-ionospheric wave propagation.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on electromagnetic waves propagating through free space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete explanation for long-distance wireless communication. Tesla argued that the true nature of wireless transmission involved a more complex interaction between the transmitter, receiver, and the Earth's conductive properties.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted at the time. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved to include both the Earth-ionospheric path and the free-space propagation described by Hertzian waves, among other mechanisms.

Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are still celebrated today, and his skepticism towards certain aspects of the Hertzian wave theory reflects his deep understanding of the subject and his desire to push the boundaries of scientific knowledge.
