Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the inappropriateness of the Hertzian wave theory are well-documented.

Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of information and energy could be achieved through a different mechanism than what was then understood as electromagnetic waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the ground or the ionosphere without the need for a line-of-sight transmission medium.

The Hertzian wave theory, named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, was based on the idea that these waves could propagate through space and be detected at a distance. However, Tesla argued that this theory did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He believed that the concept of "radio waves" as mere disturbances in the air was insufficient to explain the phenomena he observed and experienced.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary and ahead of their time. His experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and his development of the Tesla coil allowed him to demonstrate wireless transmission of power and information. However, his theories and methods were not widely accepted during his lifetime due to the limitations of technology and the scientific understanding of the time.

It's important to note that while Tesla's views on the nature of wireless transmission were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not universally accepted. The modern understanding of radio waves and wireless communication is a result of ongoing research and experimentation, building upon the foundational work of scientists like Hertz and Tesla himself.
