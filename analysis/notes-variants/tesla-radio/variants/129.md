Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a simple radiating field. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth's natural resonant frequency, a concept he referred to as "earth resonance." This idea was based on his observations and experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and his belief in the existence of a "worldwide web" of energy that could be harnessed for communication and power distribution.

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time was that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space without a physical medium, a concept that is now well-established in physics. Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that Earth's ionosphere and the ground can indeed play a role in the propagation of electromagnetic waves, supporting some aspects of Tesla's theories.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted or understood in his era. The scientific community at the time was still grappling with the implications of Maxwell's equations and the nature of electromagnetic radiation.
