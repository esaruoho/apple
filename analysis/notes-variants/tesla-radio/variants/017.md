Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as short-range, line-of-sight signals, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved over much greater distances than what Hertzian waves seemed to allow. He argued that the Earth itself played a crucial role in wireless transmission, a concept now known as "Earth resonance" or "Tesla effect." According to Tesla, the Earth could act as a conductor for these signals, allowing them to travel vast distances without requiring line-of-sight transmission.

In his patents and public demonstrations, Tesla demonstrated the ability to transmit signals over long distances using resonant circuits, which he believed were more efficient and effective than the Hertzian wave model. He claimed that his methods allowed for wireless communication and power transmission without the need for extensive infrastructure, such as towers and antennas.

However, it's important to note that Tesla's ideas about Earth resonance and wireless transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime. The scientific community at the time generally supported the Hertzian wave theory, which eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories have their merits and limitations. While Hertzian waves are indeed fundamental to radio technology, Earth resonance is also a valid phenomenon that can enhance wireless signal propagation under certain conditions. Tesla's contributions to wireless technology remain significant, even if his specific theories about Earth resonance are not universally accepted in the same form as he proposed them.
