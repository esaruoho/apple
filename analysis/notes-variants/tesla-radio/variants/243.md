Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the way Hertz and others were interpreting and using radio waves did not fully capture the potential of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's approach to wireless technology was rooted in his belief in a more efficient and natural method of transmitting information without the need for physical conductors. He argued that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation as understood at the time was too limited and did not account for the full spectrum of possibilities in wireless communication.

Some key points of Tesla's critique include:

Wireless Transmission Without Conductors: Tesla proposed that wireless transmission could be achieved without the need for physical conductors like wires. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor, allowing for the transmission of signals over vast distances without the use of antennas or other physical structures.

Resonance and Sympathetic Vibrations: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance and sympathetic vibrations in his wireless systems. He believed that by tuning devices to resonate at specific frequencies, they could communicate effectively over long distances. This concept was different from the prevailing view that radio waves required direct line-of-sight or physical antennas to transmit and receive signals.

Interference and Inefficiency: Tesla criticized what he saw as the inefficiencies and potential for interference inherent in the Hertzian wave theory. He believed that the way radio waves were being used at the time led to significant losses and interference, which hindered the practical application of wireless communication.

Innovation and Patents: Tesla patented many of his inventions related to wireless technology, including the Tesla coil, which played a crucial role in his experiments with high-frequency currents and wireless transmission. His patents often challenged existing theories and practices, leading to disputes with other scientists and inventors.

While Tesla's ideas were groundbreaking and have influenced modern wireless technology, it is important to note that the Hertzian wave theory eventually became the foundation for much of our current understanding of radio waves and wireless communication. Modern technologies such as Wi-Fi, cellular networks, and satellite communications all rely on principles that are fundamentally based on the work of Hertz and others, even though they incorporate many of Tesla's innovative concepts.

Despite Tesla's criticisms, the Hertzian wave theory has proven to be a robust framework for understanding and utilizing radio waves, and it continues to be the basis for most practical applications of wireless communication today.
