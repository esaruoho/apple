Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as being transmitted through space without wires, did not accurately describe the way his systems worked.

Tesla's inventions and theories focused on the idea of "wireless transmission" or "radiation" in a broader sense, which included not just the propagation of electromagnetic waves but also the use of resonant circuits and the manipulation of electrical fields. He believed that the proper understanding of wireless communication required a more holistic approach that considered the resonance between transmitter and receiver, rather than simply the propagation of waves.

In his view, the Hertzian wave theory was too narrow and did not account for the phenomena of resonance and the ability to transmit energy over long distances without direct line-of-sight. Tesla's experiments and patents often involved resonant circuits and the use of high-frequency alternating currents, which he believed were key to achieving efficient and long-range wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial at the time and have been subject to much debate among historians and scientists. The development of radio and wireless communication ultimately relied on a combination of various theories and technologies, including those related to electromagnetic waves and resonance.
