Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of "waves" as described by Heinrich Hertz did not accurately represent the nature of wireless transmission.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless communication involved a more direct and continuous interaction between the transmitter and receiver, rather than the propagation of waves through space. He proposed that the Earth itself played a crucial role in the transmission and reception of wireless signals, a concept often referred to as "Earth Currents."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which was later confirmed by experiments and became a cornerstone of modern radio technology. Tesla's views were not widely accepted during his lifetime, but they have since gained some support among alternative theorists and have been explored in various forms of wireless technology beyond traditional radio waves.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed to the development of wireless technology, the Hertzian wave theory remains a fundamental part of our understanding of electromagnetic radiation and is still used in practical applications today.
