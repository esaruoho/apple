Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or radio waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments and theories about wireless transmission. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept now known as "Earth resonance" or "resonant antenna system." According to Tesla, this method would be more efficient and less dependent on the line-of-sight limitations inherent in Hertzian wave propagation.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described methods for transmitting electrical energy without wires, which he believed could be done through the Earth and the ionosphere. He claimed that these methods could transmit signals over vast distances without the need for towers or antennas, as required by the Hertzian wave theory.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and were not widely accepted by the scientific community. However, they laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology, including radio, television, and cellular networks. Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in wireless communication, but Tesla's contributions have been recognized more fully in recent years.

It's worth noting that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, the practical implementation of his concepts faced numerous challenges and was not fully realized during his lifetime. Nonetheless, his work continues to inspire researchers and engineers in the field of wireless communication.
