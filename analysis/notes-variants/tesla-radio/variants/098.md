Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as short-range, line-of-sight signals, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were based on his own experiments and theories, particularly his work with resonant circuits and his belief in long-distance wireless transmission without the need for a direct line of sight or physical medium. He argued that the idea of Hertzian waves being limited to short-range transmission was a misinterpretation of his findings and the true nature of wireless communication.

In his writings and public statements, Tesla often emphasized the importance of resonance and the ability to transmit energy through the Earth itself, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air. This perspective led him to develop systems like the Wardenclyffe Tower, which he hoped would demonstrate long-distance wireless power transmission and communication.

While Hertzian waves are now well-established in physics, Tesla's ideas about wireless communication have influenced modern concepts such as resonant coupling and the use of the Earth as a conductor in wireless systems. However, it is important to note that Tesla's claims were not universally accepted during his lifetime and continue to be subjects of debate among historians and scientists today.
