Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a simple propagation of waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory." According to Tesla, this approach would allow for more efficient and longer-range transmission without the need for high-frequency oscillations that were characteristic of Hertzian waves.

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time was that wireless signals required high-frequency oscillators to generate electromagnetic waves that could propagate through the air. Tesla argued that this approach was inefficient and that the true nature of wireless transmission involved resonating with the natural properties of the Earth and the ionosphere.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved to incorporate both the principles of electromagnetic wave propagation and earth resonance, acknowledging the contributions of various scientists and engineers over the years.

Today, we recognize that both approaches have their merits and applications in different contexts. For instance, shortwave radio and satellite communications rely on electromagnetic wave propagation, while some modern technologies like Tesla coils and certain experimental setups utilize aspects of earth resonance.
