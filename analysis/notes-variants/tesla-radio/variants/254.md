Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that he referred to as "earth currents" or "space currents." He argued that these currents were different from the electromagnetic waves described by Hertz and Maxwell. According to Tesla, the key to wireless transmission lay in the resonant properties of the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than in the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space.

In his experiments and writings, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that by properly tuning the frequency of the transmitter to match the natural resonant frequency of the receiving circuit, one could achieve efficient and long-range communication without relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves alone.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and contributed significantly to the development of modern radio technology. However, his theories were not widely accepted during his lifetime due to the established scientific consensus around Hertzian waves. It wasn't until later that many of Tesla's concepts were validated and incorporated into the broader understanding of wireless communication.

Today, while we acknowledge the contributions of both Tesla and Hertz to our understanding of electromagnetic waves, modern radio technology often combines elements of both approaches, using resonant circuits and electromagnetic waves to achieve efficient and reliable communication.
