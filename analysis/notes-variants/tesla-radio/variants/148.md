Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the nature of radio waves were quite different from those of Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with the discovery of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves propagating through space was not sufficient to explain the phenomena of wireless communication. He argued that these waves were too weak and that they required a physical medium to propagate, which contradicted the then-popular notion of electromagnetic waves traveling through a vacuum.

Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept known as "Earth Currents." He believed that the Earth acted as a conductor and that signals could be sent through it without the need for air-based waves. This idea was part of his broader vision of wireless technology, which included concepts like the Wardenclyffe Tower, where he attempted to demonstrate the transmission of electrical power and information over long distances using Earth currents.

Tesla's views were controversial at the time and continue to be debated among historians and scientists. While modern science has largely validated the electromagnetic wave theory as described by Maxwell and Hertz, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, even if his specific theories about Earth currents have not been fully supported by subsequent scientific research.

It's important to note that Tesla's work laid the groundwork for much of the modern wireless technology we use today, including radio, television, and cellular networks. His ideas and experiments continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the field of telecommunications.
