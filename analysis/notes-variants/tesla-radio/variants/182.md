Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the nature of radio waves were quite different from what was commonly accepted at the time.

Tesla believed that radio waves could be transmitted without the need for a line-of-sight or direct path between the transmitter and receiver, which was a fundamental concept in the then-popular Hertzian wave theory. According to this theory, radio waves required a clear path through space to propagate effectively, much like light does. Tesla argued that this view was limiting and did not fully capture the potential of wireless communication.

In his writings and public statements, Tesla often emphasized the idea of "space transmission" or "wireless transmission," suggesting that radio waves could travel through the Earth and air without the need for a direct line of sight. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor, allowing signals to travel around the globe.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments with resonant circuits and his belief in the existence of a "worldwide network" of conductors that could transmit and receive signals. This network, which he sometimes referred to as the "World Wireless System," was intended to enable global communication and information sharing without the need for physical wires.

While Tesla's ideas were revolutionary and ahead of their time, they were not widely accepted until after his death. It was only later that scientists and engineers began to understand and exploit the principles of resonance and the Earth's ionosphere, which allowed for long-distance radio wave propagation.

It's important to note that Tesla's criticisms of the Hertzian wave theory were part of a broader debate in the early 20th century about the nature of electromagnetic waves and their applications. Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in modern radio technology, but Tesla's contributions to the development of wireless communication remain significant.
