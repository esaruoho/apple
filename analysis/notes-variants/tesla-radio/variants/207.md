Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the nature of radio waves were quite different from those of Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with the discovery of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as requiring a line-of-sight propagation and were limited by the curvature of the Earth, did not fully capture the essence of what he called "wireless" or "radio" communication. He argued that his methods involved a more direct and efficient form of energy transmission that did not rely solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air.

In his view, the key to effective wireless communication lay in the use of resonant circuits and the manipulation of the Earth's natural resonances. Tesla proposed that by creating resonant circuits that matched the natural frequencies of the Earth and the ionosphere, one could transmit signals without the need for line-of-sight or the limitations imposed by the curvature of the Earth. This approach was fundamentally different from the Hertzian wave theory, which focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space.

Tesla's claims and theories were controversial at the time and continue to be debated among historians and scientists. While Hertzian waves are now well-established as the basis for modern radio technology, Tesla's contributions to the field, including his patents and demonstrations of wireless power and communication, have been recognized and celebrated in recent years.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and that much of the technology we use today in wireless communication has evolved from both Tesla's and Hertz's work. The debate between Tesla's concepts and the established Hertzian wave theory reflects the complex and sometimes contentious nature of scientific discovery and the development of new technologies.
