Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding what is now known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space in a manner described by Heinrich Hertz did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct form of energy transfer between the transmitter and receiver, often referred to as "resonance" or "resonant coupling." He argued that the key to effective wireless communication lay in the resonance between the transmitter and receiver, rather than simply the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which could be detected at a distance without the need for a direct physical connection. Tesla felt that this approach was incomplete and did not adequately explain the phenomena he observed and utilized in his experiments and inventions.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that Tesla's concepts of resonant coupling and wireless energy transfer have significant relevance in fields such as wireless charging, RFID technology, and even some aspects of wireless communication.

While Tesla's claims about the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory were not immediately validated or widely accepted, they have since been recognized as prescient and influential in the development of modern wireless technologies.
