Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding what is now known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that the concept of radio waves propagating through space as electromagnetic waves was not fully capturing the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just empty space. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept he referred to as "earth currents" or "resonant circuits." This idea was based on his observations and experiments with high-frequency alternating currents, which he believed could be used to transmit information without the need for physical antennas or the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, focused on the propagation of these waves through space. Tesla felt that this approach was incomplete and did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and experimented with.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted at the time. The scientific community largely embraced the Hertzian wave theory, which eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology. However, Tesla's contributions and theories continue to be studied and appreciated for their originality and the insights they provide into the nature of wireless communication.
