Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's conception of wireless transmission was rooted in his belief that it could be achieved through aetheric or radiant energy, which he thought could travel through the Earth and air without the need for a physical medium like the airwaves described by Hertz. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was widely accepted at the time, suggested that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space via oscillating electric and magnetic fields. This theory was supported by experiments showing that these waves could be detected at a distance from their source.

Tesla's claims that the Hertzian wave theory was inimical to the proper understanding of wireless processes were based on several points:

Earth Resonance: Tesla believed that the Earth could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it, resonating at specific frequencies. He conducted experiments to demonstrate this, such as his famous Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to transmit power and information across long distances using the Earth's natural resonances.

Wireless Power Transmission: Tesla also believed that it was possible to transmit electrical power wirelessly, an idea that was ahead of its time. He demonstrated this in various experiments, including lighting bulbs remotely and powering devices without wires.

Interference and Reception: Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory did not adequately explain how signals could be received and transmitted without interference. He believed that the Earth's natural resonances could provide a more stable and efficient means of communication.

Philosophical Differences: There were also philosophical differences between Tesla and the scientific community of his time. Tesla was known for his unconventional ideas and his willingness to challenge established theories, which often led to conflicts with other scientists.

Despite these disagreements, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are undeniable. His patents and demonstrations laid the groundwork for modern radio technology, even if they did not fully align with the prevailing scientific understanding of the time.

It's worth noting that while Tesla's ideas about earth resonance and wireless power transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime, they have gained renewed interest in recent years due to advancements in technology and a deeper understanding of the Earth's properties.
