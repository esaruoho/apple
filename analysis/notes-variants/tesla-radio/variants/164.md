Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of his ideas about wireless transmission.

Tesla's approach to wireless technology was based on the idea of "resonance" between transmitter and receiver, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves as being transmitted in a straight line or through space without any specific interaction with the medium or the receiver was overly simplistic and did not align with his observations and experiments.

In particular, Tesla contended that his systems could transmit signals over vast distances without the need for a line-of-sight path, which was a key aspect of the Hertzian wave theory at the time. He believed that his methods involved a form of resonance that allowed for more efficient and longer-range communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The scientific community generally adopted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for radio communication, although Tesla's concepts have since gained recognition and are now understood to be valid in certain contexts, particularly in relation to resonant circuits and wireless power transfer.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in different aspects of wireless technology, and modern research continues to explore the principles behind both approaches.
