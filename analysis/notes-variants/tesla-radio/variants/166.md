Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's concerns stemmed from several aspects:

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in his wireless systems. He believed that the Earth and the atmosphere could act as a resonant system, which could be tuned to specific frequencies. This concept was not fully accounted for in the Hertzian wave theory, which focused more on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space.

Long-distance Communication: Tesla argued that long-distance communication could be achieved without the need for line-of-sight transmission or the use of towers and antennas. He believed that the Earth itself could serve as a conductor and that signals could travel through the ground and the air, effectively extending the range of communication.

Interference and Reception: Tesla also criticized the idea that radio waves were subject to interference and required complex reception apparatuses. He believed that simpler devices could achieve effective reception and transmission of signals.

Philosophical Differences: There were also philosophical differences between Tesla's approach and the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. Tesla's ideas were more aligned with the concept of aether (or ether), an invisible medium thought to fill all of space and facilitate the propagation of electromagnetic waves. The Hertzian wave theory, on the other hand, was based on the idea that these waves could propagate through free space without needing such a medium.

Despite these criticisms, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were significant. His patents and demonstrations laid the groundwork for modern radio technology, although many of his ideas about the nature of wireless communication remained speculative at the time. It wasn't until later that scientists and engineers developed technologies that better aligned with both Tesla's and Hertz's theories, ultimately leading to the practical applications we see today.
