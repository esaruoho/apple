Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being described by the prevailing theories of his time. He argued that the transmission of signals could be achieved through a more fundamental interaction between the transmitter and receiver, without the need for the intermediary medium suggested by Hertzian waves.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the idea that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and resonator for wireless signals. This perspective led him to develop systems like the Wardenclyffe Tower, which aimed to demonstrate the ability to transmit electrical energy over long distances using resonance rather than relying solely on electromagnetic radiation.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. It wasn't until later that other scientists and engineers built upon his work and developed more practical methods for wireless communication based on the principles of electromagnetic radiation.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions are important in the development of modern wireless technology. Tesla's emphasis on resonant circuits and the Earth's conductivity has influenced contemporary research into wireless technologies, while Hertz's work laid the theoretical foundation for understanding electromagnetic waves.
