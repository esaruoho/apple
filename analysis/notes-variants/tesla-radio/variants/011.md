Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being described by the prevailing theories of the time. He argued that the transmission of information through the air was not simply a matter of radiating waves but involved a more complex interaction between the transmitter and receiver.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the ability of the Earth to act as a conductor for these resonances. He proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through the resonance of the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than through the emission and reception of electromagnetic waves in the traditional sense.

While Tesla's ideas were groundbreaking and have since been validated in part, the scientific community at the time did not fully accept his theories. The development of radio technology and the understanding of electromagnetic waves progressed along different lines, with the work of Guglielmo Marconi being a significant milestone in practical wireless communication.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in our understanding of wireless communication. Tesla's contributions to the field include the development of resonant circuits and the concept of earth-ionospheric resonance, which have influenced modern technologies such as spread spectrum communication and RFID systems.
