Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to what he called "resonance" or "radiant energy," rather than just electromagnetic waves. He argued that the key to effective wireless communication lay in the resonance between the transmitter and receiver, which he believed could be achieved at specific frequencies without the need for the vast distances required by Hertzian waves.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, as it was understood at the time, suggested that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space without a medium, much like light. This theory was foundational to the development of radio technology and was supported by experiments conducted by scientists such as Oliver Lodge and others.

Tesla's claims were based on his own experimental work, including his demonstrations of wireless power transmission and communication over short distances, as well as his later claims of having transmitted signals over much greater distances, though these claims were often met with skepticism.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about wireless communication were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and sometimes difficult to prove scientifically. The widespread adoption of radio technology ultimately relied on the practical applications of Hertzian waves, although Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant.
