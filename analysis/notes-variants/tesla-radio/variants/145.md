Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than simply radiating energy through space. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself or through the ionosphere, which is a layer of the atmosphere that can reflect radio waves. This idea was based on his observations and experiments with resonant circuits and his belief in the Earth's natural resonance.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete description of how wireless communication actually worked. Tesla argued that the true mechanism of wireless transmission involved a form of resonance between the transmitter and receiver, rather than just the emission and reception of electromagnetic waves.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted at the time. The scientific community generally adopted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for understanding electromagnetic radiation and its applications in wireless communication.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in different aspects of wireless technology. Tesla's contributions to the field include the development of resonant circuits and the concept of earth-ionospheric wave propagation, which have been validated and integrated into modern wireless communication systems.
