Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his work with high-frequency alternating currents and his experiments with wireless transmission. He argued that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves as continuous and requiring a medium (such as air or space) for propagation was insufficient to explain the phenomena he observed. Tesla believed that wireless transmission involved a different mechanism, one that could transmit signals without the need for a physical medium.

In his writings and public demonstrations, Tesla often criticized the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. He suggested that the true nature of wireless transmission involved a form of energy that could travel through space without the need for a wave-like disturbance. This idea was somewhat ahead of its time and was not widely accepted during Tesla's lifetime.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about wireless transmission were innovative and influential, they were also controversial. The modern understanding of electromagnetic radiation and wireless communication is based on the work of many scientists and the development of technology over decades. Today, we understand that electromagnetic waves can propagate through space and do not require a physical medium, but the exact mechanisms and principles behind wireless transmission continue to be subjects of study and research.
