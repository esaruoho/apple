Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates many of the theoretical developments attributed to Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with the discovery of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (electromagnetic waves) was not sufficient to explain the phenomena of wireless transmission. He argued that the idea of propagating waves through space did not fully capture the essence of what he perceived as the wireless transmission process. Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the concept of "resonance" in his wireless systems, which he believed was more fundamental to the transmission and reception of signals.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which could be detected by receiving antennas. Tesla's approach was more about the resonance between the transmitter and receiver, suggesting that the key to successful wireless communication lay in the tuning and resonance of the circuits rather than the mere propagation of waves.

This difference in perspective led to some tension between Tesla and the scientific community of his time, which largely embraced the Hertzian wave theory. However, it is important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were groundbreaking, and much of modern radio technology can trace its roots back to his pioneering work.
