Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication should be based on a system of resonant circuits rather than the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the key to wireless transmission lay in the resonance between the transmitter and receiver, rather than the transmission of waves over distances.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described a method of wireless transmission that involved creating a resonant circuit at the receiving end that would resonate with the same frequency as the transmitter. This approach, which Tesla called "resonance," was different from the wave theory that was gaining popularity at the time.

Tesla's ideas were innovative but also controversial. While his work on alternating current (AC) and the development of the Tesla coil contributed significantly to the field of electrical engineering, his theories about wireless transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime. It wasn't until later that other scientists and engineers began to explore and validate aspects of his ideas, such as the use of resonant circuits in wireless communication.

Today, while we understand that electromagnetic waves play a crucial role in wireless communication, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the pursuit of more efficient and effective wireless technologies.
