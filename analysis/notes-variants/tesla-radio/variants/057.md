Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a mere propagation of waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it without the need for the air or space to carry the waves. This idea is often referred to as "Earth currents" or "resonant antenna systems."

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time was that electromagnetic waves could propagate through free space and that these waves could be detected and used for communication. Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and that it did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and utilized in his experiments.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved to incorporate both the principles of electromagnetic wave propagation and the concept of Earth currents, though the latter is less emphasized in practical applications today.

Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are still celebrated, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the development of new technologies.
