Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of his wireless transmission methods and the underlying principles of wireless communication.

Tesla's work on wireless transmission was based on the concept of "resonance" between the transmitter and receiver, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the Hertzian wave theory, which describes electromagnetic waves as propagating through space without requiring a medium, did not accurately represent the way his systems worked.

In particular, Tesla contended that the Hertzian wave theory could not explain phenomena such as the ability to transmit signals over long distances without a line-of-sight path, or the fact that his systems could operate at much lower power levels than those required by the wave theory. He also believed that the wave theory could not account for the phenomenon of "free space" transmission, where signals could travel through the air without being confined to a physical medium.

Tesla's views were controversial at the time and continue to be debated among historians and scientists. While the Hertzian wave theory is now widely accepted as the basis for modern radio and wireless communication, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire research into new forms of wireless technology.
