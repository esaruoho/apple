Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication should be based on a system of resonant circuits, which could transmit energy without the need for a physical medium like air or space. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the ground and the atmosphere using resonant frequencies. This idea was encapsulated in his concept of "earth resonance," which he believed would allow for more efficient and longer-range communication.

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time was that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space without needing a medium, a concept that was later formalized by James Clerk Maxwell's equations. Tesla's ideas were often seen as controversial and ahead of their time, but they laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The practical implementation of radio technology largely followed the principles of Hertzian waves, although Tesla's ideas have since been validated and are now recognized as valid approaches to wireless communication.
