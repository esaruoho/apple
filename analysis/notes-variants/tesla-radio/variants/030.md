Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding what is now known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space was not sufficient to explain the phenomena of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication involved a more direct and intimate interaction between the transmitter and receiver than could be explained by the then-prevailing theories of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that there was a form of "resonance" or "sympathetic vibration" between the transmitter and receiver, which allowed for the transfer of energy without the need for the waves to travel through the intervening medium.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, suggested that these waves could propagate through space and be received at a distance. Tesla argued that this model did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication, which he believed involved a more direct connection between the devices.

This difference in perspective led to some tension between Tesla and other scientists of his era who were more aligned with the Hertzian wave theory. However, it is important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are widely recognized, and his work laid the foundation for modern radio technology. Despite his reservations about the Hertzian wave theory, Tesla's experiments and inventions played a crucial role in developing practical applications for wireless communication.
