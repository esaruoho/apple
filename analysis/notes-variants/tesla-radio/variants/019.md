Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or radio waves as they are commonly known today) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth's ionosphere and the ground, a concept now known as "earth resonance" or "resonant antenna systems."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the transmission of electromagnetic waves through free space, which was the predominant view at the time. Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and that it did not account for the phenomena he observed, such as the ability to transmit signals over long distances without the need for line-of-sight transmission.

Some key points of Tesla's disagreement include:

Earth as a Conductor: Tesla believed that the Earth could be used as a conductor for wireless signals, which would allow for more efficient and longer-range transmission.
Resonance: He proposed that wireless signals could resonate with the Earth, much like sound resonates in a room, which would explain how signals could travel vast distances.
Coherence: Tesla emphasized the importance of coherence in wireless transmission, suggesting that signals should be coherent over large areas rather than being limited to line-of-sight transmission.

Despite these differences, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are undeniable. His experiments and inventions laid the groundwork for modern radio technology, even if his specific theories about earth resonance were not widely accepted during his lifetime.

Today, we understand that both approaches have their merits and that wireless communication can be achieved through various mechanisms, including both free-space propagation and earth resonance.
