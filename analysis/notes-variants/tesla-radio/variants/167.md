Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework developed by Hertz and others did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla argued that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves propagating through space in a manner similar to light waves was an oversimplification. He contended that these waves were not the primary means of transmitting information in his systems. Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through a different mechanism involving the manipulation of the Earth's natural resonant frequencies.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of what he called "resonance" and "earth currents." He believed that by creating resonant circuits and utilizing the Earth's conductive properties, one could transmit signals without relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air. This approach was fundamentally different from the prevailing view at the time, which focused on the transmission of radio waves through space.

Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and faced skepticism during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that some aspects of Tesla's theories may have merit, particularly in the context of very low frequency (VLF) and extremely low frequency (ELF) communications, which can indeed utilize the Earth's conductivity for signal transmission.

It's important to note that while Tesla's claims were innovative and sometimes controversial, they contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology and continue to inspire researchers exploring new methods of communication and energy transfer.
