Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or radio waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to what he called "radiant energy" or "ether," rather than just electromagnetic waves propagating through space. He argued that the traditional view of radio waves as requiring a line-of-sight transmission and being subject to absorption and reflection by matter was overly simplistic and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication.

Tesla proposed that wireless signals could travel through the Earth and air without the need for a direct line of sight, and he believed that this was due to the Earth's ionosphere acting as a conductor and reflector of these signals. This concept is now understood to involve the use of the ionosphere for long-distance radio wave propagation, but at the time, it was a revolutionary idea that differed significantly from the prevailing scientific understanding of the time.

In summary, Tesla's claims about the incompatibility of the electromagnetic radiation theory with his conception of wireless communication were based on his belief in a more complex and dynamic interaction between the signal and the environment, including the Earth itself. His ideas were ahead of their time and contributed to the development of modern wireless technology, although they were not fully accepted during his lifetime.
