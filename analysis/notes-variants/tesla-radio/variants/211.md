Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication should be based on a system of resonant circuits rather than the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the idea of sending signals through the air as waves was overly simplistic and did not account for the complex interactions between the transmitter and receiver.

In his patent applications and public statements, Tesla described his wireless systems as involving a form of resonance that allowed for the transmission of energy without the need for the waves to travel through the air. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it, a concept that is now referred to as Earth resonance or Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower project.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were not widely accepted at the time due to the prevailing scientific understanding of the day. The concept of radio waves as we understand them today was developed further by others, such as Guglielmo Marconi, who successfully demonstrated wireless telegraphy using Hertzian waves.

It's important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were significant, even if his specific theories about the nature of wireless transmission were not universally accepted during his lifetime. His work laid the groundwork for many modern technologies and continues to inspire researchers and engineers to this day.
