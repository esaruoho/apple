Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or what we now call radio waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He proposed that there was a "wireless" medium that could transmit energy and information without the need for physical conductors. This idea was encapsulated in his theories of "resonance" and "earth currents," which suggested that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for wireless signals.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete description of the phenomenon. Tesla argued that the Hertzian waves alone could not account for the long-distance transmission capabilities that he observed and believed in. He thought that the Earth's ionosphere and the ground itself played significant roles in the transmission and reception of these signals.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, as the scientific community at the time was more inclined to accept the established theories of electromagnetism. However, many of Tesla's ideas have since been validated by modern research, particularly in the fields of plasma physics and the study of Earth's ionosphere.

It's important to note that while Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, the development of practical radio technology was largely based on the principles of Hertzian waves, as refined by others like Guglielmo Marconi. Nonetheless, Tesla's work laid the groundwork for much of the subsequent research into wireless communication and continues to be studied and appreciated for its innovative approach to the subject.
