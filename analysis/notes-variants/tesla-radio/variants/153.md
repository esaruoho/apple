Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of energy and information could be achieved through a different mechanism than what was being described by the then-popular Hertzian wave theory.

Tesla's views were based on his own experiments and theories, which emphasized the use of resonant circuits and the concept of "resonance" to transmit and receive signals without relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the Hertzian wave theory, which describes electromagnetic waves as propagating through space, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he understood it.

In particular, Tesla contended that the idea of "wireless" communication being dependent on the propagation of waves through the air was limiting and potentially harmful to the development of true wireless technology. He believed that the key to effective wireless communication lay in the precise tuning of transmitters and receivers to resonate at the same frequency, thus allowing for efficient transfer of energy or information without the need for the waves to travel through the air.

This perspective led Tesla to develop and promote various methods of wireless transmission, including his famous Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to demonstrate the possibility of transmitting electrical power and information over long distances without wires. However, due to financial and technical challenges, this project was never fully realized during Tesla's lifetime.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they also faced significant scientific and practical challenges. The modern understanding of wireless communication is based on a combination of principles from both Tesla's and Hertz's work, as well as advancements made by other scientists and engineers in the intervening years.
