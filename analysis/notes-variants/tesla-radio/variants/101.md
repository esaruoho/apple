Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves propagating through space did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's approach to wireless technology was rooted in his belief that energy could be transmitted without wires through the medium of the Earth itself. He proposed that the Earth could act as a conductor and that signals could be sent through it, rather than through the air. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonant antenna systems."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air, which was the prevailing model at the time. Tesla argued that this theory was incomplete because it did not account for the way energy could be transferred through the Earth and the ionosphere. He believed that the Earth could store and transmit electrical energy, and that this could be used for long-distance communication and power transmission.

Some of Tesla's key points included:

Earth as a Conductor: Tesla believed that the Earth could act as a conductor for electrical currents, allowing for the transmission of energy over vast distances.
Resonance: He proposed that the Earth and the ionosphere could resonate at specific frequencies, which would allow for efficient energy transfer.
Wireless Power Transmission: Tesla worked on developing systems that could transmit power wirelessly, which he demonstrated with his Wardenclyffe Tower project in Long Island, New York.
Limitations of Hertzian Waves: Tesla criticized the reliance on Hertzian waves alone, arguing that they were not sufficient for practical applications of wireless communication and power transmission.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were also controversial and difficult to prove with the scientific tools available at the time. Modern research has shown that some aspects of Tesla's theories have merit, particularly in the realm of resonant antenna systems and the use of the Earth as a conductor for wireless communication. However, the widespread adoption of wireless technology has largely followed the principles established by Hertz and later refined by other scientists and engineers.
