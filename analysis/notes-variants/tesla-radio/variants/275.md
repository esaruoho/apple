Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or what we now call radio waves, did not fully capture his vision of wireless communication and energy transmission.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that he referred to as "radiant energy," which he thought was different from the electromagnetic waves described by Hertz. He argued that the traditional view of radio waves as requiring a line-of-sight transmission and being limited by distance was overly restrictive and did not align with his observations and experiments.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the Earth's ionosphere in facilitating long-distance wireless communication. He believed that by properly tuning the frequency and amplitude of the signals, one could achieve more efficient and longer-range transmission without the need for direct line-of-sight.

While Tesla's claims were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, many of his ideas have since been validated. For example, the concept of resonant antennas and the use of the Earth as a conductor in wireless communication has become a fundamental part of modern radio technology. Additionally, the development of various wireless technologies, including Wi-Fi and cellular networks, has shown that wireless communication can indeed operate over vast distances without the need for line-of-sight.

It's important to note that Tesla's work laid the groundwork for much of modern wireless technology, even if some of his specific theories and methods were not immediately accepted or understood. His contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his legacy continues to influence the development of new technologies.
