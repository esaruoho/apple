Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates the widespread acceptance of Heinrich Hertz's discoveries, which laid the groundwork for understanding electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation as understood at the time was not sufficient to explain the phenomena of wireless transmission. He argued that the idea of propagating waves through space did not fully capture the essence of what he perceived as the true nature of wireless communication.

In contrast, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept he referred to as "earth currents." He believed that these currents could carry information without the need for the air to be filled with electromagnetic waves. This idea was based on his observations and experiments, which suggested that signals could be transmitted through the ground and the atmosphere in a manner that was different from the then-popular wave theory.

Tesla's views were controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, his contributions to the field of wireless communication have since been recognized, and many of his ideas have been validated over time. For example, the use of ground waves and sky waves in radio communication is now well understood and utilized in modern radio technology.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were also ahead of their time in some respects. The full understanding of electromagnetic waves and their application in wireless communication would come later, with advancements in technology and further research.
