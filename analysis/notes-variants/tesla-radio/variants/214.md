Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding what is now known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that the concept of radio waves being transmitted through space as waves was not fully capturing the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct form of energy transfer between the transmitter and receiver, often referred to as "resonance" or "beamless" transmission. He argued that the idea of radio waves propagating through the air as waves was an oversimplification and did not accurately describe the true nature of wireless communication.

In contrast to the prevailing view of the time, which held that radio waves could travel through space without a physical medium, Tesla proposed that the Earth itself played a crucial role in the transmission of wireless signals. He suggested that the Earth acted as a conductor and that the signals were transmitted along its surface or through its interior, rather than through the air as waves.

This perspective led Tesla to develop and advocate for systems that utilized resonant circuits and earth currents, such as his Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to demonstrate long-distance wireless transmission using these principles. However, the practical limitations and financial challenges associated with this project ultimately prevented it from achieving its full potential.

Despite Tesla's reservations, the Hertzian wave theory eventually became the dominant model for understanding radio wave propagation, although modern physics recognizes both wave-like and particle-like properties of electromagnetic radiation. Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, even if his specific theories about the nature of radio waves have not been widely accepted in the scientific community.
