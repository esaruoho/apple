Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through aetheric currents or other means that did not rely solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the traditional view of radio waves as requiring a line-of-sight path and being subject to absorption and reflection by matter was overly simplistic and did not align with his experimental observations and theoretical framework.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the ability to transmit energy without relying on the physical propagation of waves. He believed that by tuning the frequency of the transmitter and receiver to resonate at the same frequency, one could achieve efficient and long-range communication without the need for the waves to travel through the air in a straight line.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they also led to some controversy and misunderstanding among his contemporaries. The scientific community eventually came to accept the electromagnetic wave theory as the basis for radio communication, although Tesla's contributions to the development of practical radio technology cannot be overstated.

It's worth noting that modern physics has largely validated the electromagnetic wave theory, but it continues to be an area of active research and debate, especially in the context of new technologies such as quantum communication and wireless energy transfer.
