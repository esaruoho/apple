Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of "waves" as described by Heinrich Hertz did not accurately represent the nature of wireless transmission.

Tesla's views were based on his own experiments and theories, particularly his work with resonant circuits and his belief in the existence of a continuous field of energy that could be tapped for communication and power transmission without the need for physical wires. He proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through a form of energy that would resonate at specific frequencies, similar to how a musical instrument resonates at certain pitches.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of discrete waves through space, which Tesla felt was an oversimplification. He believed that the continuous field approach was more aligned with the actual mechanisms of wireless transmission, which he thought involved a more complex interaction between the transmitter and receiver.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not universally accepted during his lifetime. The modern understanding of wireless communication is based on a combination of both Tesla's concepts and the Hertzian wave theory, with significant contributions from other scientists and engineers who refined and expanded upon these ideas.
