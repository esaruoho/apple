Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's approach to wireless technology was rooted in his belief that it could be achieved through aetheric or radiant energy, which he thought could travel without the need for a medium like air or space. He proposed that wireless transmission could be achieved through a form of energy that could penetrate matter, rather than relying on the oscillating electric and magnetic fields described by Hertzian waves.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was limited and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication. He suggested that the theory was too focused on the propagation of waves through space and did not adequately address the underlying principles of energy transmission and reception.

Tesla's ideas were often met with skepticism and criticism from the scientific community of his time, partly because they did not align with the established theories of electromagnetism. However, his contributions to the field of wireless communication are now widely recognized, and many of his concepts have been validated over time.

It's important to note that while Tesla's views on the nature of wireless communication were different from those of his contemporaries, his work laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology. The development of radio and other forms of wireless communication has since incorporated both Hertzian wave theory and Tesla's ideas, demonstrating the complex interplay between scientific theories and practical applications.
