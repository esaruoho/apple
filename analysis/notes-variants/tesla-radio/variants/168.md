Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory are well-documented.

Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of energy could be achieved through a different mechanism than what was then understood as electromagnetic waves. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were thought to be necessary for wireless communication, was not sufficient or accurate for describing the phenomena he observed and experienced.

In his view, the idea that wireless signals required a line-of-sight transmission and that they would dissipate over distance was overly restrictive. Tesla proposed that wireless signals could travel through the Earth and air without the need for a direct line of sight, and he believed that this could be achieved through a form of resonance between the transmitter and receiver.

His experiments and demonstrations, such as the Wardenclyffe Tower project, aimed to prove these concepts. However, the project was ultimately abandoned due to financial difficulties, and Tesla's ideas were not widely accepted at the time.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were not fully validated until much later. Modern technology has confirmed many aspects of Tesla's theories, particularly regarding the use of resonant circuits and the propagation of electromagnetic fields. However, the exact mechanisms and the extent to which Tesla's ideas differ from the established electromagnetic theory remain subjects of ongoing debate and research.
