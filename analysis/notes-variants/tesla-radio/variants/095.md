Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding what is now known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space in a manner similar to those described by Heinrich Hertz did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and intimate interaction between the transmitter and receiver, often referred to as "resonance" or "sympathetic vibration." He argued that the key to effective wireless communication lay in the resonance between the transmitter and receiver, rather than simply the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described wireless communication as involving a form of energy transfer that could be achieved without the need for a physical medium like air or vacuum. He emphasized the importance of the resonant frequency and the ability of the receiver to resonate at the same frequency as the transmitter, which he believed was essential for efficient and long-range communication.

While Tesla's ideas were groundbreaking and have influenced modern wireless technology, the scientific community at the time—and even today—has largely accepted the Hertzian wave theory as a valid explanation for how electromagnetic waves propagate through space. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication, including his work on resonant circuits and his experiments with high-frequency currents, remain significant and continue to be studied and appreciated for their innovative approach to the subject.
