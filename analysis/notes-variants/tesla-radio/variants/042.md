Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and disagreement with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that his own discoveries and inventions were based on principles that went beyond what Hertz had demonstrated.

Tesla's claims and criticisms can be summarized as follows:

Wireless Transmission: Tesla believed that wireless transmission could be achieved without relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the ground or the ionosphere.

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in his wireless transmission experiments. He believed that by tuning the frequency of his transmitter to match the natural resonant frequency of the receiving device, he could achieve efficient and long-distance communication without the need for electromagnetic waves.

Hertzian Waves: While Hertz had demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, Tesla did not believe that these waves were necessary for wireless communication. He thought that the concept of Hertzian waves was overly simplistic and did not fully capture the complexity of wireless phenomena.

Earth as a Conductor: Tesla proposed that the Earth could be used as a conductor for wireless signals. This idea was part of his later experiments with high-frequency currents and his attempts to transmit power and information across vast distances.

Coherers and Other Devices: Tesla also criticized the use of coherers and other devices that were commonly used in early radio experiments. He believed that these devices were unreliable and that more sophisticated methods were needed for reliable wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and often ahead of his time, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. Modern science has largely validated the role of electromagnetic waves in wireless communication, but Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant and continue to inspire research into alternative methods of wireless transmission.
