Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (radio waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through aetheric currents or "radiant energy," which he thought could travel through the Earth and air without the need for a physical medium like Hertzian waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it, a concept often referred to as "earth resonance."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was widely accepted at the time, suggested that radio waves traveled through space as electromagnetic waves. Tesla argued that this theory was incomplete and that it did not explain how long-distance wireless communication could occur without the waves dissipating too quickly.

Tesla's claims and theories were controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, his ideas about earth resonance and wireless transmission have been revisited and studied more recently, particularly in the context of modern technologies such as wireless charging and resonant coupling.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed to the development of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted in his time. The scientific community generally adopted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for radio communication until later developments confirmed some of Tesla's concepts, albeit in different forms and with additional scientific backing.
