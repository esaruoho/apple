Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates many of the developments attributed to Guglielmo Marconi and others, and he believed that his methods were fundamentally different from what was being described by the electromagnetic radiation theory.

Tesla's claims that the electromagnetic radiation theory was "inimical" to the proper understanding of wireless communication stemmed from several key points:

Resonance vs. Radiation: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that the key to successful wireless transmission was not simply radiating energy into the air but rather creating a resonant circuit that could efficiently transfer energy between the transmitter and receiver. This resonant approach was different from the idea of broadcasting energy through the air as waves.

Long-distance Transmission: Tesla claimed that his methods allowed for long-distance transmission without the need for a line-of-sight or a dense network of relay stations. He believed that the Earth itself could be used as a conductor, which would allow signals to travel vast distances without significant loss.

Interference and Reception: Tesla argued that the electromagnetic radiation theory did not adequately explain how signals could be received and transmitted without interference. He believed that the theory overemphasized the role of radiation and underplayed the importance of resonant circuits and the Earth's natural properties.

Infrasound and Other Phenomena: Tesla also explored phenomena such as infrasound and other forms of low-frequency energy, which he believed played a crucial role in wireless communication. These ideas were outside the scope of the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood at the time.

It's important to note that while Tesla's claims were innovative and often controversial, they contributed significantly to the development of modern radio technology. His work laid the groundwork for many of the principles used in contemporary wireless communication systems.

Despite Tesla's assertions, the electromagnetic radiation theory eventually became the standard framework for understanding radio waves and wireless communication. However, Tesla's contributions and his emphasis on resonance and the Earth's conductivity continue to be recognized and studied in the field of wireless technology.
