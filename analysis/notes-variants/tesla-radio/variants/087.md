Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's concerns stemmed from several aspects:

Wireless Transmission vs. Radio Waves: Tesla saw wireless transmission as a broader concept than just the propagation of radio waves. He believed that wireless communication could be achieved through various means, including his own system of resonant circuits and Earth's natural resonance.

Distance and Range: Tesla argued that the range of Hertzian waves was limited and that they required a line-of-sight or direct path between transmitter and receiver. This limitation, according to Tesla, was not inherent to wireless communication but rather a result of the current understanding of electromagnetic waves.

Interference and Reception: Tesla also believed that the reception of Hertzian waves could be affected by various environmental factors, leading to interference and reduced signal quality. He proposed that his methods could mitigate these issues.

Energy Efficiency: Tesla contended that his methods were more energy-efficient and could transmit signals over longer distances without the need for large amounts of power, which was a significant concern with Hertzian waves at the time.

Despite these criticisms, Hertzian waves eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology. However, Tesla's ideas laid the groundwork for many future developments in wireless communication, including the concept of resonant antennas and the use of the Earth as a conductor in wireless systems.

It's important to note that while Tesla's views were influential, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The scientific community gradually came to understand and integrate both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions into the field of wireless communication.
