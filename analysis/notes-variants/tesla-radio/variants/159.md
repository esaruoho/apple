Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of his conception of wireless communication. Here are some key points to consider:

Wireless Communication Concept: Tesla's vision of wireless communication was more holistic and encompassed not just the transmission of signals through electromagnetic waves but also the idea of a global network where information could be transmitted without the need for physical wires.

Hertzian Waves: Heinrich Hertz discovered that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space, which laid the groundwork for wireless communication. However, Tesla felt that this theory was overly focused on the propagation of waves and did not adequately address the underlying principles of energy transmission and reception.

Coherence and Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of coherence and resonance in his wireless systems. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a resonant medium, allowing for the transmission of signals over vast distances without the need for complex apparatus. This concept was not fully captured by the Hertzian wave theory, which primarily described the propagation of waves in free space.

Interference and Reception: Tesla was concerned that the Hertzian wave theory might lead to misunderstandings about interference and reception. He believed that the theory did not account for the complexities of signal interaction and the potential for interference from other sources, which could affect the reliability of wireless communication.

Patents and Disputes: Tesla's views on the Hertzian wave theory were partly influenced by his patent disputes with Guglielmo Marconi. Marconi's work on wireless telegraphy was based on the Hertzian wave theory, and Tesla felt that Marconi's claims were unjustified and that he had been unfairly credited for Tesla's inventions.

Experimental Evidence: Tesla conducted extensive experiments to demonstrate the feasibility of his ideas. For example, he demonstrated wireless power transmission and communication over long distances, which he believed supported his view that the Hertzian wave theory was incomplete.

In summary, while the Hertzian wave theory provided a scientific basis for wireless communication, Tesla believed that it did not fully capture the broader principles of energy transmission and reception that he considered essential for true wireless communication. His contributions to the field of wireless technology continue to be recognized, even if his views on the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory remain a subject of debate among historians and scientists.
