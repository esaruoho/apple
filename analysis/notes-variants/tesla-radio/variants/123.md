Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates many of the theoretical developments attributed to Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with the discovery of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the idea of sending signals through the air as waves was not sufficient for practical applications like radio transmission. Instead, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the Earth's ionosphere in transmitting signals.

In his view, the key to effective wireless communication was not just the propagation of electromagnetic waves but also the resonance between the transmitter and receiver. This resonance allowed for more efficient and reliable transmission over longer distances. Tesla's patents and demonstrations often focused on these principles rather than on the wave theory alone.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also at odds with the emerging scientific consensus of the time. The wave theory eventually became the accepted framework for understanding electromagnetic radiation, and it provided a solid foundation for further advancements in radio technology.

Despite these differences, both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions are crucial to the development of modern radio and telecommunications. Tesla's emphasis on practical applications and resonant systems complemented the theoretical work of scientists like Hertz, leading to the eventual integration of both approaches into the field of radio communication.
