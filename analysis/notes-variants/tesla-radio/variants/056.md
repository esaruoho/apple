Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless communication could be achieved through a medium that he referred to as "earth currents" or "resonant circuits." He proposed that signals could be transmitted without the need for high-frequency oscillations and long-distance propagation through the air. Instead, he suggested that the Earth itself could act as a conductor, allowing for the transmission of signals via resonant frequencies.

In his view, the traditional electromagnetic wave theory, which described radio waves as traveling through the air, was incomplete and potentially misleading. Tesla argued that this theory did not adequately explain how signals could be received and transmitted over vast distances without the use of large antennas or high-frequency generators.

While Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, including the development of the Tesla coil and the demonstration of wireless lighting and power, his ideas about the nature of wireless transmission were not widely accepted during his lifetime. It was only later that advancements in technology and further research confirmed aspects of his theories, particularly the role of ground currents in wireless communication.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in our understanding of wireless communication. Modern technology often combines elements of both approaches to achieve efficient and reliable wireless transmission.
