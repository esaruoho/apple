Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a simple wave propagating through space. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could be transmitted through the ground or the ionosphere, rather than through space alone.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the idea of Hertzian waves being the primary means of wireless communication was overly simplistic and did not account for the full potential of wireless technology. He emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the ability to tune into specific frequencies, which he believed would allow for more efficient and effective wireless communication.

Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, many of his concepts have since been validated by modern research in wireless technology, particularly in the areas of resonant coupling and the use of the Earth as a conductor in wireless systems.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and influential, they were also complex and sometimes difficult to understand without a deep background in physics and engineering. Modern technology has largely adopted the principles of electromagnetic radiation, but Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant and continue to inspire new research and applications in wireless technology.
