Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and disagreement with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of energy and information could be achieved through a different mechanism than what was then understood.

Tesla's views were rooted in his own experiments and theories, particularly those involving resonant circuits and the concept of "wireless" or "long-distance" communication without the need for a physical conductor. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept known as "earth resonance."

In contrast, the electromagnetic radiation theory, as developed by Hertz, suggested that radio waves could propagate through space without a medium, much like light. This theory was foundational for the development of radio technology but did not align with Tesla's vision of wireless communication.

Tesla's claims and demonstrations often highlighted the limitations of the Hertzian wave theory in practical applications. For example, he argued that the high-frequency oscillations used in Hertzian waves would dissipate quickly over long distances, making them unsuitable for long-range communication. Instead, Tesla advocated for lower frequencies that could resonate with the Earth and thus maintain their strength over greater distances.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not fully accepted during his lifetime. The practical implementation of radio technology largely followed the principles established by Hertz and others, although Tesla's contributions to the field, including his patents and demonstrations, were significant and continue to be recognized today.
