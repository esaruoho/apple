Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's conception of wireless technology was based on a more holistic approach, involving the Earth itself as a conductor and resonator. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth's ionosphere and the ground, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower."

In his view, the Hertzian wave theory was too narrow and focused on the transmission of signals through space, which he felt was inefficient and impractical for long-distance communication. Tesla argued that by utilizing the Earth's natural properties and resonant frequencies, one could achieve more efficient and powerful wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were not widely accepted or understood at the time. The scientific community largely embraced the Hertzian wave theory, which eventually led to the development of radio as we know it today. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the field of wireless technology.
