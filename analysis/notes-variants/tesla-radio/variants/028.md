Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves," named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being proposed by the prevailing scientific community. He argued that the transmission of information through the air could be achieved without the need for a physical medium like a conductor or a resonant circuit, which was the basis of the Hertzian wave theory at the time.

In contrast, Tesla advocated for a system where the transmitter and receiver were directly coupled through the Earth itself, a concept he referred to as "earth currents" or "resonance." This idea was based on his experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and his belief in the Earth's natural resonance as a means of transmitting signals over long distances.

While Tesla's ideas were revolutionary and ahead of their time, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. It wasn't until later that advancements in technology and further research confirmed aspects of his theories, particularly in the development of long-distance radio communication and the understanding of Earth-ionosphere waveguides.

It's important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, even if his specific theories about the nature of wireless transmission were not immediately embraced by the scientific community. His work laid the groundwork for many modern technologies, including radio, television, and radar.
