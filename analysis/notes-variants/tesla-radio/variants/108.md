Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas about wireless transmission were rooted in his belief that energy could be transmitted without the need for wires or physical media. He proposed that energy could be transmitted through the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" and "space currents," respectively. This concept is often associated with his work on resonance and the idea that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for electrical energy.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete description of how wireless communication could work. Tesla believed that the Hertzian wave theory was too narrow and did not account for the broader principles of energy transmission and reception that he observed and experimented with.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they also led to some controversy and misunderstanding. The scientific community at the time generally accepted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for wireless communication, and it eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology.

Despite these differences, both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions were crucial in the development of wireless communication. Tesla's work laid the groundwork for many of the principles used in modern wireless technology, while Hertz's experiments provided the theoretical framework that helped explain the behavior of electromagnetic waves.
