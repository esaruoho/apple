Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates the widespread acceptance of Heinrich Hertz's discoveries, which laid the foundation for modern electromagnetic theory.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves propagating through space did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through a vacuum could not account for the phenomena he observed and experienced in his experiments. Tesla emphasized the role of Earth and the ionosphere in transmitting signals, which is now known as ground wave propagation.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, without considering the influence of the Earth or other conductive surfaces. This difference in perspective led to some tension between Tesla's ideas and the emerging scientific consensus.

It's important to note that while Tesla's views were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of radio technology, they were not universally accepted at the time. The scientific community eventually came to understand and accept many of Tesla's concepts, although the exact mechanisms of wireless transmission remained subjects of debate and further research.

Today, we recognize that both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions are crucial to our understanding of radio communication. Tesla's emphasis on the role of the Earth and ionosphere in signal propagation has been validated by modern research, while Hertz's work on electromagnetic waves provided the theoretical framework that underpins much of our current understanding of wireless technology.
