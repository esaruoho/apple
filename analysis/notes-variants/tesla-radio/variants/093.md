Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or radio waves) did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to aether or a subtle form of matter, rather than just electromagnetic waves propagating through space. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" and "space currents," respectively.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, which was seen as a more direct and simpler explanation for wireless communication. However, Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and did not account for the full potential of wireless technology.

Some key points of Tesla's criticism include:

Earth Currents: Tesla believed that much of the signal transmission occurred through the Earth itself, which he thought could act as a conductor for the current. This idea was contrary to the Hertzian wave theory, which emphasized the propagation of waves through the air.

Space Currents: Tesla also proposed that signals could travel through the ionosphere, which is a layer of the atmosphere rich in ions. This concept was ahead of its time and only gained widespread acceptance later with advancements in technology.

Wireless Power Transmission: Tesla's experiments with wireless power transmission, such as his Wardenclyffe Tower project, aimed to demonstrate the ability to transmit electrical energy over long distances without wires. This was fundamentally different from the concept of simply transmitting information via radio waves.

Interference and Reception: Tesla believed that the reception of wireless signals could be improved by using resonant circuits, which would allow for more efficient and selective reception. This was distinct from the Hertzian wave theory, which relied more on the strength and frequency of the signal.

Despite these differences, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were immense. His patents and demonstrations laid the groundwork for modern radio and wireless technology. While his theories about earth and space currents were not widely accepted during his lifetime, they have since been validated by scientific research and technological advancements.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas were often ahead of their time and required further development and validation. The Hertzian wave theory, while initially controversial, eventually became the foundation for modern radio and telecommunications.
