Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework developed by Hertz and others did not fully capture the essence of his own wireless transmission methods.

Tesla argued that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves traveling through space in a manner similar to light waves did not accurately describe the phenomena he observed and utilized in his wireless experiments. He contended that the idea of waves propagating through the air without a medium (like a vacuum) was an oversimplification and did not reflect the true nature of wireless communication.

Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, utilizing the ionosphere and the ground as a conductor. This concept is now known as earth resonance or Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower project. He believed that this method was more efficient and had fewer practical limitations than relying solely on atmospheric waves.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were also controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. Modern science has largely validated the principles of electromagnetic radiation, but it has also expanded our understanding of wireless communication beyond what Tesla envisioned. Nonetheless, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless technology remain significant, and his skepticism towards the prevailing theories of his time continues to be discussed in historical and scientific contexts.
