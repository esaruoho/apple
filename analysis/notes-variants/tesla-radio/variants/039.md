Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his own experiments and theories. He proposed that wireless transmission could be achieved through the Earth itself, a concept known as "Earth resonance" or "resonant radiation." According to Tesla, the Earth could act as a conductor for these resonant currents, allowing for efficient and long-range communication without the need for high-frequency oscillators or antennas as required by the then-prevailing Hertzian wave theory.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was overly focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which he felt was not the primary mechanism for wireless communication. Instead, he emphasized the role of the Earth and the ionosphere in facilitating wireless transmission.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about resonant currents were innovative and have since been validated in part, they were also controversial at the time. The scientific community largely accepted the Hertzian wave theory as the correct explanation for electromagnetic radiation and its applications in wireless communication.

Despite the controversy, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant. His patents and demonstrations laid the groundwork for modern radio technology, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers working on new forms of wireless communication.
