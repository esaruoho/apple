Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves as understood at the time did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that wireless communication could be facilitated through the Earth itself and the ionosphere, which he referred to as a "wireless conductor." This idea was encapsulated in his concept of "earth resonance," where he believed that the Earth could be used as a conductor for transmitting information without the need for physical wires.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the transmission of electromagnetic waves through space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete explanation for the phenomena he observed and experimented with. Tesla's experiments often involved high-frequency alternating currents and resonant circuits, which he believed could achieve long-distance communication without the need for a line-of-sight transmission.

It's important to note that Tesla's views were not universally accepted during his lifetime, and the scientific community largely adopted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for radio communication. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, and his ideas continue to influence discussions about the nature of electromagnetic fields and their applications in modern technology.
