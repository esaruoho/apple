Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to what he called "radiant energy" or "ether," which he thought was a more efficient and direct means of transmitting information without the need for physical waves. He argued that the idea of electromagnetic waves traveling through space in a vacuum was an oversimplification and did not accurately describe the phenomena he observed and utilized in his experiments.

In contrast to the prevailing scientific understanding of his era, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept he referred to as "earth resonance." This idea was based on his observations of how electrical currents could travel along the surface of the Earth and through the ground, which he believed could be used to transmit information over long distances without the need for the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted at the time due to the limitations in scientific understanding and experimental evidence. It wasn't until later that advancements in technology and further research confirmed aspects of Tesla's theories, such as the use of resonant circuits and the discovery of the ionosphere, which helped explain how radio waves could travel around the Earth.

Today, while we recognize the importance of Hertzian waves in wireless communication, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the development of new technologies.
