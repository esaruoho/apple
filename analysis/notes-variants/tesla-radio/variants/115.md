Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his work with high-frequency alternating currents (AC) and his experiments with resonant circuits. He argued that the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space was not the primary mechanism for wireless communication, as suggested by the Hertzian wave theory. Instead, Tesla believed in a system where the Earth itself played a crucial role in the transmission and reception of signals.

In his view, the Earth could act as a conductor or a medium for transmitting and receiving signals, which would allow for more efficient and longer-range communication without the need for line-of-sight transmission. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

Tesla's skepticism towards the Hertzian wave theory was partly due to his belief that it did not account for the practical aspects of wireless communication that he had observed and experimented with. However, it's important to note that the Hertzian wave theory eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology, and Tesla's contributions to wireless communication were recognized later, even though they were not fully appreciated during his lifetime.

Despite Tesla's reservations, the Hertzian wave theory has been extensively validated and forms the basis of our current understanding of electromagnetic radiation and wireless communication.
