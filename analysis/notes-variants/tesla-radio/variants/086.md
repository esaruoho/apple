Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through aether or some form of invisible medium that could carry signals without the need for physical waves. He argued that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves as requiring a medium like air or water to propagate was incorrect. Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could travel through what he called "space," which he interpreted as a more fundamental medium than just air or water.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described methods for transmitting electrical energy and information without relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves in the way Hertzian waves were understood at the time. He emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the ability to tune into specific frequencies, which he believed would allow for more efficient and effective wireless communication.

Despite these claims, it is important to note that modern physics has largely validated the principles of electromagnetic radiation as described by James Clerk Maxwell and later confirmed by experiments conducted by Hertz. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are still recognized, and his ideas continue to inspire discussions about the nature of wireless technology and the underlying physics of signal transmission.
