Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless communication could be achieved through aether or some form of invisible medium that could carry signals without the need for physical waves propagating through space. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the Earth's natural resonant frequencies, a concept sometimes referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time, based on Hertz's experiments, suggested that electromagnetic waves could travel through free space and that antennas could transmit and receive these waves. Tesla argued that this approach was limited and did not fully exploit the potential of wireless communication.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved to incorporate both the principles of electromagnetic radiation and Tesla's concepts of earth resonance, among others. Today, we use a combination of these theories to develop efficient and effective wireless communication systems.
