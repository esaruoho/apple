Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could be transmitted through the ground or the ionosphere. This idea is often referred to as "Earth resonance" or "resonant antenna" theory.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described methods for transmitting electrical energy without wires, which he believed could be done through the natural resonant properties of the Earth and the ionosphere. He conducted experiments that suggested that certain frequencies could resonate with the Earth, allowing for efficient transmission of power and information over long distances.

Tesla's claims were controversial at the time and have been subject to much debate among scientists and historians. While modern technology has largely validated the practical applications of electromagnetic radiation for wireless communication, Tesla's specific theories about Earth resonance have not been fully supported by subsequent scientific research. However, his ideas continue to inspire researchers in the field of wireless communication and have contributed to ongoing discussions about the nature of wireless transmission.

It's important to note that while Tesla's work laid the groundwork for many aspects of modern wireless technology, the exact mechanisms of wireless communication as we understand them today differ from Tesla's original conceptions.
