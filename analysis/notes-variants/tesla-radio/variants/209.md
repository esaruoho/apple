Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's approach to wireless technology was rooted in his understanding of the Earth's natural resonant frequencies and the concept of "wireless transmission" through the ionosphere and the Earth itself. He argued that the traditional view of radio waves as requiring a line-of-sight transmission and being limited to short distances was overly restrictive and did not align with his observations and experiments.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of using the Earth as a conductor and resonator for wireless signals. He believed that by properly tuning the frequency of the transmitter and receiver, one could achieve long-range communication without the need for physical antennas or direct line-of-sight. This concept is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

Tesla's views were controversial at the time and were not widely accepted by the scientific community. However, his ideas have since gained recognition and have influenced modern research into wireless technologies, including the development of ground-penetrating radar and other forms of long-distance communication systems.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The practical applications and theoretical foundations of radio communication continued to evolve based on the work of many scientists and engineers.
