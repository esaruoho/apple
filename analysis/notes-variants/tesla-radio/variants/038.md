Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's primary concerns with the Hertzian wave theory included:

Frequency: Tesla argued that the frequencies used for wireless transmission should be much higher than those typically employed at the time. He believed that lower frequencies were not efficient for long-distance communication and that higher frequencies would allow for better signal propagation and reception.

Coherence: Tesla emphasized the importance of coherent signals, which he felt were not adequately addressed by the Hertzian wave theory. He believed that the signals needed to be more focused and less diffused to achieve effective communication over long distances.

Resonance: Tesla placed great emphasis on resonance between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that tuning the system to resonate at specific frequencies could enhance the efficiency and clarity of the communication. This idea is somewhat related to what we now understand as resonant antennas or resonant circuits.

Earth as a Conductor: Tesla proposed that the Earth itself could be used as a conductor for wireless communication. He believed that by properly grounding the transmitter and receiver, one could harness the natural properties of the Earth to facilitate long-range communication without relying solely on atmospheric conditions.

Interference: Tesla was concerned about the interference that could occur with the use of Hertzian waves, especially in crowded frequency bands. He believed that higher frequencies and better control over the signal could mitigate this issue.

Despite these differences, it's important to note that Tesla's ideas were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The development of radio technology largely followed the path laid out by the Hertzian wave theory, although many of Tesla's concepts and inventions have since been recognized and integrated into modern radio technology.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play crucial roles in the functioning of radio communications. Tesla's contributions to the field include the development of resonant circuits, which are still fundamental in modern radio design, and his work on high-frequency alternating currents, which paved the way for many modern electrical technologies.
