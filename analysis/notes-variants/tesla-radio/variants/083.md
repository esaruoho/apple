Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the theories and experiments of his time did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through aether waves, which he thought were different from the electromagnetic waves described by Hertz. He argued that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation alone was insufficient to explain the phenomena of wireless transmission. Tesla proposed that there was a more fundamental mechanism at play, involving what he called "primary waves," which were distinct from the secondary waves (electromagnetic waves) that Hertz had demonstrated.

In particular, Tesla criticized the idea that wireless signals could be transmitted through the air without any physical medium. He believed that there was an underlying medium or aether that facilitated these transmissions, and that the current understanding of electromagnetic waves was incomplete. This led him to develop his own theories and methods for wireless communication, such as his work on resonant circuits and his experiments with high-frequency currents.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. Modern physics has largely moved away from the concept of aether and instead relies on quantum mechanics and electromagnetism to explain wireless communication. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless technology remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers today.
