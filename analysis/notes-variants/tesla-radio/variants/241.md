Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that his own experiments and observations indicated that wireless transmission involved a fundamentally different mechanism than what was being described by the prevailing scientific theories of the time.

Tesla's views were based on his extensive work with high-frequency alternating currents and his belief in the existence of "resonant" or "resonance" phenomena in wireless communication. He argued that the transmission of signals through the air did not require the propagation of electromagnetic waves in the way that Hertzian waves were understood at the time. Instead, Tesla suggested that there were specific resonant frequencies that could be used to transmit information without the need for the continuous emission of electromagnetic radiation.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the Hertzian wave theory, which was becoming increasingly popular among scientists and engineers. He believed that this theory oversimplified the complex interactions between matter and electromagnetic fields, and that it failed to account for the true nature of wireless communication.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The modern understanding of radio waves and wireless communication is based on a combination of principles from both Tesla's and Hertz's work, as well as subsequent research and experimentation.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions are integral to our current understanding of wireless communication. Tesla's focus on resonant circuits and his experiments with high-frequency currents laid the groundwork for many aspects of modern radio technology, while Hertz's work on electromagnetic waves provided a theoretical foundation that has been extensively validated and applied in various fields of science and engineering.
