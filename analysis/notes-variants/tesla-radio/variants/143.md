Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that he referred to as "earth currents" or "resonant circuits." He argued that the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air was not the most efficient or accurate way to transmit information over long distances. Instead, he proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through resonant circuits that matched the natural frequencies of the Earth.

In his writings and public statements, Tesla often criticized the prevailing theories and methods of wireless communication. He believed that the use of high-frequency radio waves as proposed by Guglielmo Marconi and others was not the best approach for practical wireless communication. Tesla's ideas were more aligned with the concept of low-frequency resonant systems, which he thought could achieve better results in terms of range and efficiency.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of electrical engineering, they were not widely accepted at the time. The development of practical radio technology followed the path of high-frequency radio waves, which eventually led to the widespread adoption of radio communication as we know it today. However, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers in various fields of science and technology.
