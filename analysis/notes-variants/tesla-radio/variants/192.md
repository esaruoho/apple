Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the theories and experiments of his time were not fully capturing the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's concerns stemmed from several aspects:

Radiation vs. Resonance: Tesla argued that the concept of electromagnetic waves radiating from an antenna was not sufficient to explain how wireless communication could be achieved over long distances. He believed that the key to wireless transmission lay in the resonance between the transmitter and receiver, rather than in the propagation of waves through space.

Distance and Power: Tesla contended that the distance over which wireless signals could travel was limited by the power of the signal and the efficiency of the transmission method. He believed that higher frequencies would require more power to transmit effectively, which contradicted the prevailing view that lower frequencies were better for long-distance communication.

Interference and Reception: Tesla also criticized the idea that signals could be received simply by picking up electromagnetic waves in the air. He believed that proper reception required a resonant circuit tuned to the frequency of the transmitted signal, which would then resonate with the transmitter.

Wireless Power Transmission: Tesla's most famous claim was that wireless power transmission could be achieved without the need for physical conductors. He demonstrated this with his Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to transmit electrical energy over vast distances without wires. However, the project was never completed due to financial and technical challenges.

Despite Tesla's criticisms, the electromagnetic radiation theory eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology. However, Tesla's ideas about resonance and wireless communication have influenced various fields, including wireless charging technologies and resonant coupling in electronics.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and influential, they did not replace the established electromagnetic radiation theory but rather complemented it, offering new perspectives on wireless communication.
