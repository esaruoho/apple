Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or radio waves as they were later called, did not fully capture the essence of his vision for wireless communication.

Tesla's ideas about wireless transmission were rooted in his belief that energy could be transmitted through the Earth and the ionosphere without the need for physical antennas or conductors. He proposed that energy could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept he referred to as "earth currents" or "terrestrial currents." This idea was based on his observations of how electrical currents behaved in the natural environment and his experiments with resonant circuits.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which required antennas to transmit and receive signals. Tesla felt that this approach was limited and did not fully exploit the potential of wireless technology.

Some key points from Tesla's perspective include:

Earth as a Conductor: Tesla believed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for electrical currents, allowing for the transmission of energy over vast distances without the need for physical antennas.

Resonance: He emphasized the importance of resonance in wireless transmission, suggesting that if the transmitter and receiver were tuned to the same frequency, they could resonate with each other, facilitating efficient energy transfer.

Wireless Power: Tesla envisioned a future where wireless power transmission would be possible, enabling the transmission of electricity over long distances without wires.

Limitations of Hertzian Waves: Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was too narrow in its focus and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication. He believed that the theory was overly reliant on physical structures and did not fully explore the natural properties of the Earth and atmosphere.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were also controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. It wasn't until much later that many of his concepts, such as the use of the Earth as a conductor and the principles of resonance, were validated and incorporated into modern wireless technology.

It's important to note that Tesla's contributions to wireless communication are significant, even if his theories about the nature of electromagnetic radiation were not entirely accurate according to contemporary scientific understanding. His work laid the groundwork for many of the technologies we use today, including radio, television, and wireless communication systems.
