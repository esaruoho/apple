Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than simply radiating energy through space. He argued that the transmission of information and power could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a mere propagation of waves. This is evident in his patents and writings, where he described methods involving resonant circuits and earth currents.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance between the transmitter and receiver, suggesting that the efficiency and range of wireless communication could be greatly enhanced if the systems were tuned to resonate at the same frequency. This approach contrasted with the prevailing view that radio waves could travel through the air without needing a specific resonant medium.

It's worth noting that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were not universally accepted at the time. The development of radio technology in the early 20th century largely followed the principles of Hertzian wave theory, although Tesla's contributions to the field, including his work on high-frequency alternating currents and resonant circuits, were significant and have been recognized over time.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in the functioning of modern radio technology. Tesla's emphasis on resonant systems and earth currents has influenced various aspects of wireless communication, particularly in the development of resonant antennas and inductive coupling techniques.
