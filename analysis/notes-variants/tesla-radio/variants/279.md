Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of his conception of wireless communication and energy transmission.

Tesla's work on wireless technology was based on his belief in the existence of a continuous flow of energy through the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" or "terrestrial currents." He argued that these currents could be used for long-distance communication and power transmission without the need for physical wires.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was developed by Heinrich Hertz, described electromagnetic waves as propagating through space in a manner similar to light waves. While this theory was crucial in establishing the existence of electromagnetic waves, Tesla felt that it did not adequately explain the phenomena he observed and experimented with.

Tesla's views on the Hertzian wave theory were expressed in various writings and speeches throughout his career. For example, in 1904, he wrote in an article published in The Electrical Review:

"The Hertzian waves, as they are now called, are not the only means of transmitting electrical energy over a distance. There is another method, which I have been using for many years, and which I have found to be far more effective."

Tesla believed that his methods of wireless transmission were superior because they relied on the natural properties of the Earth and the ionosphere to carry energy and information, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about wireless technology were innovative and influential, the scientific community at the time did not fully accept his theories. The Hertzian wave theory eventually became the standard model for understanding electromagnetic radiation, and it has since been confirmed by numerous experiments and observations.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in different aspects of wireless communication and energy transmission. Modern wireless technologies often combine elements of both approaches, utilizing both ground-based and atmospheric propagation methods to achieve efficient and reliable communication.
