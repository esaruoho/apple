Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's primary concerns were rooted in the way Hertzian waves were being used to transmit information. He argued that these waves were too broad and lacked the precision needed for efficient and effective wireless communication. Tesla proposed that wireless transmission should be based on much more focused and directed energy, which he believed could achieve greater efficiency and range.

In his view, the key to successful wireless communication lay in the use of resonant circuits and the precise tuning of frequencies. This approach allowed for better control over the transmission and reception of signals, which he believed would lead to clearer and more reliable communication.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and contributed significantly to the development of modern radio technology. However, his theories were not widely accepted during his lifetime, and the practical applications of his work often relied on the principles of Hertzian waves rather than his own theories.

It's worth noting that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are still recognized today, even if some of his specific theories about the nature of electromagnetic radiation have not been fully embraced by the scientific community. His work laid the groundwork for many of the technologies we use today, including radio, television, and radar.
