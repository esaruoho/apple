Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates the widespread acceptance of Heinrich Hertz's discoveries, which laid the groundwork for understanding electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation as understood at the time was not sufficient to explain the phenomena of wireless transmission. He argued that the idea of sending signals through space via oscillating electric currents was incomplete and did not fully capture the nature of what he perceived as wireless energy transmission.

Tesla's approach emphasized the use of resonant circuits and the concept of resonance between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that the key to successful wireless communication lay in the precise tuning of these circuits to resonate at the same frequency, rather than simply transmitting electromagnetic waves over a wide range of frequencies.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, as it was understood at the time, focused more on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which could be detected by receiving antennas. Tesla felt that this approach was too broad and did not account for the specific resonant interactions that were necessary for efficient wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. It took some time for the scientific community to fully appreciate the importance of his contributions and to integrate them into the broader framework of electromagnetic theory.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play crucial roles in our understanding of wireless communication. Tesla's emphasis on resonance and tuned circuits is still relevant in modern wireless technologies, while Hertz's work on electromagnetic waves provided the foundational science that underpins much of our current understanding of wireless communication.
