Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his understanding of the propagation of electrical currents and his belief in the existence of a continuous, invisible medium through which these currents could travel without the need for a physical medium like air or vacuum. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through this medium, which he referred to as "space current."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the transmission of electromagnetic waves through space, which required a physical medium to propagate. Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication.

Some key points from Tesla's perspective include:

Space Current: Tesla believed that electrical currents could flow through the Earth and the atmosphere, forming a continuous circuit. This idea was central to his concept of wireless transmission, where signals could be sent without the need for a physical antenna or receiver.

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in wireless transmission. He believed that by tuning the frequency of the transmitter and receiver to resonate at the same frequency, efficient and long-range communication could be achieved.

Earth as a Conductor: Tesla viewed the Earth itself as a conductor that could carry electrical currents. He proposed using the Earth as a global antenna, which would allow for the transmission of signals over vast distances.

Wireless Power Transmission: Beyond just communication, Tesla also explored the possibility of transmitting power wirelessly, which further challenged the traditional view of electromagnetic waves.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. It was only after his death that many of his theories and inventions began to gain more recognition and validation.

It's important to note that modern science has largely integrated both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions into our understanding of electromagnetic waves and wireless communication. Today, we use a combination of principles from both approaches to achieve effective and efficient wireless technology.
