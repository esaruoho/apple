Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework developed by Hertz and others did not fully capture the essence of his own wireless technology and concepts.

Tesla's inventions and theories were based on a different approach to wireless communication, which he believed could transmit energy and information without the need for wires or the use of high-frequency oscillations. He argued that the traditional view of radio waves as requiring a line-of-sight transmission and being limited by the curvature of the Earth was overly restrictive and did not align with his experimental observations and theoretical models.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of what he called "resonance" between the transmitter and receiver, suggesting that the efficiency and range of wireless communication could be greatly enhanced if the frequencies used were harmonically related to the natural resonant frequencies of the receiving apparatus. This concept was quite different from the prevailing view of radio waves as simple electromagnetic disturbances that could propagate through space without such resonant interactions.

Tesla's ideas were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, but they have since been recognized as prescient and influential in the development of modern wireless technology. His work laid the groundwork for many of the principles used in today's wireless communications systems, including those based on resonance and frequency matching.

It's important to note that while Tesla's views were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, the electromagnetic radiation theory developed by Hertz and others is still a fundamental part of our understanding of how radio waves function. Both approaches have their merits and have contributed to the advancement of technology in different ways.
