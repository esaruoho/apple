Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to what he called "radiant energy" or "ether waves." He argued that the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space was not the primary mechanism for wireless communication, but rather that the Earth itself and the ionosphere played significant roles in transmitting signals.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the prevailing scientific consensus of his time, which was heavily influenced by the work of James Clerk Maxwell and others who had developed the theory of electromagnetic waves. Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and were not widely accepted until later, with advancements in technology and further research confirming many of his hypotheses.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not entirely accurate in all respects. Modern understanding of wireless communication is based on a combination of principles from both Tesla's and the Hertzian wave theories, among other contributions.
