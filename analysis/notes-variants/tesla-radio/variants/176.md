Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others, such as Heinrich Hertz. Tesla's views on the nature of wireless communication were quite different from those of his contemporaries.

Tesla believed that radio waves were not the only means of transmitting information through the air. He argued that the Earth itself could be used as a conductor for wireless signals, a concept he referred to as "earth currents" or "terrestrial currents." According to Tesla, these currents could carry information without the need for the high-frequency oscillations that Hertzian waves required.

In his experiments and writings, Tesla often criticized the Hertzian wave theory, which posited that radio waves traveled through space as waves of electromagnetic energy. Tesla contended that this theory was incomplete and did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication. He believed that the true mechanism involved the propagation of electrical currents through the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than just the transmission of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and were not widely accepted by the scientific community during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that Tesla's concept of using the Earth as a conductor for wireless signals is more aligned with how radio waves actually propagate in the real world. This includes phenomena such as ground wave propagation and skywave propagation, where radio waves interact with the Earth and the ionosphere.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and prescient, they were also complex and sometimes difficult to prove experimentally. Nonetheless, his contributions to the field of wireless communication have been significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers today.
