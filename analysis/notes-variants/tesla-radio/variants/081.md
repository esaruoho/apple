Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of his ideas about wireless transmission.

Tesla's conception of wireless technology was rooted in his belief that it could transmit information without the need for wires or conductive media. He argued that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the atmosphere. This idea was somewhat different from the prevailing view at the time, which focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space.

In his writings and public demonstrations, Tesla emphasized the importance of what he called "resonance" and "sympathetic vibration" in his wireless systems. He believed that by tuning the frequency of the transmitter and receiver to resonate with each other, he could achieve more efficient and longer-range communication. This approach contrasted with the then-popular notion of simply radiating energy in all directions and relying on the waves to find their way to the receiver.

Tesla's skepticism towards the Hertzian wave theory was partly due to his belief that it did not adequately explain the phenomena he observed and the results he achieved. However, it is important to note that modern scientific understanding has largely validated the principles underlying Hertzian waves, although Tesla's contributions to the field have also been recognized and appreciated.

Despite Tesla's reservations, the Hertzian wave theory eventually became the foundation for much of modern radio and telecommunications technology. Nonetheless, Tesla's work and theories continue to be studied and debated, offering insights into the early development of wireless communication.
