Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the essence of how wireless signals could be transmitted and received.

Tesla's views were rooted in his own experiments and theories, which emphasized the role of resonant circuits and the Earth's ionosphere in wireless communication. He argued that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves propagating through space in a vacuum-like manner was overly simplistic and did not account for the complex interactions between the signal and the environment.

In particular, Tesla contended that the idea of sending signals through the air as if they were traveling through a vacuum was not sufficient for practical wireless communication. He believed that the Earth itself played a crucial role in the transmission and reception of these signals, and that the signals would resonate with the Earth's natural frequencies. This concept is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that the Earth does indeed play a significant role in wireless communication, especially at lower frequencies. This aligns with Tesla's earlier observations and theories.

It's important to note that while Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are immense, the electromagnetic radiation theory has also been fundamental in developing our current understanding of wireless technology. Both perspectives have contributed to the advancement of the field, and modern wireless systems incorporate both principles.
