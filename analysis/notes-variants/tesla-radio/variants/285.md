Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of his wireless transmission methods and that it might even be misleading.

Tesla's work on radio communication was groundbreaking, but it was based on a different conceptual framework than what was widely accepted at the time. He emphasized the role of resonant circuits and the natural frequencies of the Earth and the ionosphere in his wireless systems. According to Tesla, these resonant phenomena were key to efficient and long-range communication without the need for line-of-sight transmission or high power levels.

The Hertzian wave theory, named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, focused more on the propagation of these waves through space. While this theory was crucial for understanding the physical behavior of electromagnetic waves, Tesla felt that it did not adequately explain the practical applications of wireless communication that he envisioned.

In particular, Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory did not account for the resonant properties of the Earth and the ionosphere, which he believed were essential for long-distance wireless transmission. He also criticized the reliance on high-power transmission, which he thought was inefficient and unnecessary.

It's important to note that Tesla's views were not universally accepted during his lifetime, and the scientific community largely embraced the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for radio communication. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless technology have been recognized over time, and his ideas continue to be studied and debated in the context of modern wireless communications.
