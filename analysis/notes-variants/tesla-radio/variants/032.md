Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the theories and experiments of his time were not fully capturing the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's concerns stemmed from several aspects:

Wireless Transmission: Tesla believed that wireless transmission could be achieved without the need for a physical medium like air or space. He thought that the Earth itself could serve as a conductor for wireless signals, a concept he referred to as "earth currents" or "terrestrial currents."

Coherers and Detectors: Tesla criticized the use of coherers and detectors in radio receivers, which were based on Hertz's findings. He believed these devices were not efficient and did not fully utilize the potential of wireless communication.

Frequency and Power: Tesla argued that the frequencies used in early radio experiments were too low and that higher frequencies would be more effective for long-distance transmission. Additionally, he emphasized the importance of high power in wireless transmission.

Interference and Interception: Tesla was concerned about the interference and interception of wireless signals, which he believed could be mitigated through better design and technology.

Philosophical Differences: There were also philosophical differences between Tesla and the scientific community of his time. Tesla's ideas often went beyond the conventional scientific understanding of the day, leading to disagreements.

Despite Tesla's criticisms, his contributions to the field of wireless communication were significant. His patents and demonstrations laid the groundwork for modern radio technology, even though some of his specific claims and methods were not widely accepted during his lifetime.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also ahead of their time in many respects. The full realization of his vision would come much later, with advancements in technology and further research into wireless communication.
