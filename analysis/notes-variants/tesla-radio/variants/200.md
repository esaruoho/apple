Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves propagating through space did not fully capture the essence of his wireless transmission methods.

Tesla's approach to wireless communication was based on the idea of utilizing the Earth and the ionosphere as conductors for energy transmission. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted without the need for a line-of-sight transmission medium. This concept is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonant antenna systems."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete description of how wireless communication could work. Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory did not account for the way his systems worked, particularly the ability to transmit signals over long distances without the need for a direct line-of-sight path.

Tesla's claims were controversial at the time and have been subject to much debate among scientists and historians. While modern technology has largely validated the principles of Hertzian waves, it is also true that Tesla's ideas about earth resonance and wireless transmission have found some support in recent research and applications.

It's important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were significant, even if his specific theories about the nature of wireless transmission were not universally accepted during his lifetime. His work laid the groundwork for many modern technologies, including radio and wireless communication systems.
