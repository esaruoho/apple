Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or electromagnetic waves propagating through space, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than through the propagation of electromagnetic waves in free space. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept known as "earth resonance." This idea was encapsulated in his patents and described in his writings, where he argued that the Earth could be used as a conductor to transmit and receive signals without the need for the high-frequency oscillations required by the Hertzian wave theory.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was widely accepted at the time, suggested that electromagnetic waves could travel through the air and were necessary for wireless communication. Tesla's experiments and theories challenged this view, leading him to develop methods for wireless transmission that did not rely on the propagation of these waves.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved to incorporate both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions, recognizing that wireless signals can be transmitted through various mediums including the Earth and the ionosphere, as well as through the air using electromagnetic waves.
