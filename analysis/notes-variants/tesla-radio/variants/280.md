Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and disagreement with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a different mechanism than simple electromagnetic radiation. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonant antenna." According to Tesla, this method allowed for more efficient and longer-range communication without the need for high-frequency oscillations that were characteristic of Hertzian waves.

In his experiments and writings, Tesla described how he could transmit and receive signals over vast distances using low-frequency currents that resonated with the Earth. This approach contrasted sharply with the then-popular view that radio waves required high frequencies to travel long distances effectively.

While Tesla's ideas about earth resonance were innovative and have been subject to modern scientific scrutiny, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. The practical success of radio technology based on Hertzian waves led to their widespread adoption, and Tesla's theories were largely sidelined.

It's important to note that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, even if his specific theories about earth resonance did not gain widespread acceptance. His work laid the groundwork for much of modern telecommunications, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers exploring new methods of wireless communication.
