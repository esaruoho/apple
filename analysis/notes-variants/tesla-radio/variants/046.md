Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of his conception of wireless communication and energy transmission.

Tesla's work on radio and wireless technology predates the widespread acceptance of the Hertzian wave theory. He conducted experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and developed devices such as the Tesla coil, which he used to demonstrate the transmission of electrical energy without wires. Tesla argued that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space in a manner described by Hertz did not accurately represent the phenomena he observed and experienced.

In particular, Tesla believed that the Hertzian wave theory focused too much on the propagation of waves through space and not enough on the inherent properties of the medium itself. He suggested that the wireless transmission of information and energy could be achieved through a more direct interaction between the transmitter and receiver, rather than relying solely on the propagation of waves.

Tesla's views were controversial at the time and continue to be debated among historians and scientists. While Hertzian waves are now understood to be a fundamental part of wireless communication, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire discussions about the nature of wireless technology and its underlying principles.
