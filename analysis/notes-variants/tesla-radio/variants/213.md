Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates many of the developments attributed to Guglielmo Marconi, who is often credited with the invention of radio. However, Tesla's claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory being inimical to the proper understanding of wireless communication were rooted in his own theoretical framework and practical experiences.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it. He argued that the idea of transmitting signals through space as waves was not sufficient to explain the phenomena he observed and experienced in his experiments. Tesla emphasized the importance of a more holistic approach to wireless communication, which included factors such as the Earth's ionosphere, the ground effect, and the use of resonant circuits.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described methods for wireless transmission that involved resonant circuits and the manipulation of the Earth's natural resonances. He believed that these methods could achieve more efficient and longer-range communication than the wave-based theories of the time. Tesla's approach was based on the idea of resonant coupling between transmitter and receiver, which he thought could be more effective than simply radiating energy into the air.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted at the time. The scientific community and industry were still developing and refining the concepts of electromagnetic waves and wireless communication. Over time, the work of scientists like Oliver Heaviside and others helped to refine the understanding of electromagnetic waves, eventually leading to the widespread adoption of wave-based models for radio communication.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and the wave-based approaches have their merits and applications in different contexts. Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers working on new technologies in this domain.
