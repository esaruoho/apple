Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory (which he sometimes referred to as the "Hertzian wave theory") were part of a broader debate within the scientific community.

Tesla believed that the concept of radio waves as simply electromagnetic waves traveling through space was inadequate for fully explaining or utilizing wireless communication. He argued that there was more to wireless transmission than just the propagation of these waves. Tesla's views were based on his experiments and theories, which included concepts such as resonance between transmitter and receiver, and the idea that wireless signals could be transmitted without relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

Some key points of Tesla's critique include:

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that wireless signals could be transmitted more effectively if they resonated at the same frequency as the receiving device.

Earth's Role: Tesla proposed that the Earth itself played a crucial role in wireless transmission. He suggested that the Earth could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it, rather than just through the air.

Coherence: Tesla argued that the signals should be coherent, meaning they should maintain their integrity over long distances. This was in contrast to the view that signals would naturally dissipate over distance.

Interference: Tesla was concerned about interference from other sources, which could disrupt wireless transmissions. He believed that proper design and engineering could mitigate this issue.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of modern wireless technology, they were not universally accepted at the time. The electromagnetic radiation theory, as developed by Heinrich Hertz and further refined by others, provided a robust framework for understanding and applying wireless communication. However, Tesla's contributions and his emphasis on certain aspects of wireless transmission have been recognized and continue to influence modern research and applications in wireless technology.

It's worth noting that Tesla's views were often controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. It took some time for many of his ideas to be validated and integrated into the broader understanding of wireless communication.
