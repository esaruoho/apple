Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's contributions to wireless technology were revolutionary, but his views on the nature of electromagnetic waves differed from those of other scientists, most notably Heinrich Hertz.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves as understood at the time did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication. He argued that these waves were too diffuse and spread out over large areas, making them unsuitable for practical wireless transmission. Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through a medium that would allow for more focused and efficient communication.

One of Tesla's key points was that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and a medium for wireless transmission. This idea is now known as Earth-ionosphere waveguide, which suggests that radio waves can travel along the surface of the Earth and through the ionosphere, a layer of the atmosphere rich in ions and free electrons. Tesla believed that this natural resonance between the Earth and the ionosphere could be harnessed for wireless communication, which he demonstrated with his experiments and inventions.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was gaining acceptance among scientists, suggested that radio waves could propagate through space without needing a physical medium. While this theory eventually proved to be correct in many respects, Tesla felt that it did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and experienced in his experiments.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas were ahead of their time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that some aspects of Tesla's theories were prescient. For example, the concept of using the Earth as a conductor for wireless communication aligns with contemporary understanding of ground wave propagation.

In summary, Nikola Tesla's claims about the inappropriateness of the electromagnetic radiation theory for understanding wireless processes were rooted in his belief that wireless communication could be more effectively achieved through the natural resonances and conductive properties of the Earth and ionosphere, rather than through the diffusive propagation of Hertzian waves.
