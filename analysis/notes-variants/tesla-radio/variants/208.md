Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as localized disturbances propagating through space, did not fully capture the essence of his vision for wireless communication.

Tesla's conception of wireless transmission was rooted in his belief that energy could be transmitted without the need for physical conductors or wires. He argued that the Earth itself could act as a conductor, and that energy could be transmitted through the Earth's natural resonant frequencies. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was based on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, did not align with Tesla's views. Tesla contended that the Hertzian wave theory was overly focused on the transmission of energy through space as a wave, rather than through the Earth's natural properties. He believed that this focus led to an incomplete understanding of the true nature of wireless communication.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology. However, his theories were not widely accepted during his lifetime due to the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. It wasn't until later that advancements in technology and further research confirmed aspects of Tesla's ideas, particularly in the realm of long-distance wireless communication and Earth resonance.

Today, while we understand that both Hertzian waves and Earth resonance play roles in wireless communication, Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the development of new technologies.
