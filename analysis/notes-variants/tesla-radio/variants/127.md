Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves propagating through space did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct interaction between the transmitter and receiver, often referred to as "resonance" or "sympathetic vibration." He argued that the energy was not simply radiated out into space but rather that there was a more intimate connection between the transmitting and receiving devices.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory suggested that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space without requiring a physical medium, much like sound waves can travel through air. Tesla felt that this approach was too abstract and did not adequately explain the phenomena he observed and experimented with.

This disagreement is part of the broader debate in the early days of wireless technology between those who favored a more direct, resonant approach and those who embraced the wave theory. Tesla's work on resonance and his development of tuned circuits and resonant antennas were central to his claims about wireless communication, which he demonstrated in various experiments and inventions, including his Wardenclyffe Tower project.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also at odds with the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. Modern physics has largely reconciled these perspectives, recognizing both the importance of resonance and the wave nature of electromagnetic radiation in wireless communication.
