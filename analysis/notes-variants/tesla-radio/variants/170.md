Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory, in explaining his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the theory did not fully capture the essence of how wireless signals could be transmitted and received without the need for a physical conductor.

Tesla's views were rooted in his experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and his development of resonant circuits. He argued that the transmission of radio waves required a much more efficient and less dissipative method than what was described by the Hertzian wave theory. According to Tesla, the theory focused too much on the idea of waves propagating through space and not enough on the resonant properties of the medium through which the signal traveled.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of resonant coupling between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that if the receiver was tuned to resonate at the same frequency as the transmitter, it would be able to efficiently absorb the energy being transmitted, even over long distances. This concept is now understood as the basis for resonant inductive coupling, which is used in various modern technologies such as wireless charging pads.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and contributed significantly to the development of radio technology. However, his theories were not widely accepted during his lifetime, and it was not until later that many of his concepts were validated and incorporated into the field of wireless communication. Today, we recognize Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless technology and appreciate the foundational role his work played in the development of modern radio and telecommunications systems.
