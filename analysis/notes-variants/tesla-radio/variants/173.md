Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views can be summarized as follows:

Coherence vs. Incoherence: Tesla argued that the wireless transmission of information should be done using coherent waves, which he believed could carry more information and be more efficient. He saw the incoherent waves described by Hertz as less effective for long-distance communication.

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in wireless communication. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a resonant medium, allowing signals to travel vast distances without the need for high power or large antennas. This concept is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance principle."

Frequency and Power: Tesla also believed that the frequency and power of the transmitted signal were crucial. He advocated for lower frequencies and higher power, which he thought would allow for better penetration and transmission over longer distances.

Interference and Noise: Tesla was concerned about the interference and noise that could arise from the use of incoherent waves. He believed that coherent waves would minimize these issues and allow for clearer communication.

Philosophical Differences: Beyond technical disagreements, there were philosophical differences between Tesla and the scientific community of his time. Tesla was a proponent of free energy and believed that wireless technology could be used to transmit energy as well as information, which was not widely accepted at the time.

Despite these disagreements, Tesla's work laid the foundation for modern wireless communication. His inventions, such as the Tesla coil and his experiments with radio waves, contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology. However, his ideas about coherent waves and earth resonance were not widely adopted until much later, with advancements in technology making them more feasible.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and influential, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The scientific community largely embraced the principles of Hertzian waves, which formed the basis for early radio technology.
