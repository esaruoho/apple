Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as electromagnetic waves propagating through space, did not adequately describe the way wireless signals could be transmitted and received.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just empty space. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept now known as "earth resonance." This idea was based on his observations of how electrical currents could travel through the ground and the air, and how they could be used to transmit information without the need for physical wires.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, which was a more limited and less intuitive concept for many at the time. Tesla's approach emphasized the use of resonant circuits and the natural properties of the Earth to enhance signal transmission and reception.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. Modern science has since confirmed that both Hertzian waves and Earth resonance play roles in wireless communication, but Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, particularly in the realm of wireless technology and the understanding of electromagnetic phenomena.
