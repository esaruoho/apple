Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework proposed by Hertz and others did not fully capture the essence of his wireless transmission methods.

Tesla argued that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves propagating through space in a manner similar to light waves did not accurately describe the way his systems worked. He contended that his systems involved a more direct form of energy transmission, often referred to as "resonance," where the transmitter and receiver were tuned to resonate at the same frequency, allowing for efficient transfer of energy without the need for the waves to travel through the air.

Tesla's views were based on his experimental observations and theoretical considerations. He believed that the traditional wave theory could not account for phenomena such as the long-distance transmission of signals without the use of a line-of-sight path, which would be required if the signals were simply electromagnetic waves. Instead, Tesla suggested that there was a form of "wireless" communication that did not rely solely on the propagation of waves but rather on the resonance between the transmitter and receiver.

It is important to note that Tesla's ideas were ahead of his time and were not widely accepted during his lifetime. Modern physics has since provided explanations for many of the phenomena Tesla observed, including the role of resonant circuits and the behavior of electromagnetic fields in free space. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his skepticism towards the wave theory contributed to ongoing debates and research in the area of electromagnetic field theory and wireless technology.
