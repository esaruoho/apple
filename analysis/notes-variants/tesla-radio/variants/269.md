Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that his own experiments and observations suggested that radio waves were not simply a form of electromagnetic radiation but had unique properties that required a different theoretical framework.

Tesla's views were based on his extensive work with high-frequency alternating currents and his experiments with wireless transmission. He argued that the propagation of radio waves could be achieved through a medium that did not require the oscillations of electric and magnetic fields propagating through space, as described by the Hertzian wave theory. Instead, Tesla proposed that radio waves could be transmitted through the Earth or the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" or "space currents."

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the prevailing scientific consensus of his time, which was heavily influenced by Heinrich Hertz's discoveries. Tesla believed that the Hertzian wave theory was incomplete and did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication. He emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the role of the Earth in transmitting signals, which were concepts that were not fully understood or accepted at the time.

It's worth noting that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of radio technology, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. It took several decades for scientists and engineers to fully understand and validate many of Tesla's theories and experimental findings. Today, while some aspects of Tesla's work have been confirmed, others remain subjects of ongoing debate and research.
