Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the nature of radio waves were quite different from those of Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with proving the existence of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves traveling through space was not an accurate representation of how wireless communication could be achieved. He argued that these waves were too weak and dissipative for practical long-distance communication. Instead, Tesla proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept known as "earth resonance."

In his patents and public statements, Tesla described his methods of wireless transmission as involving the use of resonant circuits and the manipulation of the Earth's natural resonances. He believed that by creating resonant circuits at specific frequencies, one could transmit energy without relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air.

Tesla's views on this matter were controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, his ideas have since gained more recognition, especially with the advent of modern technologies such as ground-penetrating radar and other forms of electromagnetic resonance.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories and methods were innovative and influential, they did not replace the established electromagnetic wave theory. Instead, they complemented it, offering alternative ways to understand and utilize wireless communication. Modern research continues to explore various aspects of Tesla's ideas, including the potential for using the Earth as a medium for wireless signal transmission.
