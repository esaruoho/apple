Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his own experiments and theories regarding wireless transmission. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept often referred to as "Earth resonance" or "resonant antenna." According to Tesla, the Earth could act as a conductor for these signals, allowing them to travel vast distances without the need for a line-of-sight transmission medium.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete description of how wireless communication actually worked. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through the air was too simplistic and did not account for the complex interactions between the signal and the environment.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology. However, they also led to some controversy and misunderstanding among his contemporaries. Despite the skepticism, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are undeniable, and his work continues to influence the development of modern technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular networks.
