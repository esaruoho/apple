Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (named after Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's primary concerns were rooted in the idea that the transmission of radio waves could be achieved without relying on the propagation of these waves through the air. He proposed that radio waves could be transmitted through the Earth itself or through the ionosphere, which is part of the upper atmosphere where the air is ionized by the sun's radiation. This concept is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory primarily focused on the transmission of electromagnetic waves through space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete description of how wireless communication could work. Tesla believed that the Earth and the ionosphere could act as conductors and resonators for radio waves, allowing for more efficient and widespread transmission of signals.

Tesla's views were not widely accepted at the time, and the Hertzian wave theory became the dominant explanation for radio wave propagation. However, his ideas about earth resonance and wireless transmission have since gained recognition, especially in the context of modern technologies such as longwave broadcasting and certain forms of underground communication systems.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and influential, they were also complex and sometimes difficult to prove experimentally. Nonetheless, his contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire research into alternative methods of signal transmission.
