Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding what is now known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space in a manner similar to those described by Heinrich Hertz did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and intimate interaction between the transmitter and receiver, often referred to as "resonance" or "sympathetic vibration." He argued that the energy was transferred through a medium that was not simply empty space but rather a resonant system involving the Earth and the ionosphere.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which describes electromagnetic waves as propagating through space without requiring a medium, did not align with Tesla's understanding of how wireless communication worked. Tesla contended that the Hertzian theory oversimplified the process and did not account for the unique properties of wireless transmission that he observed and utilized.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about resonance and wireless communication were innovative and have since been validated in various forms, the Hertzian wave theory remains a fundamental part of our understanding of electromagnetic radiation and wireless communication. Modern technology has built upon both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions to develop the sophisticated systems we use today.
