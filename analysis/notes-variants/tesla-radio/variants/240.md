Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being proposed by the prevailing scientific community at the time. He argued that the transmission of information through the air was not simply a matter of sending out waves that could be detected by receiving antennas. Instead, he thought that there was a more fundamental way of transmitting energy and information that did not rely on the propagation of waves alone.

One of Tesla's key points was that the wireless transmission of information should be possible without the need for a line-of-sight or physical medium to carry the signal. This idea was somewhat ahead of its time and was not widely accepted until later developments in technology, such as the discovery of the ionosphere and advancements in antenna design.

Tesla's skepticism towards the Hertzian wave theory was part of a broader debate about the nature of wireless communication. While the Hertzian wave theory provided a framework for understanding how electromagnetic waves could be transmitted and received, Tesla felt that this model was incomplete and did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and experimented with.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas were not entirely dismissed; they contributed to the ongoing development of wireless technology. However, the practical applications of wireless communication that emerged in the early 20th century were based more on the principles of Hertzian waves than on Tesla's theories. Nonetheless, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his work continues to inspire researchers and engineers today.
