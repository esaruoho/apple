Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as proposed by Heinrich Hertz. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as radio waves propagating through space, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views can be summarized as follows:

Wireless Transmission: Tesla believed in a more direct form of wireless transmission, often referred to as "resonance" or "resonant coupling." He thought that wireless signals could be transmitted without the need for the physical propagation of electromagnetic waves through the air. Instead, he proposed that signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself or through the ionosphere, depending on the frequency and the nature of the signal.

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance between the transmitter and receiver. He believed that if the transmitter and receiver were properly tuned to resonate at the same frequency, they could communicate effectively without the need for the electromagnetic waves to travel through the air. This idea is somewhat similar to the concept of resonant antennas but operates on a different principle.

Criticism of Hertzian Waves: Tesla criticized the concept of Hertzian waves as being too broad and not specific enough to explain the phenomena of wireless communication. He argued that the theory did not account for the selective nature of wireless signals and their ability to travel over long distances without significant loss.

Experiments and Demonstrations: Tesla conducted numerous experiments to demonstrate his ideas. For example, he demonstrated wireless power transmission and communication over significant distances using resonant circuits. One famous demonstration was the Wardenclyffe Tower project in New York, where Tesla attempted to transmit electrical energy and information across the Atlantic Ocean.

Patents and Controversies: Tesla's ideas and inventions sometimes led to patent disputes with other inventors and companies. His claims about the nature of wireless communication were often at odds with those of Guglielmo Marconi, who used Hertzian waves to develop practical radio communication systems.

While Tesla's theories and experiments were groundbreaking and influential, they also faced criticism and skepticism from the scientific community of his time. The widespread adoption of Hertzian waves for radio communication eventually overshadowed Tesla's alternative theories, although his contributions to the field remain significant.

It's important to note that modern understanding of wireless communication incorporates elements from both Tesla's and Hertz's theories, reflecting the complex and evolving nature of scientific discovery and technological advancement.
