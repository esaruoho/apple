Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a different mechanism than simple electromagnetic radiation. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonance method." According to Tesla, this method allowed for more efficient and longer-range communication without the need for high-frequency oscillations and large antennas.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was widely accepted at the time, suggested that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space and be received by antennas. Tesla argued that this theory was incomplete and that it did not account for the phenomena he observed and experimented with, such as long-distance wireless transmission and the effects of the Earth's ionosphere on radio waves.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about earth resonance were innovative and have since been recognized as contributing to our understanding of electromagnetic phenomena, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. Modern science has largely confirmed the validity of Hertzian waves for wireless communication, but Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, particularly in the realm of alternating current (AC) technology and the development of early radio systems.
