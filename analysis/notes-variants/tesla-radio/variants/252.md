Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied by others during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission and his claims about the nature of radio waves were quite different from those of Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with the discovery of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation as understood at the time was not sufficient to explain the phenomena he observed and experienced in his experiments. He argued that the transmission of information and energy over distances could be achieved through a medium that was fundamentally different from what Hertzian waves implied. Tesla proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through the Earth's ionosphere, a concept now known as "earth resonance."

In his patents and public statements, Tesla described methods for transmitting electrical energy without wires, which he believed could be done through the natural resonant properties of the Earth and the ionosphere. This idea was quite revolutionary at the time and did not align with the then-prevailing view that radio waves were simply electromagnetic waves propagating through space.

Tesla's views on this matter were controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, modern research has shown that Tesla's ideas about the Earth's role in wireless communication have some validity. For example, the concept of using the Earth as a conductor for radio frequency currents is now understood in the context of ground wave propagation and has applications in various forms of wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and ahead of their time, they were also complex and sometimes difficult to prove conclusively with the technology available at the time. Nonetheless, his contributions to the field of wireless communication and his skepticism towards the purely radiative model of electromagnetic waves have continued to influence discussions and research in the area of wireless technology.
