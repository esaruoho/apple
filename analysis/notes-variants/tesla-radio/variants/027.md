Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as being transmitted through space without wires, did not accurately represent the nature of his inventions and discoveries.

Tesla's primary concerns with the Hertzian wave theory included:

Wireless Transmission: Tesla believed that wireless transmission could be achieved without relying on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He proposed that energy could be transmitted through the Earth itself, a concept known as "Earth resonance" or "resonant radiation."

Efficiency and Range: The Hertzian wave theory suggested that signals would spread out and weaken over distance, making long-range communication difficult. Tesla's approach aimed to overcome these limitations by focusing on specific frequencies and resonant circuits.

Interference: Tesla worried that the widespread adoption of Hertzian waves might lead to interference and signal degradation, which could undermine the reliability of wireless communication systems.

Philosophical Differences: There were also philosophical differences between Tesla's ideas and those of other scientists who supported the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla saw wireless communication as a means to transmit information and energy without the need for physical connections, whereas others focused more on the technical aspects of wave propagation.

Despite these disagreements, it is important to note that Tesla's work laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology. While his theories about Earth resonance and resonant radiation were not widely accepted during his lifetime, they have since been validated in part by experiments and research. Today, we understand that both wired and wireless technologies can coexist and complement each other in various applications.

In summary, Tesla's claims about the inappropriateness of the Hertzian wave theory were rooted in his belief that wireless communication could be achieved through different principles than those described by the prevailing scientific understanding of his time.
