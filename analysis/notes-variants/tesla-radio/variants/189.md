Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his work with high-frequency alternating currents and his experiments with wireless transmission. He argued that the idea of propagating signals through space as waves was not sufficient to explain the phenomena he observed and experienced. Tesla believed that there was more to wireless communication than just the transmission of electromagnetic waves; he thought that there was a direct connection between the transmitter and the receiver, often referred to as "resonance" or "sympathetic induction."

In his writings and lectures, Tesla described his wireless system as involving a form of energy transmission that was not dependent on the propagation of waves but rather on the resonance between the transmitter and the receiver. This view was somewhat controversial at the time and led to disagreements with other scientists and engineers who were working within the framework of Hertzian wave theory.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The scientific community eventually came to understand and accept many of the principles that Tesla had proposed, although the exact mechanisms of wireless communication remain complex and are still subjects of ongoing research and debate.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in modern wireless technology. Tesla's contributions to the development of wireless communication have been recognized, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the field.
