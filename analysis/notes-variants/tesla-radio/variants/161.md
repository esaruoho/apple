Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla's work on radio and wireless technology was groundbreaking, but his views on the electromagnetic radiation theory were quite different from those of his contemporaries.

Tesla believed that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space, as described by Heinrich Hertz, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through a vacuum was an oversimplification and did not accurately represent the way wireless signals could be transmitted and received.

Tesla's conception of wireless transmission involved a more active role for the transmitter and receiver. He believed that the transmitter could directly influence the receiver without the need for a medium like air or water. This view was based on his experiments with resonant circuits and his belief in the existence of a "wireless" medium that could carry signals over long distances without the need for physical conductors.

In contrast, the electromagnetic wave theory proposed by Hertz suggested that radio waves could travel through space without a physical medium. While this theory was correct in many respects, Tesla felt it did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and experimented with.

It's important to note that Tesla's views were not universally accepted at the time, and the electromagnetic wave theory eventually became the standard model for understanding radio waves. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication and his skepticism towards the wave theory have been recognized and studied extensively in the history of science.
