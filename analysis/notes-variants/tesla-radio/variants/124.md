Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to aether or some form of invisible matter that could carry electrical currents without the need for physical waves. He argued that the idea of electromagnetic waves traveling through space was too simplistic and did not account for the phenomena he observed, such as the ability to transmit signals over long distances without the need for a line-of-sight path.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the prevailing scientific understanding of the time, which was heavily influenced by the work of James Clerk Maxwell and others. He believed that the true mechanism of wireless transmission involved aether currents or some other form of subtle energy that could propagate signals without the need for the oscillating electric and magnetic fields described by Hertzian waves.

It is important to note that Tesla's ideas were not widely accepted at the time and were met with significant skepticism from the scientific community. However, his contributions to the field of wireless communication and his advocacy for alternative theories have been recognized and appreciated in modern times. Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in the functioning of wireless technology, but Tesla's emphasis on the role of aether-like phenomena has not been substantiated by contemporary physics.
