Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be summarized as follows:

Wireless Transmission vs. Hertzian Waves: Tesla argued that his system of wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method of energy transfer compared to what he perceived as the indirect and less effective method of Hertzian waves. He believed that his system could transmit energy over long distances without the need for a physical medium like wires.

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in his wireless transmission system. He believed that by tuning the receiving and transmitting devices to resonate at the same frequency, he could achieve more efficient and longer-range communication. This resonant coupling was a key aspect of his patents and demonstrations.

Incompatibility with Hertzian Waves: Tesla contended that the prevailing theories of Hertzian waves did not adequately explain the phenomena he observed and experienced. He felt that these theories were too focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space and did not account for the direct energy transfer that he believed was possible.

Patent Disputes: Tesla's views also played a role in patent disputes. For instance, the Marconi Wireless Telegraph Company faced challenges from Tesla over patents related to wireless communication. Tesla's claims about the incompatibility of Hertzian waves with his conception of wireless transmission contributed to these disputes.

Public Demonstrations: Tesla often demonstrated his wireless transmission capabilities in public, such as by lighting bulbs without wires or sending signals across large distances. These demonstrations were intended to showcase the superiority of his methods over those based solely on Hertzian waves.

It is important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, the scientific community eventually came to understand that both his methods and the Hertzian wave theory had valid applications in wireless communication. Modern technology has integrated both approaches, recognizing the strengths of each in different contexts.
