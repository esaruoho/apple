Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being described by the prevailing theories of the time. He argued that the transmission of information through the air was not simply a matter of sending out waves that could be detected by receiving antennas, but rather involved a more complex interaction with the Earth itself.

In his view, the Earth acted as a conductor and played a crucial role in the transmission and reception of signals. This idea is encapsulated in his concept of "earth currents" or "resonant circuits," which he believed were essential for efficient wireless communication. Tesla's experiments and patents often focused on these principles, which differed significantly from the then-popular notion of radio waves propagating through space without the need for a conductive medium.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and prescient, they were also ahead of their time in many respects. The technology and understanding of electromagnetism at the turn of the 20th century were still developing, and it took some time for the scientific community to fully appreciate and validate Tesla's contributions to wireless communication. However, his work laid the groundwork for much of modern radio and telecommunications technology.
