Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on wireless transmission was based on his belief in the existence of aether or "radiant energy," which he thought could be used for long-distance communication without the need for wires.

The electromagnetic radiation theory, as proposed by Heinrich Hertz, suggested that radio waves could propagate through space without a medium, which contradicted Tesla's ideas about the necessity of a medium for these waves to travel. Tesla believed that radio waves required a medium, such as the Earth itself, to transmit information. This is why he often referred to his system as "wireless" rather than "radio."

Tesla's claims that the electromagnetic radiation theory was inimical to the proper understanding of the wireless process were rooted in his belief that the theory did not fully capture the nature of how his systems worked. He argued that his methods involved a different mechanism of energy transmission, one that was more efficient and less dependent on the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory as understood at the time.

It's important to note that while Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were groundbreaking, the electromagnetic radiation theory eventually became the accepted framework for understanding radio waves. However, Tesla's insights and experiments continue to be studied and appreciated for their innovative approach to the subject.
