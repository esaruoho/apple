Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless communication could be achieved through aetheric or longitudinal waves, which he thought were different from the transverse waves described by Hertz. He argued that the transverse wave theory, which was the prevailing explanation for electromagnetic radiation at the time, did not adequately account for the phenomena he observed and experienced with his own inventions, such as the Wardenclyffe Tower.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the transverse wave theory, suggesting that it was an oversimplification and that it did not fully explain the propagation of energy through space without wires. He proposed that there was a more fundamental way to understand and utilize wireless communication, which he believed involved aetheric currents or longitudinal waves.

It's important to note that Tesla's ideas were not widely accepted during his lifetime, and the scientific community largely adopted the transverse wave theory as the correct description of electromagnetic radiation. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his work continues to inspire researchers and engineers to this day.
