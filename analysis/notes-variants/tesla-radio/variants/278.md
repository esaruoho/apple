Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves, as described by Heinrich Hertz, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he understood it.

Tesla's work on wireless technology was based on his belief in a system that could transmit energy and information without the need for wires or conductive media. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor, allowing for the propagation of signals through the atmosphere and the ground. This concept is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonant antenna systems."

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which describes electromagnetic waves propagating through space, does not account for the idea that the Earth can be used as a medium for signal transmission. Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was incomplete and that it did not fully explain how his systems worked.

Some key points in Tesla's arguments include:

Resonance: Tesla believed that the Earth and the ionosphere could resonate at specific frequencies, allowing for efficient transmission and reception of signals. This concept is different from the simple propagation of electromagnetic waves.

Long-distance Communication: Tesla claimed that his systems could transmit signals over vast distances without the need for a line-of-sight path, which is a fundamental requirement for Hertzian waves.

Energy Transmission: Tesla also proposed that his systems could transmit electrical energy wirelessly, which goes beyond mere communication.

Interference: Tesla believed that the Hertzian wave theory did not adequately address the issue of interference and that his methods were less susceptible to such problems.

While Tesla's ideas were innovative and have been influential in the development of modern wireless technology, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. It wasn't until much later that some of his concepts, particularly those related to resonant systems, began to gain more traction in scientific and engineering circles.

It's important to note that Tesla's claims were often met with skepticism and controversy. Many of his experiments and theories were ahead of their time, and it took decades for some of his ideas to be validated or incorporated into mainstream technology. Today, many aspects of wireless communication, including long-range transmission and energy transfer, are based on principles that align with Tesla's original vision, albeit with a deeper understanding of the underlying physics.
