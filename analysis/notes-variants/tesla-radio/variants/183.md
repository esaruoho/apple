Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's views were rooted in his own experimental work and theoretical framework, which differed from the then-popular theories proposed by Heinrich Hertz.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the idea of waves propagating through space without a medium (vacuum) was an oversimplification and did not accurately represent the true nature of wireless transmission.

Tesla's conception of wireless technology was more aligned with the idea of "radiant energy" or "ether waves," which he believed could be transmitted through the Earth itself. He proposed that wireless signals could travel through the ground and the air, rather than relying solely on atmospheric waves. This view was based on his extensive experiments with resonant circuits and his belief in the Earth's natural electrical properties.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the Hertzian wave theory for its limitations and for not fully explaining the phenomena he observed. He emphasized the importance of resonance and the role of the Earth in transmitting and receiving wireless signals. Tesla's approach was more holistic, considering the entire system of transmitter, receiver, and the medium between them (the Earth) as a single entity.

It's worth noting that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not widely accepted at the time. The scientific community eventually came to accept the electromagnetic radiation theory as a valid explanation for wireless communication, although Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant and continue to be studied and appreciated today.
