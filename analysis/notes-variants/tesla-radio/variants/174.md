Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves as understood at the time did not fully capture the essence of his vision for wireless communication.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that there was a subtle, invisible connection between objects that could facilitate the transmission of information and energy without the need for physical waves. This concept is often referred to as "resonance" or "sympathetic vibration," where the Earth itself could act as a conductor and a resonator for wireless signals.

In contrast, the prevailing scientific understanding at the time, based on Hertz's experiments, focused on the transmission of electromagnetic waves through space. Tesla argued that this approach was limited and did not fully explain the phenomena he observed and utilized in his inventions, such as the Wardenclyffe Tower project.

It's important to note that Tesla's views were not universally accepted during his lifetime, and the scientific community largely embraced the electromagnetic wave theory as the basis for wireless communication. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless technology, including his patents and demonstrations, have been recognized and continue to influence modern wireless communication systems.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in different aspects of wireless technology. Modern wireless communication systems utilize both the propagation of electromagnetic waves and the principles of resonance and sympathetic vibration, among other techniques.
