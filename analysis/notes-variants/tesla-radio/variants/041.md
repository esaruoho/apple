Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of his conception of wireless communication. He argued that the Hertzian waves, as described by Heinrich Hertz, were not the same as the "radiant energy" he used in his experiments and theories.

Tesla's views on this matter can be summarized as follows:

Difference Between Radiant Energy and Hertzian Waves: Tesla believed that the "radiant energy" he used in his experiments was fundamentally different from the Hertzian waves described by Hertz. He thought that Hertzian waves were too weak and did not carry the same power or information-carrying capacity that he needed for long-distance communication.

Coherence and Directionality: Tesla emphasized the importance of coherence and directionality in his wireless systems. He believed that the energy he transmitted was coherent and could be directed, whereas Hertzian waves were considered to be incoherent and diffusive.

Energy Transmission: Tesla argued that his system involved the transmission of energy through the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than through the air as Hertzian waves suggested. This idea is encapsulated in his concept of "earth resonance," where he believed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and transmitter of electrical energy.

Patent Disputes: Tesla's claims often stemmed from patent disputes with Guglielmo Marconi, who was using Hertzian waves for wireless telegraphy. Tesla felt that Marconi's methods were not as efficient or as capable of long-distance communication as his own system.

Scientific Debate: The debate between Tesla and proponents of the Hertzian wave theory was part of a broader scientific discussion about the nature of electromagnetic radiation and its applications. While Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not universally accepted at the time.

It's important to note that modern science has largely validated the principles underlying Hertzian waves, but Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, even if his specific theories about the nature of electromagnetic radiation were not entirely accurate.
