Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves as understood at the time did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla argued that his system of wireless transmission involved a different mechanism than what was being described by the prevailing scientific theories of the day. He emphasized the importance of resonance and the ability of his devices to transmit energy without the need for a physical medium like air or space. This resonant approach was central to his ideas about long-distance wireless power transmission and communication.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was based on the idea of electromagnetic waves propagating through space, did not account for the specific phenomena Tesla observed and utilized. Tesla's experiments often involved creating resonant circuits that could transmit and receive signals over significant distances, which he believed could be achieved without the need for the high-frequency oscillations that were characteristic of Hertzian waves.

It's important to note that while Tesla's claims were innovative and sometimes controversial, they also contributed significantly to the development of modern wireless technology. The debate between Tesla's ideas and the established Hertzian wave theory was part of the broader scientific discourse of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and it helped shape the future of wireless communication and electrical engineering.
