Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his work with high-frequency alternating currents and his experiments with wireless transmission. He argued that the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space was not the primary mechanism for transmitting information wirelessly. Instead, he proposed that wireless communication could be achieved through a form of resonance between the transmitter and receiver, without the need for the waves to travel through the air.

In his view, the key to wireless communication was not the transmission of waves but the establishment of a resonant circuit between the transmitter and receiver. This resonant circuit would allow for the transfer of energy and information without the need for the waves to propagate through the medium. Tesla believed that this approach would be more efficient and effective than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not widely accepted at the time. The scientific community generally embraced the electromagnetic wave theory as the basis for wireless communication, and it is this theory that forms the foundation of modern radio technology.

Despite the controversy, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers working on new technologies in the realm of wireless communication.
