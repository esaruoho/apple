Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through aetheric or longitudinal waves, which he thought were different from the transverse waves described by Hertz. He argued that the idea of radio waves traveling through space as transverse waves was an oversimplification and did not accurately represent the mechanism of wireless communication.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the prevailing scientific consensus on the nature of electromagnetic waves. He believed that the true nature of wireless transmission involved a more complex interaction with the Earth's ionosphere and the aether itself. This perspective led him to develop alternative theories and methods for wireless communication, such as his experiments with high-frequency currents and resonant circuits.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted at the time. The modern understanding of electromagnetic waves and wireless communication is based on the work of many scientists, including those who refined and expanded upon the Hertzian wave theory.

Today, we understand that both transverse and longitudinal components can exist in electromagnetic waves, and that Tesla's contributions to wireless technology are recognized for their importance, even if his specific theories about aetheric waves have not been validated by subsequent scientific research.
