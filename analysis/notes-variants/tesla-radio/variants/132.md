Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his own experiments and theories regarding wireless transmission. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself or through the ionosphere, rather than through space as Hertzian waves suggested. This idea is often referred to as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory."

In his view, the idea of propagating electromagnetic waves through the air was not sufficient for long-distance communication without the use of towers or other physical structures to amplify the signal. Tesla argued that these structures were necessary because the waves would dissipate too quickly in the atmosphere.

Furthermore, Tesla believed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, allowing for more efficient and widespread transmission. He conducted experiments with his Wardenclyffe Tower, which he hoped would demonstrate the feasibility of transmitting electrical energy over vast distances using the Earth's natural properties.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and ahead of their time, they were not widely accepted at the time due to the limitations of technology and the prevailing scientific understanding of the day. It wasn't until later that advancements in technology and further research confirmed aspects of Tesla's theories, particularly the role of the ionosphere in radio wave propagation.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in modern wireless communication, but Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant, even if his specific ideas about earth resonance have not been fully realized in practical applications.
