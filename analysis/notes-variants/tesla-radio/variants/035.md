Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being described by the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. He argued that the transmission of information through the air was not simply a matter of radiating energy in all directions, but rather involved a more focused and directed form of energy transfer.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized the idea that radio waves were akin to light waves or sound waves, suggesting instead that they were more akin to a form of vibration or resonance that could be manipulated for communication purposes. He emphasized the importance of resonant circuits and the ability to tune into specific frequencies, which he felt was more aligned with the principles of wireless communication.

While Tesla's ideas were groundbreaking and have since been validated by subsequent research, at the time, many scientists and engineers were still developing the practical applications of radio technology based on the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla's approach to wireless communication, which included concepts like resonant antennas and tuned circuits, laid the groundwork for much of modern radio and telecommunications technology.

It's important to note that Tesla's skepticism towards the Hertzian wave theory did not mean he was opposed to the use of radio waves; rather, he was advocating for a more refined and efficient method of utilizing them. His contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, even if his views on the underlying physics were not universally accepted during his lifetime.
