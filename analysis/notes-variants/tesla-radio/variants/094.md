Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter stemmed from his belief in a more holistic and efficient method of transmitting energy and information without relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He argued that the traditional view of radio waves as discrete particles traveling through the air was too simplistic and did not account for the full potential of wireless technology.

In particular, Tesla emphasized the importance of what he called "resonance" or "sympathetic vibration," which he believed was key to efficient wireless transmission. He thought that by tuning the frequency of the transmitter and receiver to resonate at the same frequency, one could achieve much greater efficiency and range in wireless communication. This resonant approach contrasted with the prevailing view that radio waves could be transmitted and received over any distance without specific tuning.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. Modern understanding of radio waves and wireless communication has evolved, incorporating both the principles of electromagnetic radiation and Tesla's concepts of resonance and efficient transmission.

Today, we recognize that both the electromagnetic radiation theory and Tesla's ideas have their merits and have been integrated into the broader framework of wireless technology.
