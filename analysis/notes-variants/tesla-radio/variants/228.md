Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves," named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He proposed that there was a subtle connection between matter and energy that allowed for the transmission of information without the need for physical waves. This idea was encapsulated in his concept of "radiant energy" or "primary waves," which he thought could travel through the Earth and air without the need for a vacuum or a medium like air or water.

In contrast, the prevailing view at the time, based on Hertz's experiments, suggested that electromagnetic waves could propagate through space and could be detected by receiving antennas. Tesla argued that this approach was incomplete and that it did not explain the phenomena he observed, such as the ability to transmit signals over long distances without the need for a line-of-sight path.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about wireless communication were innovative and contributed significantly to the field, they were not widely accepted during his lifetime. Modern physics has largely validated the electromagnetic wave theory, but it also acknowledges that Tesla's contributions were crucial in developing practical applications for wireless technology.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in different aspects of wireless communication. Tesla's work laid the groundwork for understanding how to use the Earth itself as a conductor for wireless signals, which is still used in various forms of modern communication systems.
