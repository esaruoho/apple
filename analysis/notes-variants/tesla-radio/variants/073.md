Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or what we now call radio waves, did not fully capture the essence of his vision for wireless communication.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more than just the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space. He proposed that there was a "wireless" medium that could carry signals without the need for physical conductors. This idea was based on his observations and experiments, particularly with his resonant transformer circuits, which he believed could transmit energy over vast distances without the need for a direct line of sight or physical connection.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through free space, which Tesla felt was an incomplete description of the phenomenon. Tesla argued that the Hertzian waves alone could not account for the long-distance transmission capabilities that he observed and demonstrated with his systems.

It's important to note that while Tesla's claims were innovative and ahead of their time, they were also controversial and often misunderstood. The scientific community at the time generally accepted the Hertzian wave theory as the correct explanation for radio wave propagation. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication, including the development of resonant circuits and the Wardenclyffe Tower project, have been recognized as significant milestones in the history of technology.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play roles in the functioning of modern wireless communication systems. Tesla's work laid the groundwork for many of the principles used in wireless technology, even if his specific claims about the nature of wireless transmission were not entirely accurate from a modern scientific perspective.
