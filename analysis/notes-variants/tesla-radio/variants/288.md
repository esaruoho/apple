Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves, as described by Heinrich Hertz, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he understood it.

Tesla's work on wireless technology was based on his belief in a system of resonant circuits, which he thought could transmit energy without the need for a physical conductor. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as propagating through space without any medium, did not align with his ideas of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the waves could be transmitted more efficiently and effectively if they were able to resonate with the natural frequencies of the Earth and the atmosphere.

In his patents and public statements, Tesla often criticized the prevailing scientific understanding of the time, suggesting that the theory of Hertzian waves was incomplete or insufficient for practical applications of wireless communication. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor, allowing for the transmission of signals over vast distances without the need for towers or other physical infrastructure.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of modern radio and wireless technology, the scientific community at the time did not fully accept his theories. The concept of Hertzian waves eventually became the foundation for much of the subsequent research and development in wireless communication.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories play important roles in the functioning of modern radio and telecommunications systems. Tesla's contributions to the field include the development of resonant circuits and the design of early radio transmitters, which laid the groundwork for many of the technologies we use today.
