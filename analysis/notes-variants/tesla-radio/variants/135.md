Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory, in explaining his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the Hertzian waves, or radio waves as they are commonly known today, were not sufficient for his vision of wireless technology.

Tesla's primary concerns with the Hertzian wave theory included:

Infinite Speed: Tesla argued that the speed of light and radio waves being infinite was problematic because it implied instantaneous transmission, which he felt was not practical for long-distance communication. He believed that there should be a finite speed for signals to travel, allowing for better control and understanding of the signal propagation.

Interference and Noise: Tesla was concerned about the interference and noise that could occur with Hertzian waves, especially in the presence of other electrical devices and natural phenomena. He believed that his system, which involved resonant circuits, could mitigate these issues more effectively.

Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of resonance in his wireless systems. He believed that by using resonant circuits, one could achieve more efficient and selective transmission and reception of signals. This approach differed from the general use of Hertzian waves, which did not inherently rely on resonance.

Energy Transmission: Tesla's work extended beyond just communication to include energy transmission. He believed that his system could transmit both information and energy over vast distances without the need for physical conductors, which he saw as a significant advantage over traditional wired systems.

Despite these criticisms, the Hertzian wave theory eventually became the foundation for modern radio technology. However, Tesla's ideas and inventions, such as the Tesla coil and his experiments with high-frequency currents, have contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology and continue to inspire researchers and engineers to this day.
