Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work on radio waves. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework developed by Hertz and others did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a simple electromagnetic wave. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonance theory." According to Tesla, this approach would allow for more efficient and widespread transmission of wireless signals without the need for high-frequency oscillators and large antennas, which were common in the early days of radio.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was based on the idea that radio waves could travel through space without a medium, was seen by Tesla as a more limited and less effective method for wireless communication. Tesla argued that this theory did not account for the natural resonant properties of the Earth and the atmosphere, which he believed were crucial for achieving long-range and efficient wireless transmission.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology, they were not widely accepted at the time. Modern understanding of radio waves and wireless communication has evolved to incorporate both the concepts of Hertz and Tesla, recognizing that both the medium and the space can play roles in signal propagation.
