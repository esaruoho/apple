Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and disagreement with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves (or electromagnetic waves) did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a different mechanism than simple electromagnetic radiation. He argued that the propagation of signals through the air was not just a matter of waves traveling through space but involved a more complex interaction with the Earth itself. Tesla proposed that the Earth could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it, a concept now known as ground wave propagation or earth currents.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized what he saw as the oversimplification of wireless communication to mere wave propagation. He believed that the true nature of wireless transmission involved a combination of aerial and ground effects, and that the proper understanding of this process required a more holistic approach.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless technology, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. Modern understanding of wireless communication has evolved to include both electromagnetic wave propagation and ground wave effects, among other phenomena.

Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, even if some of his specific theories about the nature of wireless transmission have not been fully validated by subsequent scientific research. His work laid the groundwork for much of modern telecommunications.
