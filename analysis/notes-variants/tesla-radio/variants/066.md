Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's work on radio communication predates many of the theoretical developments attributed to Heinrich Hertz, who is often credited with the discovery of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic radiation did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it. He argued that the idea of waves propagating through space without a medium (vacuum) was not entirely accurate for practical applications of wireless technology. Tesla favored a more direct form of energy transmission through the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as "earth currents" or "space currents."

In his view, the wireless transmission of information and power could be achieved through these currents, which would travel along the surface of the Earth or through the ionosphere, rather than through the air as described by the wave theory. This approach aligned more closely with his experiments and inventions, such as the Wardenclyffe Tower project in Long Island, New York, where he attempted to demonstrate the transmission of electrical energy over long distances using earth currents.

Tesla's skepticism towards the wave theory was partly due to its limitations in explaining phenomena like the range and efficiency of wireless communication. He believed that the wave theory did not account for the observed effects of wireless transmission adequately and that a more comprehensive theory was needed.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not widely accepted at the time. The wave theory eventually became the standard framework for understanding electromagnetic radiation, and it has been successfully used in the development of modern radio and telecommunications technologies. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers exploring new methods of wireless energy transfer and communication.
