Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory, in explaining his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the way radio waves were being described and used at the time did not fully capture the essence of what he had discovered.

Tesla's work on radio waves began in the late 19th century, and he conducted experiments that led him to believe that radio waves could be used for communication without the need for wires. He patented several devices related to radio technology, including the Tesla coil, which is still widely used today for generating high-frequency alternating currents.

However, Tesla argued that the prevailing scientific understanding of radio waves, based on Heinrich Hertz's discoveries, was incomplete. Hertz demonstrated that electromagnetic waves could be transmitted through space, but Tesla felt that this description did not adequately account for the phenomena he observed and experienced in his experiments. Specifically, Tesla believed that the concept of "wireless" transmission implied that signals could travel without any medium, which contradicted his understanding of the physical nature of these waves.

Tesla's views were controversial and often met with skepticism from the scientific community of his time. Nevertheless, his contributions to radio technology and wireless communication have been recognized, and many of his ideas have since been validated or incorporated into modern theories of electromagnetism.

It's important to note that while Tesla's work laid the foundation for much of modern radio technology, the full understanding of electromagnetic waves and their behavior has evolved over time, incorporating both classical physics and quantum mechanics.
