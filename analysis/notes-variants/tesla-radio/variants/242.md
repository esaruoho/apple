Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to his conception of wireless technology. Tesla believed that the theoretical framework of electromagnetic waves, as described by Heinrich Hertz, did not fully capture the essence of what he perceived as the true nature of wireless communication.

Tesla's views were rooted in his experimental work and his philosophical approach to science. He emphasized the importance of resonance and the ability of an antenna to resonate with a specific frequency, which he believed was key to efficient wireless transmission. According to Tesla, the concept of Hertzian waves alone was insufficient because it did not account for the resonant properties of antennas and the way they could be tuned to specific frequencies.

In contrast, Tesla's patents and demonstrations often focused on resonant circuits and the idea of tuning transmitters and receivers to the same frequency. This approach allowed for more efficient and selective communication, which Tesla argued was essential for practical wireless applications.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, the modern understanding of radio and wireless communication is based on a combination of both Tesla's and Hertz's contributions. The development of radio technology has evolved significantly since Tesla's time, incorporating advancements in electronics, signal processing, and other fields.
