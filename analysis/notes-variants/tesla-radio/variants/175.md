Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views can be summarized as follows:

Wireless Transmission vs. Radio Waves: Tesla argued that the transmission of information through the air (what we now call wireless communication) should not be limited to the concept of radio waves. He believed that the medium for wireless communication was not just electromagnetic waves but also included other forms of energy that could propagate without wires.

Coherence and Resonance: Tesla emphasized the importance of coherence and resonance in his wireless systems. He believed that the Earth itself could act as a resonant medium for wireless signals, which would allow for more efficient and longer-range communication. This idea was based on his experiments with high-frequency alternating currents and his belief in the Earth's natural resonance.

Incompatibility with Hertzian Theory: Tesla felt that the Hertzian wave theory, which focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, was too narrow and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication. He believed that the theory was overly simplistic and did not consider the complex interactions between the transmitter, receiver, and the medium through which the signal travels.

Experiments and Patents: Tesla conducted numerous experiments to demonstrate his ideas. For example, he patented various devices such as the Tesla Coil, which allowed for the generation of high-frequency alternating currents and the demonstration of wireless lighting and communication. These experiments supported his claims about the capabilities of wireless technology beyond what was described by the Hertzian wave theory.

Public Demonstrations: Tesla often highlighted his demonstrations of wireless communication, such as his famous Wardenclyffe Tower project, which aimed to transmit power and information across long distances without wires. These demonstrations were intended to show the practical applications of his theories and to challenge the prevailing scientific understanding of wireless communication.

While Tesla's ideas were groundbreaking and have influenced modern wireless technology, they were also controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. It took several decades for many of his concepts to gain acceptance and for the field of wireless communication to evolve into what it is today.

It's important to note that while Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, the development of radio and wireless technology has been a collaborative effort involving many scientists and engineers over the years.
