Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of "waves" as described by Heinrich Hertz did not accurately represent the nature of wireless transmission.

Tesla's views were based on his own experiments and theories, particularly his work with resonant circuits and his belief in the existence of a continuous current or "etheric current" that could transmit energy without the need for physical waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that signals could be transmitted through it, a concept now known as Earth resonance or Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower project.

While Hertzian waves (electromagnetic waves) do play a crucial role in modern wireless communication, Tesla's ideas contributed significantly to the development of wireless technology. His work laid the groundwork for many of the principles used in today's radio and telecommunications systems, even if some of his specific theories were not entirely accurate.

It's important to note that Tesla's views were often controversial and not widely accepted during his lifetime. However, they have since been recognized as influential in the evolution of wireless technology.
