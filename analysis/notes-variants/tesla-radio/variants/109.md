Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the wireless transmission of information and energy could be achieved through a different mechanism than what Hertzian waves suggested.

Tesla's views were based on his experiments and theories about the nature of wireless communication. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, a concept now known as "earth resonance" or "Tesla's Wardenclyffe Tower." According to Tesla, the Earth could store and transmit electrical energy without the need for the high-frequency oscillations that Hertzian waves required.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory, which was widely accepted at the time, suggested that radio waves traveled through space as transverse electromagnetic waves. Tesla argued that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication and that it was overly focused on the propagation of waves rather than the underlying principles of energy transfer and signal transmission.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, but they have since gained recognition as precursors to modern wireless technology. The concept of using the Earth as a conductor for wireless signals is now understood to be a form of ground wave propagation, which is still used in various forms of radio communication today.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, the scientific community at the time lacked the technological means to fully test and validate his theories. Modern research has provided evidence supporting some of Tesla's concepts, particularly in the realm of resonant systems and the use of the Earth as a conductor.
