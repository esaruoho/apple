Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of "waves" as described by Heinrich Hertz did not accurately represent the nature of wireless transmission.

Tesla's ideas were rooted in his belief that wireless communication involved a more direct and continuous transfer of energy between transmitter and receiver, rather than the propagation of waves through space. He proposed that the Earth itself played a crucial role in facilitating this communication, a concept known as "earth resonance."

In his view, the Hertzian wave theory was too focused on the idea of electromagnetic waves traveling through space, which he felt was an oversimplification. Tesla believed that the true mechanism of wireless transmission involved a form of energy that could penetrate the Earth and be transmitted without the need for a line-of-sight or the propagation of waves.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial at the time. The scientific community largely accepted the wave theory as the correct explanation for electromagnetic phenomena, and it was later confirmed by experiments and theoretical developments.

Today, we understand that both Tesla's and Hertz's theories have their merits and applications. Modern wireless technology combines elements of both approaches, using antennas and transmitters to send signals through the air, while also taking advantage of the Earth's conductive properties to enhance signal propagation.
