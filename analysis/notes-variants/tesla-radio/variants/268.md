Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and disagreement with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily associated with Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that his own experiments and observations indicated that wireless transmission involved a different mechanism than what was being described by the then-popular Hertzian wave theory.

Tesla's views were based on his extensive work with high-frequency alternating currents (AC) and his belief in the existence of "resonant" or "resonance" phenomena in wireless communication. He argued that the key to wireless transmission lay in resonating circuits rather than the propagation of waves through space. This resonant approach suggested that the transmitter and receiver would need to be tuned to the same frequency, much like musical instruments resonate at specific frequencies.

In contrast, the Hertzian wave theory focused on the propagation of electromagnetic waves through space, which could theoretically transmit information without requiring a direct line of sight between the transmitter and receiver. However, Tesla contended that this theory did not fully capture the essence of how wireless communication actually worked.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about resonance and wireless communication were innovative and influential, they were not universally accepted during his lifetime. The scientific community eventually came to accept the wave theory as the primary explanation for wireless communication, although Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant. His work laid the groundwork for many modern technologies, including radio, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers today.
