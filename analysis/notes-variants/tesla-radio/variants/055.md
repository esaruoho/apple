Nikola Tesla indeed had significant disagreements with the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, which was primarily based on Heinrich Hertz's work. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, or electromagnetic waves propagating through space, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's approach to wireless transmission was rooted in his belief in the Earth's natural resonant properties and the ability to transmit information through the ground and the air without relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves. He proposed that wireless signals could be transmitted through the Earth itself, using the Earth as a conductor, and that this method would be more efficient and effective than relying on the air for signal propagation.

In his writings and public statements, Tesla often criticized the Hertzian wave theory, arguing that it oversimplified the complex interactions between the transmitter, receiver, and the medium through which the signal travels. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves was too narrow and did not account for the full potential of wireless communication.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were also controversial and not widely accepted at the time. The scientific community eventually came to accept the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for wireless communication, although Tesla's contributions to the field remain significant. His work laid the groundwork for many modern technologies, including radio and wireless communication systems.
