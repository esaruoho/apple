Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism and criticism towards the prevailing electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and popularized by others, including Heinrich Hertz. Tesla's views on this matter were rooted in his own experimental work and theoretical framework.

Tesla believed that the concept of "Hertzian waves" or electromagnetic waves propagating through space did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he understood it. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through a vacuum could not account for the observed phenomena in wireless transmission, such as the ability to transmit signals over long distances without direct line-of-sight.

In contrast, Tesla proposed that wireless signals were transmitted through the Earth and the ionosphere, which he referred to as the "earth resonance" or "resonance method." This approach emphasized the role of the Earth and the ionosphere as conductors and resonators of electrical energy, rather than relying solely on the propagation of electromagnetic waves.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism during his lifetime, as the scientific community at the time was more inclined to accept the wave theory as explained by Hertz and later elaborated upon by other scientists. However, modern research has provided some support for Tesla's ideas about earth resonance and wireless transmission, although the exact mechanisms remain subjects of ongoing study and debate.

It's important to note that while Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are significant, the electromagnetic radiation theory remains a cornerstone of our understanding of how radio waves travel through space. Nonetheless, Tesla's insights and experiments continue to inspire researchers and engineers in the field of wireless technology.
