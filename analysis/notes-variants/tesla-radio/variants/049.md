Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being described by the prevailing scientific theories of his era. He argued that the transmission of information through the air could be achieved without the need for a physical medium like a wire, and that the energy used in wireless transmission was not simply radiating away but was being stored and reused in a resonant manner.

In his patents and writings, Tesla described a system where the Earth itself could act as a conductor and a receiver, allowing for the transmission of signals over vast distances. This idea was based on the concept of resonance, where a transmitter and a receiver would resonate at the same frequency, effectively creating a wireless connection without the need for a continuous flow of energy through the air.

Tesla's claims and theories were often met with skepticism from the scientific community of his time, partly because the technology to test these ideas was not yet available. However, his work laid the groundwork for many modern technologies, including radio and wireless communication, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers today.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and influential, they were also complex and sometimes difficult to reconcile with the established physics of the time. Nonetheless, his contributions to the field of wireless communication have been significant and enduring.
