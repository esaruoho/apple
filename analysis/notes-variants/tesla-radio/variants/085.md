Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his own experiments and theories. He proposed that wireless transmission could be achieved through the Earth itself, a concept known as "earth resonance" or "resonant radiation." According to Tesla, the Earth could act as a conductor for these resonant currents, allowing for efficient and long-distance transmission without the need for high-frequency oscillations that were characteristic of Hertzian waves.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the high-frequency oscillators used in the Hertzian wave theory were not necessary for wireless communication. Instead, he believed that lower frequencies and the natural properties of the Earth could be harnessed to transmit information over vast distances. This idea was revolutionary at the time and contrasted sharply with the prevailing scientific understanding of the day.

Despite Tesla's claims and demonstrations, the scientific community largely adopted the Hertzian wave theory as the basis for wireless communication. It wasn't until later that advancements in technology and further research confirmed aspects of Tesla's ideas, particularly in the realm of long-distance radio communication and the use of the Earth as a medium for signal propagation.

Today, while we recognize the contributions of both Tesla and Hertz to the field of wireless communication, modern technology has largely moved beyond the limitations of early Hertzian wave theory, incorporating various forms of electromagnetic radiation and advanced signal processing techniques to achieve efficient and reliable wireless communication.
