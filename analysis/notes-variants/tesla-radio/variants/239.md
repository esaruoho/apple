Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his own experiments and theories. He proposed that wireless transmission could be achieved through the Earth itself, a concept now known as "Earth resonance" or "Tesla coil" technology. According to Tesla, the Earth could act as a conductor for electrical currents, allowing for the transmission of signals without the need for a line-of-sight transmission medium like radio waves.

In his writings and public demonstrations, Tesla argued that the traditional view of electromagnetic waves as propagating through space in straight lines was overly simplistic and did not account for the complex interactions between the Earth and the transmitted signals. He believed that the Earth could be used as a resonant medium, much like a tuning fork, to transmit and receive signals over vast distances.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and were not widely accepted by the scientific community. However, they laid the groundwork for modern concepts such as ground wave propagation and the use of the Earth's ionosphere for radio communications.

It's important to note that while Tesla's theories were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, the practical implementation of his ideas required further development and experimentation. Modern technology has since confirmed many aspects of Tesla's theories, particularly in the realm of long-distance radio communication and the use of the Earth's natural properties for signal transmission.
