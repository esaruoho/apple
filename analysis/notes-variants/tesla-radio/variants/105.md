Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time. He believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless communication as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views on this matter were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission involved a more direct and efficient method than what was being proposed by the prevailing theories of his era. He argued that the idea of sending signals through the air as waves was too indirect and inefficient for practical applications.

In contrast, Tesla believed that wireless communication could be achieved through a more direct method involving resonant circuits. He proposed that if two devices were tuned to resonate at the same frequency, they could communicate directly without the need for the intervening medium suggested by the wave theory. This idea is encapsulated in his concept of "resonance" and "sympathetic vibration," which he believed could explain how wireless signals could travel over long distances without the need for a physical medium.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and prescient, they were not widely accepted or understood at the time. The wave theory of electromagnetic radiation was eventually proven correct and became the foundation for modern radio technology. However, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication remain significant, and his ideas continue to inspire researchers and engineers to this day.
