Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the nature of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to aether or a subtle medium, rather than just electromagnetic waves propagating through space. He argued that the idea of Hertzian waves being the primary means of wireless communication was overly simplistic and did not account for the full potential of wireless technology.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla often criticized what he saw as the limitations of the electromagnetic wave theory. He believed that the theory did not adequately explain phenomena such as the long-range transmission of signals without the need for a line-of-sight path, which is a characteristic of his own wireless systems.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they also diverged from the prevailing scientific understanding of the time. The modern understanding of wireless communication largely aligns with the principles of electromagnetic radiation, although Tesla's contributions to the field, including the development of resonant circuits and the Wardenclyffe Tower project, have been recognized as significant milestones in the history of wireless technology.
