Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the electromagnetic radiation theory and its relationship to his conception of wireless communication. Tesla believed that the theories of electromagnetic waves, particularly those associated with Heinrich Hertz, were not fully capturing the essence of what he perceived as wireless transmission.

Tesla's views on this matter can be traced back to his experiments and observations. He argued that the methods used to transmit and receive electromagnetic waves at the time were not sufficient for practical wireless communication as he envisioned it. Specifically, Tesla contended that the use of high-frequency oscillators and resonant circuits could achieve more efficient and longer-range communication than the methods based solely on Hertzian waves.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla described how he believed in the existence of a "wireless" or "etheric" medium that could carry signals without the need for physical conductors. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor for these signals, which would allow for much greater range and efficiency in communication.

Tesla's ideas were revolutionary at the time and were not widely accepted by the scientific community. However, his work laid the groundwork for modern wireless technology, including radio, television, and cellular networks. Despite his skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication are now recognized and celebrated.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative, they also contained elements that were not supported by contemporary science. The development of radio and other forms of wireless communication did eventually rely on the principles of electromagnetic radiation, but Tesla's unique perspective and experimental approach continue to be studied and appreciated for their historical significance.
