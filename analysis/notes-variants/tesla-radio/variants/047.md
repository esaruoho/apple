Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory, particularly as it was being developed and applied during his time. Tesla's views were rooted in his own experimental work and theoretical insights into wireless communication.

Tesla believed that the concept of electromagnetic waves propagating through space, as proposed by Heinrich Hertz, did not fully capture the essence of how wireless signals could be transmitted and received. He argued that the idea of waves traveling through a vacuum was an oversimplification and did not account for the actual mechanisms involved in wireless transmission.

Tesla's approach to wireless communication emphasized the use of resonant circuits and the Earth's ionosphere as a medium for signal propagation. He believed that by creating resonant systems, one could achieve more efficient and longer-range communication without relying solely on the concept of electromagnetic waves traveling through empty space.

In his patents and public statements, Tesla described his inventions as utilizing "resonance" or "sympathetic vibration," which he felt was more aligned with the true nature of wireless communication. He also criticized the reliance on Hertzian waves as being too simplistic and potentially harmful to the development of practical wireless technology.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and influential, they were not universally accepted at the time. The scientific community generally adopted the wave theory as the basis for understanding electromagnetic radiation, although it is now understood that both approaches have their merits and can coexist in explaining different aspects of wireless communication.

Today, we recognize that Tesla's contributions to the field of wireless communication were groundbreaking, even if his specific theories about the nature of electromagnetic waves were not entirely accurate according to modern physics. His work laid the foundation for much of what we understand about wireless technology today.
