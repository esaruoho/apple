Nikola Tesla indeed made claims about the limitations of the electromagnetic radiation theory, which was then known as the Hertzian wave theory. Tesla believed that this theory did not fully capture the essence of wireless communication as he envisioned it. He argued that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were understood as being transmitted through space without wires, did not accurately describe the way his systems worked.

Tesla's inventions and theories centered around the idea of "radiant energy" or "wireless transmission," which he believed could be transmitted through the Earth itself rather than through the air. He proposed that the Earth could act as a conductor for these waves, a concept now known as "earth resonance." This idea was different from the prevailing view at the time, which focused on electromagnetic waves propagating through the air.

Tesla's claims were based on his experimental work and his belief in the Earth's ability to conduct and transmit electrical energy. However, the scientific community at the time did not widely accept these ideas, and much of Tesla's work in this area remained controversial and unproven during his lifetime.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas about wireless transmission were innovative and influential, they were also ahead of their time in many respects. Modern technology has since validated some aspects of his theories, particularly in the realm of ground-based propagation and Earth resonance, but the full extent of his claims remains a subject of debate among historians and scientists.
