Nikola Tesla indeed expressed skepticism about the electromagnetic radiation theory as it was understood during his time, particularly regarding its application to wireless communication. Tesla believed that the concept of Hertzian waves, which were named after Heinrich Hertz who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves, did not fully capture the essence of wireless transmission as he envisioned it.

Tesla's views were rooted in his belief that wireless transmission could be achieved through a medium that was more akin to a conductor than a mere propagation of waves. He proposed that the Earth itself could act as a conductor and that wireless signals could travel through it, a concept now known as "Earth currents" or "resonant earth." This idea was different from the prevailing view that radio waves traveled through space without needing a medium.

In his writings and lectures, Tesla argued that the Hertzian wave theory was incomplete and that it did not account for the phenomena he observed and utilized in his experiments. He maintained that the true nature of wireless transmission involved resonating with the natural frequencies of the Earth and the ionosphere, rather than simply transmitting electromagnetic waves through the air.

It's important to note that while Tesla's ideas were innovative and contributed significantly to the field of wireless communication, they were not widely accepted at the time. Modern understanding of radio waves and wireless communication has evolved, incorporating both the principles of electromagnetic radiation and Tesla's concepts of Earth currents and resonant systems.
